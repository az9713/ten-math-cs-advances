8.1 The map of consequences

What is settled: Weiss's 2000 question and the soficity conjecture — a nonsofic countable group exists, unconditionally, with no quantum machinery and no stability hypotheses. The earlier conditional routes (Bowen–Burton via flexible stability [BB20]; Gohla–Thom / Chapman–Dikstein–Lubotzky via $p$-adic Deligne extensions [GT24, CDL24]) are bypassed. In the invariant-random-subgroup language of the Aldous–Lyons problem: for $N \lhd F_d$, the quotient $F_d/N$ is sofic iff the point mass $\delta_N$ is a co-sofic IRS [Gel18, §6]; presenting our finitely generated $G$ as $F_d/N$ therefore yields a deterministic non-co-sofic unimodular network (a marked Cayley diagram), where the 2024–25 refutation of Aldous–Lyons [BCLV24, BCV25] — built on subgroup tests and the $\mathsf{MIP}^*=\mathsf{RE}$ compression machinery — produced a random, non-normal-subgroup example that could not yield a nonsofic group. The two results are complementary, neither implying the other.

What is pointedly not settled (Fig. 24):

  • Hyperlinearity of $R^\times$ (= Connes's embedding problem for groups, still open: no nonhyperlinear group is known). Hyperlinearity replaces permutations by finite-dimensional unitaries in normalized Hilbert–Schmidt distance; since permutation matrices satisfy $\|P_\sigma - P_\tau\|_{2,n}^2 = 2\,d_{\mathrm H}(\sigma, \tau)$, every sofic group is hyperlinear — so nonsoficity is the weaker negative and says nothing about hyperlinearity of $R^\times$. (The $\mathsf{MIP}^* = \mathsf{RE}$ refutation of Connes's embedding conjecture concerns von Neumann algebras and produces no group.) Conditional routes (Dogon [Dog23]; Dogon–Vigdorovich [DV26]) still await their stability hypotheses.
  • Surjunctivity of $R^\times$ (Gottschalk's conjecture: injective continuous $H$-equivariant maps $A^H \to A^H$ are surjective). Gromov–Weiss: sofic $\Rightarrow$ surjunctive. Now that $R^\times$ is not sofic, it is the natural test case: if $R^\times$ is surjunctive, there is a surjunctive nonsofic group (and the copy $V \le R^\times$ is surjunctive too, since surjunctivity passes to subgroups); if not, Gottschalk's conjecture is false. Either way something interesting; currently unknown. (Bowen–Chapman's surjunctive non-co-sofic IRS [BC25] does not decide it.)
  • Lück determinant & algebraic eigenvalue conjectures for $R^\times$: both hold for sofic groups; both are open for $R^\times$ — the first group for which they are genuinely in doubt.
  • Kaplansky direct finiteness ($ab = 1 \Rightarrow ba = 1$ in group rings $k[H]$): the tempting shortcut fails — $t_0 s_0 = 1 \neq s_0 t_0$ lives in $R$, not in $k[R^\times]$, because $s_0, t_0$ are not units. Characteristic zero is settled (stably finite [BFF24]); positive characteristic for $R^\times$ is open, and surjunctivity of $R^\times$ would settle it.
  • Kervaire–Laudenbach (one-variable equations over groups): holds for hyperlinear groups [NT22]; for $R^\times$ it is now tied to the open hyperlinearity question — failure would exhibit a nonhyperlinear group.
A stronger identity, not needed. Khanh and Thanh proved $G = \mathrm{EL}_D(R)$ is actually all of $R^\times$ [KT26, Cor. 4.4]. The proof above never uses this: it only needs $G \le R^\times$ and Proposition B (subgroups of sofic groups are sofic).

9. Machine verification of every algebraic identity

The algebra of §5–§7 is finitary: every identity is an equation between finite sums of monomials $s_a t_b$. That makes it mechanically checkable, and we do — every boxed identity above is re-verified in exact arithmetic by the listing below (a trimmed version of the full engine figgen/ch03_figs.py, which checks more cases).

Encoding. By the word calculus (Lemma J(1)), every product of generators collapses to $0$ or to a monomial $s_a t_b$; hence every element in the subring generated by the $s_i, t_j$ is a finite $\mathbb F_2$-sum of such monomials, stored as a set of pairs $(a, b)$ with XOR as addition (mod-2 sums cancel in pairs). Multiplication implements Lemma J(1) on pairs: $(a,b)\cdot(c,d)$ is $(a c', d)$ if $c = b c'$, $(a, d b')$ if $b = c b'$, else $0$. Equality is tested by action on the string model of §5.1: an element $x$ with maximal $t$-word depth $D$ acts as zero iff it kills every cylinder symbol of depth $D$ — a finite, exact test (the action on any $e_{w\omega}$ depends only on the first $D$ bits $w$). A subtlety, stated honestly: this verifies each identity in the string representation. That suffices to certify the hand proofs (any error there would be an identity false in the model, which the checker would catch), and in fact the representation is faithful — the Leavitt algebra $L_K(1,n)$ is a simple ring [Lea62], so its nonzero representations are injective — making the machine test equivalent to equality in $R$.
