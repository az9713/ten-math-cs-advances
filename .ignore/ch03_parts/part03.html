4. The expander-matching criterion (Proposition 2.3), in full

This section proves the chapter's analytic contribution: a general group-theoretic criterion, independent of the Leavitt algebra, that turns Kun's many expanders into Kun–Thom's one expander. The extra hypothesis — the one the $\Lambda \times B$ example of §3.5 lacks — is that some elements conjugate both the property-(T) subgroup and the commuting group into the property-(T) subgroup.

Proposition 2.3. Let $\Gamma \le G$ be infinite finitely generated groups, both with property (T). Suppose $$G = \langle \Gamma, t_1, \dots, t_m \rangle, \qquad t_i \Gamma t_i^{-1} \le \Gamma \quad (1 \le i \le m), \qquad m \ge 1,$$ and that a finitely generated subgroup $J \le G$ satisfies $$[\Gamma, J] = 1, \qquad \Gamma \cap J = \{1\}, \qquad t_1 J t_1^{-1} \le \Gamma.$$ Then soficity of $G$ implies that $J$ is LEF.

Before the proof, absorb the hypotheses. $[\Gamma, J] = 1$ means every element of $\Gamma$ commutes with every element of $J$; together with $\Gamma \cap J = \{1\}$ this identifies the subgroup $\Gamma J \le G$ with the direct product $\Gamma \times J$ (the map $(g, j) \mapsto gj$ is an injective homomorphism). The conjugations by $t_1$ compress both factors into $\Gamma$ at once: $$t_1 (\Gamma \times J) t_1^{-1} \;=\; \bigl(t_1 \Gamma t_1^{-1}\bigr) \times \bigl(t_1 J t_1^{-1}\bigr) \;\le\; \Gamma.$$ This nesting is exactly what the direct product $\Lambda \times B$ of §3.5 does not have. The proof runs in five steps; Fig. 9 is the map.

Setup. Assume $G$ is sofic and fix one sofic approximation $p_n : G \to \mathrm{Sym}(Y_n)$; by disjoint amplification (§1) arrange $N = |Y_n| \to \infty$. All $o(N)$ estimates refer to this one approximation; for readability write $p_g = p_n(g)$ and suppress $n$ on graphs and partitions.

Step 1: locate a dominant original component inside each transported component. Choose a finite symmetric generating set $S_\Gamma \ni 1$ for $\Gamma$. Adjoin the distinct nonidentity elements among $t_i^{\pm 1}$ to obtain a symmetric generating set $S_G$ for $G$; for each adjoined inverse pair fix one of the original $t_i$ as its positive representative. Thus every positive nonidentity $G$-generator either lies in $\Gamma$ or is one of the compressing elements $t_i$. Normalize inverse labels to inverse permutations and nonidentity involutive labels to exact involutions (allowing their $o(N)$ fixed points); this changes only $o(N)$ values [ES06, Lemma 2.1].

Apply Import 1 (Theorem 2.1) twice: to the restriction of $p_n$ to $\Gamma$ with generating set $S_\Gamma$, and to $p_n$ itself with $S_G$. Record the outputs:

Both partitions live on the same vertex set $Y_n$; neither is assumed to refine the other. Each reference graph differs from its generator graph in $o(N)$ edges. Since $L_\Gamma$ has no edges between original components, each $S_\Gamma$-generator crosses between members of $\mathcal Q$ at only $o(N)$ vertices; and if $w = s_1 \cdots s_r$ is a fixed $\Gamma$-word with $p_w = p_{s_1} \cdots p_{s_r}$, the same crossing estimate holds for $p_w$ ($r$ is fixed, each factor crosses rarely).

Set $\tau_i = p_{t_i}$ and transport the whole reference graph through it (Fig. 10): $$I_i = \tau_i L_\Gamma, \qquad \mathcal P_i = \{\tau_i C : C \in \mathcal Q\}.$$ Each member of $\mathcal P_i$ is a $\gamma_\Gamma$-expanding connected component of $I_i$ (relabeling vertices by a bijection preserves boundaries). Now use the hypothesis $t_i \Gamma t_i^{-1} \le \Gamma$: apart from $o(N)$ edited edges, the edges of $I_i$ follow the permutations $\tau_i p_s \tau_i^{-1}$ for $s \in S_\Gamma$; approximate multiplicativity identifies each of these, outside $o(N)$ vertices, with $p_w$ for a fixed $\Gamma$-word $w$ representing $t_i s t_i^{-1} \in \Gamma$. Such $p_w$ almost preserve the original components, so $$\#\{\text{$I_i$-edges joining different members of } \mathcal Q\} \;=\; o(N) \qquad (1 \le i \le m). \tag{4}$$

For $P \in \mathcal P_i$, choose $Q(P) \in \mathcal Q$ maximizing the overlap $|P \cap Q(P)|$, and define the unmatched mass $$L(P) \;=\; |P| - |P \cap Q(P)|.$$ Partition $P$ into the pieces $P \cap Q$, $Q \in \mathcal Q$. If the largest piece exceeds half of $|P|$, apply the expansion (3) of $P$ (inside $I_i$) to each other piece — their total size is $L(P)$; otherwise every piece has at most half the vertices, and apply expansion to all of them. Each edge between different pieces is counted at most twice, so in both cases $$\gamma_\Gamma\, L(P) \;\le\; 2\,\#\{\text{$I_i[P]$-edges joining different members of } \mathcal Q\}.$$ Summing over $P \in \mathcal P_i$ and using (4): $\sum_{P} L(P) = o(N)$. Choose a sequence $\eta_n \downarrow 0$ slowly enough that, simultaneously for every $i$, $$\sum_{\substack{P \in \mathcal P_i \\ L(P) \gt \eta_n |P|}} |P| \;=\; o(N). \tag{5}$$ (Possible since $\sum_P L(P) = o(N)$: the components violating $L(P) \le \eta_n|P|$ carry at most $\eta_n^{-1} \sum_P L(P)$ vertices; pick $\eta_n \to 0$ slower than the $o(N)$ rate.) So: for each $i$, all but $o(N)$ vertices lie in transported components with relative unmatched mass at most $\eta_n$. What is not yet ruled out: two transported components selecting the same target $Q(P)$.

Step 2: a bounded median normalization compares component sizes. For $z \in Y_n$ let $C(z) \in \mathcal Q$ be its original component and $M(z) = |C(z)|$ its size. First, transport almost never shrinks $M$: suppose $P = \tau_i C$ has $L(P) \le \eta_n |P|$. For every $z \in C$ with $\tau_i z \in P \cap Q(P)$, $$M(\tau_i z) = |Q(P)| \;\ge\; |P \cap Q(P)| \;\ge\; (1 - \eta_n)|P| \;=\; (1 - \eta_n)\,M(z),$$ and by (5) the failing components plus unmatched slivers hold $o(N)$ vertices, so $$M(\tau_i z) \;\ge\; (1 - \eta_n)\, M(z) \qquad \text{outside } o(N) \text{ vertices} \quad (1 \le i \le m). \tag{6}$$ Likewise $M(p_s z) = M(z)$ outside $o(N)$ vertices for each $s \in S_\Gamma$ (Γ-generators almost never change component).

The catch: $M$ is unbounded, so “$o(N)$ exceptional vertices” cannot control its total variation. The fix is to compress $M$ into $(0,1)$ by a median normalization. For each ambient component $A \in \mathcal A$, let $m_A \gt 0$ be a median of the vertex-indexed multiset $\{M(z) : z \in A\}$ (at least half the values are $\le m_A$ and at least half are $\ge m_A$; an original component $C$ contributes $|C \cap A|$ copies of $|C|$). Define $$f(z) \;=\; \frac{M(z)}{M(z) + m_A}, \qquad z \in A.$$ Then $0 \lt f \lt 1$, $f$ is increasing in $M$, and $1/2$ is a median of $f$ on every $A$ (since $f(z) \ge 1/2 \iff M(z) \ge m_A$).

Every $G$-generator crosses between ambient components at only $o(N)$ vertices, and on every non-crossing arc both endpoints use the same median $m_A$. For $s \in S_\Gamma$ this gives $f(p_s z) = f(z)$ outside $o(N)$ vertices. For a positive compressing generator $t_i$, feed (6) through the elementary inequality $$\frac{(1-\eta)x}{(1-\eta)x + a} \;\ge\; \frac{x}{x + a} - \eta \qquad (x, a \gt 0,\; 0 \le \eta \lt 1),$$ which holds because the difference of the two fractions is $$\frac{x}{x+a} - \frac{(1-\eta)x}{(1-\eta)x + a} \;=\; \frac{\eta\, x a}{(x+a)\bigl((1-\eta)x + a\bigr)} \;=\; \eta \cdot \frac{x}{x+a} \cdot \frac{a}{(1-\eta)x + a} \;\le\; \eta$$ (both final factors are $\le 1$). Since $t \mapsto t/(t+a)$ is increasing, (6) gives $f(\tau_i z) \ge \frac{(1-\eta_n)M(z)}{(1-\eta_n)M(z)+m_A} \ge f(z) - \eta_n$. So every positive $G$-generator $s$ satisfies $f(p_s z) \ge f(z) - \eta_n$ outside $o(N)$ vertices: $\Gamma$-generators almost preserve $f$, compressions almost never decrease it.

Now the permutation bookkeeping. Because $p_s$ is a bijection of $Y_n$, $$\sum_{z \in Y_n} \bigl( f(p_s z) - f(z) \bigr) \;=\; 0$$ — the sum reindexes to zero. The total decrease (sum of negative parts) is at most $\eta_n N + o(N) = o(N)$: nonexceptional vertices decrease by at most $\eta_n$, exceptional ones by at most $1$ (since $0 \lt f \lt 1$). A sum that vanishes has equal increase and decrease, so the total absolute variation is $o(N)$: $$\sum_{z \in Y_n} \bigl| f(p_s z) - f(z) \bigr| \;=\; o(N).$$ The same holds for inverse generators ($|f(p_{s^{-1}}z) - f(z)|$ reindexes the same multiset). Summing over $S_G$ and passing from the generator graph to $L_G$ (each edited edge changes the sum by at most $1$): $$\sum_{\{z,w\} \in E(L_G)} |f(z) - f(w)| \;=\; o(N). \tag{7}$$

Expansion converts small variation into concentration at the median (Fig. 11). Fix $A \in \mathcal A$. The finite coarea identity states $$\int_0^1 \bigl| \partial_A \{z \in A : f(z) \gt t\} \bigr| \, dt \;=\; \sum_{\{z,w\} \in E(L_G[A])} |f(z) - f(w)|,$$ because an edge $\{z, w\}$ crosses the level set $\{f \gt t\}$ exactly for $t$ between $f(z)$ and $f(w)$ — an interval of length $|f(z) - f(w)|$. For $0 \lt t \lt 1/2$ the sublevel set $\{f \le t\}$ misses the median half, so it has at most $|A|/2$ vertices; for $1/2 \lt t \lt 1$ the same holds for $\{f \gt t\}$. Apply the expansion (3) of $A$ to these sets (complementary sets share one boundary), and integrate the layer-cake identity $\sum_{z \in A}|f(z) - \tfrac12| = \int_0^{1/2} |\{f \le t\}|\,dt + \int_{1/2}^1 |\{f \gt t\}|\,dt$ against it: $$\gamma_G \sum_{z \in A} \Bigl| f(z) - \tfrac12 \Bigr| \;\le\; \sum_{\{z,w\} \in E(L_G[A])} |f(z) - f(w)|.$$ Summing over $A \in \mathcal A$ and using (7): $$\sum_{z \in Y_n} \Bigl| f(z) - \tfrac12 \Bigr| \;=\; o(N).$$ Choose $\delta_n \downarrow 0$ slowly enough that the exceptional set $$E_n = \Bigl\{ z : \bigl|f(z) - \tfrac12\bigr| \gt \delta_n \Bigr\} \qquad \text{satisfies} \qquad |E_n| = o(N).$$ Finally convert back to sizes: on $A$, inverting $f$ gives $M(z) = m_A \frac{f(z)}{1 - f(z)}$, and for $z, w \in A \setminus E_n$ both ratios $\frac{f}{1-f}$ lie in $\bigl[\frac{1-2\delta_n}{1+2\delta_n}, \frac{1+2\delta_n}{1-2\delta_n}\bigr]$, so $$\rho_n^{-1} \;\le\; \frac{M(w)}{M(z)} \;\le\; \rho_n, \qquad \rho_n = \Bigl( \frac{1 + 2\delta_n}{1 - 2\delta_n} \Bigr)^{\!2} \longrightarrow 1. \tag{8}$$ Original components meeting the same ambient component (off $E_n$) have sizes agreeing up to a factor $\to 1$.

Step 3: match the first transported partition almost bijectively. All the $t_i$ were needed above (they are part of $S_G$, so they shaped $L_G$ and the medians). From here on only the first transport matters: set $\tau = \tau_1$, $\mathcal P = \mathcal P_1$. Retain a component $P = \tau C \in \mathcal P$ precisely when $$L(P) \le \eta_n |P| \qquad \text{and} \qquad \exists\, z \in C : \quad \tau z \in P \cap Q(P), \quad z, \tau z \notin E_n, \quad z, \tau z \text{ in the same } A \in \mathcal A. \tag{9}$$ The witness $z$ ties the sizes together: the last condition means $z$ and $\tau z$ share one normalizing median.

Nonretained components are negligible. The crossing set $H_n = \{z : z, \tau z \text{ in different members of } \mathcal A\}$ has size $o(N)$, because $t_1$ is a fixed word in $S_G$ and each generator crosses $\mathcal A$ rarely. If $P$ passes the loss bound but has no witness, then every $z \in \tau^{-1}(P \cap Q(P))$ fails one of the witness conditions: $$\tau^{-1}\bigl(P \cap Q(P)\bigr) \;\subseteq\; H_n \cup E_n \cup \tau^{-1}(E_n).$$ The sets $\tau^{-1}(P \cap Q(P))$ are disjoint as $P$ varies and each holds at least $(1 - \eta_n)|P|$ vertices, so witness-less components contribute $o(N)$ vertices; components failing the loss bound contribute $o(N)$ by (5).

For a retained $P$ with witness $z$: $M(z) = |C| = |P|$ and $M(\tau z) = |Q(P)|$, so the size comparison (8) (applied in the shared $A$, off $E_n$) gives $$\rho_n^{-1} |P| \;\le\; |Q(P)| \;\le\; \rho_n |P|.$$ Since at most $\eta_n |P|$ vertices of $P$ lie outside $Q(P)$: $|P \setminus Q(P)| \le \eta_n|P|$ and $|Q(P) \setminus P| \le (\rho_n - 1 + \eta_n)|P|$, whence $$|P \,\triangle\, Q(P)| \;\le\; (\rho_n - 1 + 2\eta_n)\,|P| \;=\; o(|P|).$$ In particular $|P \cap Q(P)| \ge (1-\eta_n)|P| \ge (1-\eta_n)\rho_n^{-1}|Q(P)| \gt \frac12 |Q(P)|$ for all large $n$: each retained transported component occupies a strict majority of its target. Distinct transported components are disjoint, so no two can both hold a majority of the same target: $P \mapsto Q(P)$ is injective on retained components (Fig. 12). The matched intersections $P \cap Q(P)$ cover $N - o(N)$ vertices.

The payoff of injectivity: $\Gamma$-words preserve transported components. Fix a $\Gamma$-word $w$. Discard vertices outside the matched intersections, vertices whose $p_w$-image leaves them, and vertices where $p_w$ crosses between original components — all $o(N)$. For each surviving $z$: both $z$ and $p_w z$ lie in matched intersections of the same original component; injectivity says only one transported component matches it, so both lie in the same member of $\mathcal P$: $$\#\{z : z \text{ and } p_w z \text{ lie in different members of } \mathcal P\} \;=\; o(N). \tag{10}$$ Now conjugate back. For $j \in J$, the hypothesis $t_1 J t_1^{-1} \le \Gamma$ lets us choose a fixed $\Gamma$-word $w_j$ representing $t_1 j t_1^{-1}$; put $$q_j \;=\; \tau^{-1} p_{w_j} \tau.$$ Approximate multiplicativity gives $q_j = p_j$ outside $o(N)$ vertices. Moreover $\tau q_j z = p_{w_j} \tau z$, and the transported component of $\tau z$ is $\tau C(z)$; so $z$ and $q_j z$ share an original component exactly when $\tau z$ and $p_{w_j}\tau z$ share a transported component. Applying (10) to $w_j$: $$\#\{z : C(q_j z) \neq C(z)\} \;=\; o(N). \tag{11}$$ Every fixed generator of $\Gamma$ or of $J$ now preserves the original-component partition outside $o(N)$ vertices.

Step 4: select one component carrying all required word tests. Choose finite symmetric generating sets $T_\Gamma = S_\Gamma$, $T_J \subseteq J \setminus \{1\}$, and $T = T_\Gamma \cup T_J$ (so $1 \in T_\Gamma$; $T_J$ empty is allowed if $J$ is trivial). For $s \in T_\Gamma$ define $q_s = p_s$; for $j \in T_J$ use the $q_j$ of Step 3. Normalize inverse labels, and make involutive $J$-labels exact by replacing all cycles of length $\gt 2$ with fixed points; these changes touch $o(N)$ vertices and perturb any fixed word test at only $o(N)$ starting points. Since $[\Gamma, J] = 1$ and $\Gamma \cap J = \{1\}$, the group $\Gamma \times J$ sits inside $G$, and the permutations $q_t$ pass each of its fixed equality and distinctness tests outside $o(N)$ vertices.

Collect every kind of exception into one explicit set. The generator exits are $$\mathcal C_n = \{z : C(q_t z) \neq C(z) \text{ for some } t \in T\}, \qquad |\mathcal C_n| = o(N)$$ (by the crossing estimates for $\Gamma$-generators and (11) for $J$-generators). Let $\Delta_n$ be the vertices incident to the edge-multiset difference between the $\Gamma$-generator graph and $L_\Gamma$; $|\Delta_n| = o(N)$. For $\ell \ge 0$ let $W_\ell$ be the (finite) set of formal words in the alphabet $T$ of length $\le \ell$, including the empty word; for $w \in W_\ell$ write $q_w$ for the product of permutations and $\overline w \in \Gamma \times J$ for the represented element. The word-test failures are $$F_{n,\ell} \;=\; \bigcup_{\substack{w, w' \in W_\ell \\ \overline w = \overline{w'}}} \{z : q_w z \neq q_{w'} z\} \;\cup\; \bigcup_{\substack{w, w' \in W_\ell \\ \overline w \neq \overline{w'}}} \{z : q_w z = q_{w'} z\},$$ and for $\ell \ge 1$ the full exceptional set is $$B_{n,\ell} \;=\; \Delta_n \,\cup\, F_{n,\ell} \,\cup \bigcup_{w \in W_{\ell-1}} q_w^{-1}(\mathcal C_n)$$ (the last term flags word paths that step across a component border at any prefix). For fixed $\ell$: $F_{n,\ell}$ is a finite union of equality tests (each fails at $o(N)$ points by approximate multiplicativity) and distinctness tests (each fails at $o(N)$ points because $p$ of a nonidentity element moves almost every point), so $|B_{n,\ell}| = o(N)$. Choose $\ell_n \to \infty$ slowly enough that $$e_n \;=\; \frac{|B_{n,\ell_n}|}{N} \longrightarrow 0, \qquad B_n = B_{n,\ell_n}.$$

Now pigeonhole over the original components, weighting by size. If every $Q \in \mathcal Q$ had $|Q \cap B_n| \gt e_n |Q|$, summing over $\mathcal Q$ would give $|B_n| \gt e_n N$ — false. So some component $Q_n \in \mathcal Q$ satisfies $$|Q_n \cap B_n| \;\le\; e_n\,|Q_n| \tag{12}$$ for every $n$. This one tile carries all the tests (Fig. 13). And it is large: for each element of the word-metric ball of $\Gamma$ (generating set $T_\Gamma$) of radius $\lfloor \ell_n/2 \rfloor$, fix a representing word of that length or shorter; starting at any $z \in Q_n \setminus B_n$, the exit tests keep every such word path inside $Q_n$ and the distinctness tests give different endpoints for different elements. The whole ball injects into $Q_n$; since $\Gamma$ is infinite, its balls grow without bound, so $$|Q_n| \longrightarrow \infty.$$

Step 5: complete the partial actions and remove the additive expansion error. Write $P = Q_n$ (an original component now). For one representative of each nonidentity inverse pair in $T$, restrict $q_t$ to its internal arcs $$A_t = \{z \in P : q_t z \in P\}, \qquad q_t : A_t \to q_t(A_t),$$ a partial bijection of $P$ whose missing domain $P \setminus A_t$ and missing range $P \setminus q_t(A_t)$ have equal cardinality; choose any bijection between them to complete it to a permutation $\widehat q_t \in \mathrm{Sym}(P)$ (for an involution the missing domain equals the missing range, complete by fixed points; use $\widehat q_t^{-1}$ for the inverse label, identity for $1$). Since $P \setminus A_t \subseteq \mathcal C_n \cap P \subseteq B_n \cap P$, the bound (12) says only $O(e_n |P|)$ values changed. Every word path of length $\le \ell_n$ from $P \setminus B_n$ stayed inside $P$ anyway, so its tests are untouched. To get maps on the whole group, fix once and for all a representative word $w_g$ for each $g \in \Gamma \times J$ (empty for $1$, the letter itself for $t \in T$) and set $\widehat p_n(g) = \widehat q_{w_g} \in \mathrm{Sym}(Q_n)$. For fixed $g, h$, the multiplication test compares $w_{gh}$ against the concatenation $w_g w_h$ — a fixed pair of formal words, covered by the $F$-tests once $\ell_n$ exceeds their lengths — and the distinctness test compares $w_g$ to the empty word. As $\ell_n \to \infty$, each fixed test's failure fraction tends to $0$: $\widehat p_n$ is a sofic approximation of $\Gamma \times J$ on $Q_n$.

One defect remains: expansion. Let $I_0$ be the $T_\Gamma$-generator multigraph of $\widehat p_n$. Since $\Delta_n \cup \mathcal C_n \subseteq B_n$, the edits relative to the original expander satisfy $|E(I_0) \,\triangle\, E(L_\Gamma[P])| = O_{|S_\Gamma|}(e_n |P|)$, so for some $a_n \to 0$, $$|\partial_{I_0} X| \;\ge\; \gamma_\Gamma \min\{|X|, |P \setminus X|\} - a_n |P| \qquad (X \subseteq P). \tag{13}$$ An additive error $a_n|P|$ is fatal for small sets ($X$ of size $\ll a_n|P|$ might have no boundary at all), so we excise the worst offender. Fix $0 \lt \lambda \lt \gamma_\Gamma$. If some nonempty $B_{\mathrm{cut}} \subseteq P$ has $|B_{\mathrm{cut}}| \le |P|/2$ and $|\partial_{I_0} B_{\mathrm{cut}}| \lt \lambda |B_{\mathrm{cut}}|$, choose such a set of maximum cardinality; otherwise $B_{\mathrm{cut}} = \emptyset$. By (13) applied to $B_{\mathrm{cut}}$: $\gamma_\Gamma |B_{\mathrm{cut}}| - a_n|P| \le |\partial_{I_0}B_{\mathrm{cut}}| \lt \lambda|B_{\mathrm{cut}}|$, so $$|B_{\mathrm{cut}}| \;\le\; \frac{a_n}{\gamma_\Gamma - \lambda}\,|P| \;=\; o(|P|).$$ Put $Z = P \setminus B_{\mathrm{cut}}$. Claim: for all large $n$, the induced graph $I_0[Z]$ is a $\lambda$-expander. Let $X \subseteq Z$, $0 \lt |X| \le |Z|/2$. If $|X| + |B_{\mathrm{cut}}| \le |P|/2$, then maximality of $B_{\mathrm{cut}}$ forces the union to expand: $\lambda(|X| + |B_{\mathrm{cut}}|) \le |\partial_{I_0}(X \cup B_{\mathrm{cut}})| \le |\partial_{I_0[Z]} X| + |\partial_{I_0} B_{\mathrm{cut}}|$, and subtracting $|\partial_{I_0}B_{\mathrm{cut}}| \lt \lambda|B_{\mathrm{cut}}|$ leaves $|\partial_{I_0[Z]}X| \ge \lambda|X|$ (if $B_{\mathrm{cut}} = \emptyset$ this is immediate from its defining maximality). Otherwise $|X| \gt |P|/2 - |B_{\mathrm{cut}}| = (\tfrac12 - o(1))|P|$, and with $d$ bounding degrees, (13) gives $$|\partial_{I_0[Z]} X| \;\ge\; \gamma_\Gamma |X| - a_n|P| - d\,|B_{\mathrm{cut}}| \;\ge\; \lambda |X|$$ for large $n$. The claim holds (Fig. 14).

The permutations $\widehat q_t$ still act on $P$; restrict each to $\{z \in Z : \widehat q_t z \in Z\}$ and complete to $\widetilde q_t \in \mathrm{Sym}(Z)$ exactly as before (fixed points for involutions, inverses for inverse labels). Each $\widetilde q_t$ differs from $\widehat q_t$ at $\le |B_{\mathrm{cut}}| = o(|Z|)$ vertices, every internal $\Gamma$-edge of $I_0[Z]$ survives, and completion edges can only add boundary — so the $T_\Gamma$-generator graph of $(\widetilde q_t)$ contains the $\lambda$-expander $I_0[Z]$ as a spanning submultigraph and is itself a $\lambda$-expander on all of $Z$. For any fixed word $w$, the $\widetilde q$-path and the $\widehat q$-path from $z$ first differ only when a prefix hits a repaired value, and prefixes act by permutations, so $$\bigl|\{z \in Z : \widetilde q_w z \neq \widehat q_w z\}\bigr| \;\le\; |w| \cdot |B_{\mathrm{cut}}| \;=\; o(|Z|):$$ every fixed equality, commutation, and distinctness test still fails at only $o(|Z|)$ vertices, and $|Z| = (1 - o(1))|P| \to \infty$. The maps $g \mapsto \widetilde q_{w_g}$ are therefore a sofic approximation of $\Gamma \times J$ whose $\Gamma$-generator graph is one uniform $\lambda$-expander on the entire set $Z$. Import 2 (Kun–Thom) applies with $K = \Gamma$ and concludes: $J$ is LEF. ∎

What was actually used. Property (T) of $\Gamma$ entered through Import 1 (original components expand) and Import 2 (the final step); property (T) of $G$ entered through Import 1 applied to $S_G$ (ambient components expand — the engine of Step 2's concentration). The compressions $t_i\Gamma t_i^{-1} \le \Gamma$ gave (4); the single compression $t_1 J t_1^{-1} \le \Gamma$ gave the $J$-preservation (11). Commutation $[\Gamma, J] = 1$ was needed only at the very end, to make $\Gamma \times J$ a group that the repaired permutations approximate. Every estimate was an $o(N)$ bookkeeping step; the only nonelementary inputs are the two imports.
