6. The binary-carry construction: $\Lambda$ and $\Gamma_0$

Now the toy is globalized. The plan: build a countable $K$-module $D = V \oplus B$ (the “untwisted” side, giving $\Lambda = D \rtimes K$), exhibit its dual $\widehat D = X \times Y$ concretely, and then install a second compact group law on the same space $X \times Y$ by inserting the binary carry — producing a compact group $C_0$ whose discrete dual $E_0$ gives the twisted group $\Gamma_0 = E_0 \rtimes K$.

6.1 The linear and quadratic modules

Let $V = R^4$ with standard basis $e_1, \dots, e_4$ over $R = \mathbb F_2[t]$, and set $e = e_1$. As an $\mathbb F_2$-vector space, $V$ has the countable basis $u = t^j e_i$ ($1 \le i \le 4$, $j \ge 0$). Define the squaring map and the divided square: $$b(v) = v \otimes_{\mathbb F_2} v \;\in\; V \otimes_{\mathbb F_2} V, \qquad B = \mathrm{span}_{\mathbb F_2}\{b(v) : v \in V\}, \qquad D = V \oplus B.$$ (The tensor product is over $\mathbb F_2$, not over $R$ — $B$ is huge.) $K$ acts on $V$ through $\pi_2 : K \twoheadrightarrow \mathrm{SL}_4(R)$, diagonally on $V \otimes V$; since $k\cdot b(v) = (k v)\otimes(k v) = b(k\cdot v)$, the action preserves $B$ and hence $D$, and $$0 \longrightarrow V \xrightarrow{\;v \mapsto (v,0)\;} D \xrightarrow{\;(v,w)\mapsto w\;} B \longrightarrow 0$$ is a split $K$-equivariant exact sequence of $\mathbb F_2$-vector spaces (so $D$ has exponent $2$). The map $b$ is not linear; its failure of linearity is measured by the polarization identity $$b(u + v) - b(u) - b(v) \;=\; u\otimes v + v \otimes u,$$ which is bilinear in $(u,v)$ — immediate by expanding $(u+v)\otimes(u+v)$.

Lemma I (structure of $B$). Let $(u_i)_{i \ge 1}$ be an ordered $\mathbb F_2$-basis of $V$. Then:
  1. the elements $u_i \otimes u_i$ and $u_i\otimes u_j + u_j \otimes u_i$ ($i \lt j$) form an $\mathbb F_2$-basis of $B$;
  2. for $v = \sum_i x_iu_i$ (finite sum, $x_i \in \mathbb F_2$), $$b(v) = \sum_i x_i\,(u_i\otimes u_i) \;+\; \sum_{i \lt j} x_ix_j\,(u_i\otimes u_j + u_j\otimes u_i);$$
  3. $B = (V\otimes_{\mathbb F_2} V)^{S_2} = \Gamma^2_{\mathbb F_2}(V)$, the subspace of tensors fixed by swapping the factors (the “divided square”) — and this is not the symmetric-square quotient $\mathrm{Sym}^2 V = (V\otimes V)_{S_2}$: in characteristic $2$ the canonical map from invariants to coinvariants kills every off-diagonal basis vector $u_i\otimes u_j + u_j\otimes u_i$.

(2) Expand $b(v) = \bigl(\sum x_iu_i\bigr)\otimes\bigl(\sum x_ju_j\bigr) = \sum_{i,j}x_ix_j\,u_i\otimes u_j$ and group the $(i,j)$ and $(j,i)$ terms (using $x_i^2 = x_i$ in $\mathbb F_2$). (1) By (2), every $b(v)$ lies in the span of the listed elements, so they span $B$; conversely each listed element lies in $B$: the diagonal ones as $b(u_i)$, and $u_i\otimes u_j + u_j\otimes u_i = b(u_i + u_j) + b(u_i) + b(u_j)$ by polarization (signs are moot mod $2$). Linear independence is inherited from the tensor-product basis $(u_i\otimes u_j)_{i,j}$ of $V \otimes V$. (3) Each listed basis vector is fixed by the flip $u\otimes w \mapsto w\otimes u$; conversely a flip-invariant tensor has symmetric coefficient matrix, hence is a combination of diagonal terms and matched off-diagonal pairs — exactly the span of the listed vectors. The canonical map to coinvariants sends $u_i\otimes u_j + u_j\otimes u_i \mapsto 2\,[u_i\otimes u_j] = 0$ in characteristic $2$. ∎

Dualize. By the third panel of Fig. 5, the duals of the countable $\mathbb F_2$-vector spaces $V$ and $B$ are the compact products $$X = V^* = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb F_2}(V, \mathbb F_2), \qquad Y = B^* = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb F_2}(B, \mathbb F_2),$$ with pointwise-convergence topology, and the pairing $(\ell, q) \mapsto \bigl((v,w) \mapsto (-1)^{\ell(v) + q(w)}\bigr)$ identifies $$\widehat D = X \times Y, \qquad m = m_X \otimes m_Y \;\text{(independent fair coins on all coordinates)},$$ with coordinatewise addition as group law and dual actions $(k\cdot\ell)(v) = \ell(k^{-1}v)$, $(k\cdot q)(w) = q(k^{-1}w)$. One reading of $Y$ is decisive for everything that follows (Fig. 11): by Lemma I(2), for $q \in Y$, $$q(b(v)) \;=\; \sum_i x_i\, q(u_i\otimes u_i) \;+\; \sum_{i\lt j} x_ix_j\, q(u_i\otimes u_j + u_j\otimes u_i),$$ so $Y$ parametrizes exactly the Boolean functions on $V$ of degree $\le 2$ with zero constant term: the diagonal coordinates of $q$ supply linear terms, the pair coordinates supply genuinely quadratic terms; on any finite-dimensional subspace, $v \mapsto q(b(v))$ is a Boolean polynomial of degree at most $2$.

6.2 The carry group $C_0$

For $a \in \mathbb F_2$ write $\tilde a \in \{0,1\}\subset\mathbb Z$ for its standard lift. Given a pair $(\ell, q) \in X \times Y$, define a $\mathbb Z/4$-valued function on $V$: $$F_{\ell,q} : V \to \mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z, \qquad F_{\ell,q}(v) \;=\; \widetilde{\ell(v)} + 2\,\widetilde{q(b(v))} \pmod 4,$$ — “$\ell$ is the ones digit, $q\circ b$ the twos digit” — and let $$C_0 = \{F_{\ell,q} : (\ell, q) \in X\times Y\} \subseteq (\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^V,$$ with the product topology on the ambient power and the action $(k\cdot F)(v) = F(k^{-1}\cdot v)$, which preserves $C_0$ because $k\cdot F_{\ell,q} = F_{k\cdot\ell,\,k\cdot q}$ (machine-checked for a sampled transvection in §10). The four-point carry identity, applied at every $v$ simultaneously, computes pointwise addition in $C_0$. For $s, s' \in \mathbb F_2$: $\tilde s + \tilde{s'} = \widetilde{s+s'} + 2\tilde s\tilde{s'} \pmod 4$; the cross-term is quadratic in $\ell$, and packaging it as an element of $Y$ is the key definition: $$r_{\ell,\ell'} \;=\; (\ell\otimes\ell')\big|_B \in Y, \qquad \text{i.e.} \quad r_{\ell,\ell'}(u\otimes w) = \ell(u)\,\ell'(w), \qquad \text{so } r_{\ell,\ell'}(b(v)) = \ell(v)\ell'(v).$$ Since the $b(v)$ span $B$, the map $(\ell,\ell')\mapsto r_{\ell,\ell'}$ is symmetric, bilinear, normalized ($r_{\ell,0} = 0$), continuous for the pointwise topologies (each evaluation uses finitely many coordinates), and $K$-equivariant; bilinearity gives the cocycle identity $r_{\ell,\ell'} + r_{\ell+\ell',\ell''} = r_{\ell',\ell''} + r_{\ell,\ell'+\ell''}$. Applying the carry identity at each $v \in V$: $$F_{\ell,q} + F_{\ell',q'} \;=\; F_{\ell+\ell',\; q+q'+r_{\ell,\ell'}}. \tag{4.2}$$ So $C_0$ contains $0 = F_{0,0}$ and is closed under addition; since the ambient group has exponent $4$, inverses exist inside ($-F = 3F$): $C_0$ is a subgroup. Moreover $(\ell,q)$ can be recovered from $F_{\ell,q}$ — reduction mod $2$ of the values gives $\ell$, and then the twos digits at all $v$ give $q(b(v))$, determining $q$ since the $b(v)$ span $B$ — so $(\ell,q)\mapsto F_{\ell,q}$ is a bijection under which pointwise addition becomes the twisted law $$(\ell,q)\star_0(\ell',q') \;=\; \bigl(\ell+\ell',\; q+q'+r_{\ell,\ell'}\bigr).$$

Proposition 4.1. $C_0$ is a compact subgroup of $(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^V$, and $$\Phi_0 : X\times Y \to C_0, \qquad \Phi_0(\ell,q) = F_{\ell,q}$$ is a $K$-equivariant homeomorphism carrying $m = m_X\otimes m_Y$ to the Haar measure of $C_0$. It is a topological group isomorphism from $(X\times Y, \star_0)$, but not a homomorphism for coordinatewise addition on $X \times Y = \widehat D$: the element $F_{\ell,0}$ with $\ell(e) = 1$ has order $4$, while $\widehat D$ has exponent $2$.

Each coordinate $F_{\ell,q}(v)$ depends continuously (in fact through finitely many coordinates) on $(\ell,q)$, so $\Phi_0$ is continuous; it is bijective by the recovery argument above. A continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism onto its image, so $C_0 = \Phi_0(X\times Y)$ is compact (hence closed) and $\Phi_0$ is a homeomorphism. Equivariance is the identity $k\cdot F_{\ell,q} = F_{k\ell,kq}$, which reduces to $b(k\cdot v) = k\cdot b(v)$.

Haar measure. Under $\Phi_0$, translation by $F_{\ell',q'}$ becomes, by (4.2), the triangular map $(\ell,q)\mapsto(\ell+\ell',\, q+q'+r_{\ell,\ell'})$ (Fig. 13). This map preserves $m$: the $X$-marginal is a translation of $X$, and for each fixed $\ell$ the fiber map $q \mapsto q + (q' + r_{\ell,\ell'})$ is a translation of $Y$; Fubini gives invariance. So $(\Phi_0)_*m$ is a translation-invariant Borel probability measure on the compact group $C_0$ — its Haar measure, by uniqueness.

Group law. (4.2) says precisely that $\Phi_0$ is an isomorphism for $\star_0$. For the failure with coordinatewise addition: pick $\ell$ with $\ell(e) = 1$ (exists: extend $e$ to a basis). Then $r_{\ell,\ell}(b(e)) = \ell(e)^2 = 1$, and $F_{\ell,0}(e) = 1 \in \mathbb Z/4$, so $F_{\ell,0} + F_{\ell,0} = F_{0, r_{\ell,\ell}} \neq 0$: the element has order $4$ (its value at $e$ is $1$, of additive order $4$). An isomorphism to the exponent-$2$ group $\widehat D$ is impossible. ∎

6.3 Dualizing the carry: $E_0$, $\Gamma_0$, and the first counterexample

The carry is invisible to the measured space but survives duality. We use the standard duality package for compact/discrete abelian groups:

Import 6 (Pontryagin duality package; standard harmonic analysis). For compact metrizable abelian $C$ and countable discrete abelian $A$: (i) the dual of a compact metrizable group is countable discrete (its characters are an orthonormal set in the separable $L^2(C)$ — orthogonality exactly as in Lemma E), and biduality holds: $\widehat{\widehat C\,} \cong C$ canonically; (ii) a short exact sequence of such groups with continuous maps dualizes to a short exact sequence in the opposite direction (annihilator duality); (iii) restriction of characters along a closed subgroup is onto the subgroup's dual. All three are classical [e.g. Rudin, Fourier Analysis on Groups].

Let $E_0 = \widehat{C_0}$, written additively, with the dual $K$-action; it is countable by Import 6(i). The stars of the show are the evaluation characters: for $v \in V$, $$\varepsilon^{(0)}_v \in E_0, \qquad \varepsilon^{(0)}_v(F) = i^{\,F(v)} \quad (i = \sqrt{-1}),$$ — a character because $F \mapsto F(v)$ is a homomorphism $C_0 \to \mathbb Z/4$ and $x \mapsto i^x$ embeds $\mathbb Z/4$ into $\mathbb T$. Immediately $4\varepsilon^{(0)}_e = 0$; and choosing $\ell$ with $\ell(e) = 1$ gives $\varepsilon^{(0)}_e(F_{\ell,0}) = i^1 = i$, so $\varepsilon^{(0)}_e$ has order exactly $4$: the carry, reborn as torsion in a discrete group.

Lemma 4.2 (the two ladders). There are $K$-equivariant exact sequences of compact groups $$0 \to Y \to \widehat D = X\times Y \to X \to 0, \qquad 0 \to Y \xrightarrow{\;\jmath_0\;} C_0 \xrightarrow{\;\rho_0\;} X \to 0,$$ with $\jmath_0(q) = F_{0,q}$ and $\rho_0(F_{\ell,q}) = \ell$, whose Pontryagin duals are $$0 \to V \to D = V\oplus B \to B \to 0, \qquad 0 \to V \xrightarrow{\;\iota_0\;} E_0 \xrightarrow{\;\sigma_0\;} B \to 0,$$ where $\iota_0(v)(F_{\ell,q}) = (-1)^{\ell(v)}$ and $\sigma_0$ is characterized by $\eta(F_{0,q}) = (-1)^{q(\sigma_0(\eta))}$ for all $q$. The $\widehat D$/$D$ sequences split $K$-equivariantly; the $C_0$/$E_0$ sequences do not split, even as plain abelian groups. Moreover $$2\varepsilon^{(0)}_v = \iota_0(v), \qquad \sigma_0(\varepsilon^{(0)}_v) = b(v).$$

The $\widehat D$ and $D$ sequences split by their product/direct-sum structure. For the carry sequence: (4.2) shows $\jmath_0$ and $\rho_0$ are continuous homomorphisms ($\jmath_0$: no carry when $\ell = 0$; $\rho_0$: the $\ell$-parts add on the nose), with $\ker\rho_0 = \{F_{0,q}\} = \jmath_0(Y)$ and $\rho_0$ onto ($\rho_0(F_{\ell,0}) = \ell$); all maps are $K$-equivariant. Import 6(ii) dualizes it to $0 \to \widehat X \to E_0 \to \widehat Y \to 0$; identify $\widehat X = V$ and $\widehat Y = B$ (biduality for the vector-space dualities of §6.1). Unwinding the identifications: the image of $v \in V$ is the character $F \mapsto (-1)^{(\rho_0 F)(v)} = (-1)^{\ell(v)}$, which is $\iota_0(v)$; and $\sigma_0(\eta)$ is the element of $B$ representing $\eta\circ\jmath_0 \in \widehat Y$, i.e. the characterization stated. For the evaluation characters: $$\varepsilon^{(0)}_v(F_{0,q}) = i^{2\widetilde{q(b(v))}} = (-1)^{q(b(v))} \implies \sigma_0(\varepsilon^{(0)}_v) = b(v),$$ $$\bigl(\varepsilon^{(0)}_v\bigr)^2(F_{\ell,q}) = i^{2F_{\ell,q}(v)} = (-1)^{F_{\ell,q}(v)} = (-1)^{\ell(v)} \implies 2\varepsilon^{(0)}_v = \iota_0(v)$$ (the twos digit contributes $(-1)^{2(\cdot)} = 1$). Since the $b(v)$ span $B$, the characters $\varepsilon^{(0)}_v$ also witness surjectivity of $\sigma_0$ directly. Finally, if the discrete sequence split, $E_0 \cong V\oplus B$ would have exponent $2$, contradicting the order-$4$ element $\varepsilon^{(0)}_e$; dualizing back (Import 6), $\rho_0$ admits no continuous homomorphic section. ∎

Define the two semidirect products of the section title: $$\Lambda \;=\; D \rtimes K, \qquad \Gamma_0 \;=\; E_0 \rtimes K.$$

Proposition 4.3. $L(\Gamma_0) \cong L(\Lambda)$, but $\Gamma_0 \not\cong \Lambda$.

By Proposition F (the chapter's (2.1)) applied to $E_0$, then Proposition 4.1's identification $\Phi_0$ of measured $K$-spaces $(X\times Y, m) \cong (C_0, \mathrm{Haar})$ — note $\widehat{E_0} = C_0$ by biduality — then Proposition F again in reverse for $D$: $$L(\Gamma_0) \cong L^\infty(C_0)\rtimes K \cong L^\infty(X\times Y, m)\rtimes K \cong L(\Lambda),$$ the middle isomorphism being the transport principle (Proposition G) along $\theta = \Phi_0^{-1}$.

Nonisomorphism: since $K$ is torsion-free, any finite-order $(d, k) \in \Lambda$ has $k^m = 1$ in $K$ (project to $K$), so $k = 1$, so the element lies in $D$ — which has exponent $2$. Thus $\Lambda$ has no element of order $4$. But $\varepsilon^{(0)}_e \in E_0 \subset \Gamma_0$ has order $4$ (Lemma 4.2). ∎

This pair already refutes Connes's conjecture — provided the groups are ICC with property (T), which is the business of the next section.
