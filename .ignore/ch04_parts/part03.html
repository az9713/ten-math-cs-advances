5. The acting group $K$: torsion-free, ICC, property (T)

The construction needs a countable group $K$ with four properties at once: property (T), the ICC property, torsion-freeness (crucial: it will make the torsion of $\Gamma_n$ an intrinsic invariant), and a surjection onto $Q = \mathrm{SL}_4(R)$ for the characteristic-two polynomial ring $$R = \mathbb F_2[t].$$ The natural candidate $Q$ itself fails torsion-freeness (Remark below), so the chapter lifts to characteristic zero and imposes a congruence condition at the different prime $3$: $$K \;=\; \ker\Bigl( \mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb Z[t]) \xrightarrow{\;g(t)\,\mapsto\, g(0)\ \mathrm{mod}\ 3\;} \mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb F_3)\Bigr),$$ the matrices over $\mathbb Z[t]$ whose constant term reduces to the identity mod $3$ (Fig. 8). Both $K$ and $Q$ are countable.

Recall from the Chapter 3 explainer the elementary linear group: for a commutative ring $S$, $$\mathrm{EL}_n(S) = \bigl\langle I + fE_{ij} \;:\; f \in S,\ i \neq j \bigr\rangle \;\le\; \mathrm{SL}_n(S),$$ where $E_{ij}$ is the $(i,j)$ matrix unit. Two imported theorems power the property-(T) claim:

Import 3 (Suslin [Sus77, Cor. 6.6]). For $n \ge 3$, $\;\mathrm{EL}_n(\mathbb Z[t]) = \mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb Z[t])$: every determinant-one matrix over $\mathbb Z[t]$ is a product of elementary matrices. (This uses that $\mathbb Z$ is a regular ring of Krull dimension $1$ with $SK_1(\mathbb Z) = 0$; it is the polynomial-ring analogue of Gaussian elimination, and genuinely deep — over some rings it fails.)
Import 2 (Ershov–Jaikin-Zapirain [EJ10, Thm. 1.1]). For every finitely generated associative unital ring $S$ and $n \ge 3$, the group $\mathrm{EL}_n(S)$ has property (T). (The identical import, with the identical citation, served as Import 3 of the Chapter 3 explainer.)

Combining: $\mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb Z[t]) = \mathrm{EL}_4(\mathbb Z[t])$ has property (T).

Proposition 3.1. $K$ has property (T).

The map $g(t) \mapsto g(0) \bmod 3$ is a group homomorphism into the finite group $\mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb F_3)$, so its kernel $K$ has finite index in $\mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb Z[t])$ (at most $|\mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb F_3)|$ cosets). Property (T) passes to finite-index subgroups (Import 5). ∎

Next, torsion-freeness, in two layers: a classical congruence lemma at the prime $3$ handles constant terms, and a $t$-adic argument handles the rest (Fig. 9).

Lemma 3.2 (level-3 congruence lemma; Minkowski). For every $d \ge 2$, the group $\ker\bigl(\mathrm{SL}_d(\mathbb Z) \to \mathrm{SL}_d(\mathbb F_3)\bigr)$ is torsion-free.

If the kernel had a nonidentity torsion element, it would have one of prime order $p$ (take a suitable power). Write it as $A = I + 3^r B$ with $r \ge 1$ maximal, so that $B \not\equiv 0 \pmod 3$.

Case $p \neq 3$. Expand $A^p = (I + 3^rB)^p = I + p\,3^r B + \binom{p}{2}3^{2r}B^2 + \cdots$; every term after the second is divisible by $3^{2r}$, and $2r \ge r+1$. So $I = A^p \equiv I + p\,3^rB \pmod{3^{r+1}}$, forcing $p\,B \equiv 0 \pmod 3$; as $p$ is invertible mod $3$, $B \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ — contradicting maximality of $r$.

Case $p = 3$. Now $A^3 = I + 3^{r+1}B + 3^{2r+1}B^2 + 3^{3r}B^3$, so $$0 \;=\; \frac{A^3 - I}{3^{r+1}} \;=\; B + 3^rB^2 + 3^{2r-1}B^3.$$ Since $r \ge 1$, both correction terms are divisible by $3$, and reduction mod $3$ again gives $B \equiv 0 \pmod 3$, a contradiction. ∎

Lemma 3.3. $K$ is torsion-free and ICC, and reduction mod $2$ gives a surjection $\pi_2 : K \twoheadrightarrow Q = \mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb F_2[t])$.

Torsion-free. Let $g(t) \in K$ with $g(t)^m = I$, $m \ge 1$. The constant term $g(0)$ is then a torsion element of $\ker(\mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb Z) \to \mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb F_3))$, so $g(0) = I$ by Lemma 3.2. If $g \neq I$, let $r \ge 1$ be the lowest $t$-degree where it differs from $I$: $g(t) = I + t^rA(t)$ with $A(0) \neq 0$. Then, working mod $t^{r+1}$ (only the linear term in $t^rA$ survives), $$g(t)^m \;\equiv\; I + m\,t^r A(0) \;\not\equiv\; I \pmod{t^{r+1}},$$ because $mA(0) \neq 0$ over the characteristic-zero domain $\mathbb Z$ — contradicting $g^m = I$. (This is exactly where lifting to $\mathbb Z[t]$ pays: over $\mathbb F_2[t]$ the coefficient $m A(0)$ can vanish.)

ICC. For $i \neq j$ and $f \in \mathbb Z[t]$ set $u_{ij}(f) = I + 3fE_{ij}$; its constant term is $\equiv I$ mod $3$, so $u_{ij}(f) \in K$. Let $g \in K$, $g \neq I$. A matrix commuting with all the $E_{ij}$ is scalar, and a scalar $cI \in \mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb Z[t])$ has $c^4 = 1$, so $c = \pm1$ and $g$ would have finite order $\le 2$ — impossible in the torsion-free $K$ unless $g = I$. So some $[E_{ij}, g] = E_{ij}g - gE_{ij} \neq 0$. If two parameters give the same conjugate, $u_{ij}(f)\,g\,u_{ij}(f)^{-1} = u_{ij}(h)\,g\,u_{ij}(h)^{-1}$, then $g$ commutes with $u_{ij}(f)u_{ij}(h)^{-1} = u_{ij}(f - h)$, which expands to $3(f-h)[E_{ij}, g] = 0$; as $\mathbb Z[t]$ is an integral domain and $[E_{ij},g] \neq 0$, this forces $f = h$. Distinct $f$'s therefore give distinct conjugates: the class of $g$ is infinite.

Surjectivity of $\pi_2$. Reduction mod $2$ sends $u_{ij}(f) \mapsto I + \bar f E_{ij}$ (note $3 \equiv 1$ mod $2$), and $f \mapsto \bar f$ maps $\mathbb Z[t]$ onto $R = \mathbb F_2[t]$: every elementary matrix over $R$ is hit. By Lemma H below, the elementary matrices generate $\mathrm{SL}_4(R)$, so $\pi_2$ is onto. ∎

Lemma H (Euclidean elementary generation). If $S$ is a Euclidean domain whose only unit is $1$ (e.g. $S = \mathbb F_2[t]$ with the degree function), then $\mathrm{SL}_n(S) = \mathrm{EL}_n(S)$ for every $n \ge 2$.

Three standard moves, all products of elementary matrices: (i) add an $S$-multiple of one row to another (that is an elementary matrix, acting on the left); (ii) the same for columns (acting on the right); (iii) swap two rows up to sign, via $\mathrm{row}_i \mathrel{+}= \mathrm{row}_j$, $\mathrm{row}_j \mathrel{-}= \mathrm{row}_i$, $\mathrm{row}_i \mathrel{+}= \mathrm{row}_j$.

Let $A \in \mathrm{SL}_n(S)$. Run Euclid on the first column: while it has two nonzero entries, subtract a multiple of the one of smaller degree from the other to strictly reduce the larger degree (division with remainder); iterate until one nonzero entry $d$ remains, and bring it to the top by (iii). Elementary operations do not change the determinant, and expanding along the first column shows $d$ divides $\det A = 1$; since the only unit is $1$, $d = 1$. Clear the rest of the first row with column operations (ii). The matrix is now $\begin{pmatrix}1 & 0\\ 0 & A'\end{pmatrix}$ with $A' \in \mathrm{SL}_{n-1}(S)$; induct, down to the trivial $1 \times 1$ case. The product of the inverse moves expresses $A$ as a product of elementary matrices. ∎

Remark 3.4 (why not just use $Q$?). The orbit and module computations below factor through $Q$ — but $Q$ itself cannot serve as the acting group, because it has torsion of exactly the wrong order: $$u = I + E_{12} + E_{23} \in Q, \qquad u^2 = I + E_{13} \neq I, \qquad u^4 = I$$ (squares over $\mathbb F_2$: with $N = E_{12}+E_{23}$, $u^2 = I + N^2 = I + E_{13}$ since $2N = 0$ and $N^2 = E_{13}$; then $u^4 = I + N^4 = I$). An order-$4$ element in the acting group would contaminate the very invariant — order-$4$ torsion — that distinguishes $\Gamma_0$ from $\Lambda$. Torsion-freeness of $K$ quarantines all torsion of $\Gamma_n = E_n \rtimes K$ inside $E_n$, which is what makes $\mathrm{Tor}(\Gamma_n) = E_n$ an intrinsic subgroup in §8.
Milestone. $K$ is countable, torsion-free, ICC, has property (T), and surjects onto $Q = \mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb F_2[t])$ via $\pi_2$. Through $\pi_2$, $K$ acts on every $\mathbb F_2[t]$-module in sight; through its torsion-freeness, the twisted groups built next will carry their fingerprints in their torsion subgroups.
