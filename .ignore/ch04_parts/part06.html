8. The infinite family $\Gamma_n$

One counterexample down; now the infinite fiber. The idea is almost embarrassingly simple: slide the carry. Instead of feeding position $j$'s carry from position $j$, feed it from position $j + n$ — the first $n$ coordinate positions in each of the four directions then never participate in any carry. Each shift $n$ gives a new compact group law $\star_n$ on the same measured $K$-space, hence a new group $\Gamma_n$ with the same factor; and the shift is recoverable from the abstract group.

8.1 Pulling back the carry

For $n \ge 0$ define the shift $$T_n : X \to X, \qquad (T_n\ell)(v) = \ell(t^nv).$$ Because $K$ acts $R$-linearly on $V$ (matrices over $R$ commute with multiplication by $t^n$), $T_n$ is $K$-equivariant; it is continuous (each output coordinate reads one input coordinate) and surjective (a functional prescribed on $t^nV$ extends to $V$). Its kernel is $$Z_n = \{\ell \in X : \ell|_{t^nV} = 0\} \;\cong\; \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb F_2}(V/t^nV, \mathbb F_2), \qquad |Z_n| = 2^{4n}$$ (since $V/t^nV \cong (R/(t^n))^4$ has $\mathbb F_2$-dimension $4n$; the count $|Z_n| = 2^{4n}$ is machine-checked in §10). Now pull the cocycle back through the shift and twist: $$c_n(\ell, \ell') = r_{T_n\ell,\, T_n\ell'} \in Y, \qquad (\ell,q)\star_n(\ell',q') = \bigl(\ell+\ell',\; q+q'+c_n(\ell,\ell')\bigr). \tag{6.3}$$ In coordinates $x_{i,j} = \ell(t^je_i)$ and $y_{i,j} = q(b(t^je_i))$: since $c_n(\ell,\ell')(b(t^je_i)) = x_{i,j+n}\,x'_{i,j+n}$, the coordinate pair $(x_{i,j+n},\, y_{i,j})$ adds by the four-point carry law $\diamond$ of §4, while the first $n$ coordinates $x_{i,0},\dots,x_{i,n-1}$ of each direction feed no carry at all (Fig. 18). For $n = 0$, Proposition 4.1's map $\Phi_0$ identifies $(X\times Y, \star_0)$ with the function-space group $C_0$; we adopt this as a standing identification and write $$C_n = (X\times Y, \star_n) \qquad (n \ge 0).$$

Lemma 6.1. For every $n \ge 0$, $\star_n$ makes $C_n$ a compact abelian group with Haar measure $m$, and $K$ acts on $C_n$ by continuous automorphisms through the coordinate action $k\cdot(\ell,q) = (k\cdot\ell, k\cdot q)$ — which does not depend on $n$.

$c_n$ inherits continuity, symmetry, bilinearity, and $K$-equivariance from $r$ and $T_n$. Bilinearity gives the cocycle identity $c_n(\ell,\ell') + c_n(\ell+\ell',\ell'') = c_n(\ell',\ell'') + c_n(\ell,\ell'+\ell'')$, which is exactly associativity of (6.3); symmetry gives commutativity; $(0,0)$ is the identity and $(\ell,\, q + c_n(\ell,\ell))$ inverts $(\ell, q)$ (check: the carry term produced on adding is $c_n(\ell,\ell)$, cancelling). The law is continuous, so $C_n$ is a compact abelian topological group; equivariance of $c_n$ makes each $k$ an automorphism. Translations are triangular over $X$ exactly as in Proposition 4.1, so they preserve $m$; a translation-invariant Borel probability measure on a compact group is its Haar measure. ∎

Two maps organize the bookkeeping. The projection $\rho_n(\ell,q) = \ell$ exhibits $C_n$ as an extension of $X$ by $Y$; and comparing carries, $$p_n : C_n \to C_0, \qquad p_n(\ell, q) = (T_n\ell,\, q)$$ is a continuous surjective $K$-equivariant homomorphism — because $c_n(\ell,\ell') = r_{T_n\ell, T_n\ell'}$ literally says “the $\star_n$-carry is the $\star_0$-carry of the shifted arguments” — with $$\ker p_n = Z_n \times \{0\}, \qquad |{\ker p_n}| = 2^{4n}.$$ So $p_n$ forgets exactly the $4n$ carry-free bits. (A continuous linear section of $T_n$ shows $C_n \cong C_0 \times (\mathbb Z/2)^{4n}$ as compact groups — but for $n \gt 0$ this splitting is never $K$-equivariant, and §8.5 will extract $n$ from precisely this failure.)

8.2 The dual groups $E_n$ and the groups $\Gamma_n$

Set $$E_n = \widehat{C_n}, \qquad \Gamma_n = E_n \rtimes K,$$ consistent (for $n = 0$) with §6 under the standing identification. Define $\iota_n(v)(\ell,q) = (-1)^{\ell(v)}$ for $v \in V$, and let $\sigma_n(\eta) \in B$ be determined by $\eta(0, q) = (-1)^{q(\sigma_n(\eta))}$ for all $q \in Y$ (restriction to $\{0\}\times Y$ is a character of $Y$, and $\widehat Y = B$).

Lemma 6.2. $E_n$ is countable and fits in the $K$-equivariant exact sequence $$0 \longrightarrow V \xrightarrow{\;\iota_n\;} E_n \xrightarrow{\;\sigma_n\;} B \longrightarrow 0. \tag{6.7}$$ For $v \in V$ define $\varepsilon^{(n)}_v = \varepsilon^{(0)}_v \circ p_n \in E_n$. Then $$\sigma_n\bigl(\varepsilon^{(n)}_v\bigr) = b(v), \qquad 2\,\varepsilon^{(n)}_v = \iota_n(t^nv). \tag{6.8}$$ In particular $\varepsilon^{(n)}_e$ has order $4$.

Dualize the compact exact sequence $0 \to Y \to C_n \xrightarrow{\rho_n} X \to 0$ (Import 6(ii)) and identify $\widehat X = V$, $\widehat Y = B$: this is (6.7), with the stated formulas for $\iota_n, \sigma_n$ falling out of the identifications exactly as in Lemma 4.2. Countability is Import 6(i) ($C_n$ compact metrizable). For (6.8), evaluate: writing elements of $C_n$ as pairs $(\ell,q)$ and recalling $p_n(\ell,q) = (T_n\ell, q) \leftrightarrow F_{T_n\ell,\,q}$, $$\varepsilon^{(n)}_v(0, q) = \varepsilon^{(0)}_v(F_{0,q}) = (-1)^{q(b(v))} \implies \sigma_n(\varepsilon^{(n)}_v) = b(v),$$ $$\bigl(\varepsilon^{(n)}_v(\ell,q)\bigr)^2 = i^{\,2F_{T_n\ell, q}(v)} = (-1)^{(T_n\ell)(v)} = (-1)^{\ell(t^nv)} \implies 2\varepsilon^{(n)}_v = \iota_n(t^nv).$$ Since $t^ne \neq 0$ and $\iota_n$ is injective, $2\varepsilon^{(n)}_e \neq 0$, and $4\varepsilon^{(n)}_e = 2\iota_n(t^nv)|_{v=e} = 0$: order $4$. ∎

Read (6.8) twice. Every $E_n$ contains order-four lifts of every $b(v)$ — so no $E_n$ is the untwisted $D$ — but the double of the lift is $\iota_n(t^nv)$: the carry's location $n$ is stamped into the doubling map. §8.5 turns that stamp into an isomorphism invariant.

8.3 Common factor, and rigidity for free

Proposition 6.3. $L(\Gamma_n) \cong L(\Lambda)$ for every $n \ge 0$.

By Lemma 6.1 the identity map $(C_n, m) \to (X\times Y, m)$ is a $K$-equivariant isomorphism of probability spaces (the action is coordinatewise and $n$-independent; only the group laws differ). Propositions F and G give $$L(\Gamma_n) \cong L^\infty(C_n, m)\rtimes K \cong L^\infty(X\times Y, m)\rtimes K \cong L(\Lambda).$$ ∎

Proposition 6.4. Every $\Gamma_n$ is ICC and has property (T).

$\Lambda$ is ICC with property (T) (Propositions 5.3, 5.9); $L(\Gamma_n) \cong L(\Lambda)$ (Proposition 6.3); transfer both through the factor (Lemma 2.1). ∎

8.4 Finite-index embeddings and commensurability

Proposition 6.5. For every $n \ge 0$, $\Gamma_n$ contains a subgroup isomorphic to $\Gamma_0$ of index $2^{4n}$; consequently the groups $(\Gamma_n)_{n\ge0}$ are mutually commensurable.

Dualize the compact exact sequence $0 \to Z_n\times\{0\} \to C_n \xrightarrow{p_n} C_0 \to 0$ (Import 6(ii)): $$0 \longrightarrow E_0 \xrightarrow{\;p_n^*\;} E_n \xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{res}_{Z_n}\;} \widehat{Z_n} \longrightarrow 0, \qquad p_n^*(\eta) = \eta\circ p_n,$$ where $\mathrm{res}_{Z_n}(\eta)$ is the restriction $\ell \mapsto \eta(\ell, 0)$ to the kernel (onto, by Import 6(iii)). The embedding $p_n^*$ is $K$-equivariant, so $p_n^*(E_0)\rtimes K \cong \Gamma_0$ sits inside $\Gamma_n = E_n\rtimes K$ with index $$[\Gamma_n : p_n^*(E_0)\rtimes K] = [E_n : p_n^*(E_0)] = |\widehat{Z_n}| = |Z_n| = 2^{4n}$$ (a finite abelian group has $|\widehat{Z}| = |Z|$: its characters biject with it, e.g. by the structure theorem, or by counting an orthonormal basis of functions on $Z$). Each $\Gamma_n$ thus shares the finite-index subgroup (a copy of) $\Gamma_0$ with every other: mutual commensurability (Fig. 19). ∎

8.5 Recovering $n$: the intrinsic invariant

By (6.3), doubling in $C_n$ is $2(\ell,q) = (0,\, c_n(\ell,\ell))$, so $C_n$ — and dually $E_n$ — has exponent dividing $4$. Since $K$ is torsion-free, an element $(\eta, k) \in \Gamma_n$ of finite order must have $k = 1$; hence $$\mathrm{Tor}(\Gamma_n) = E_n,$$ a characteristic abelian subgroup of the abstract group $\Gamma_n$ — any isomorphism of the $\Gamma$'s must match up the $E$'s and intertwine the conjugation actions of the quotients $\Gamma_n/E_n \cong K$. For an abelian group $A$ write $A[2] = \{a : 2a = 0\}$ and $2A = \{2a\}$; both are preserved by automorphisms, so $$A_n \;=\; E_n[2]\,/\,2E_n$$ with its $\Gamma_n/E_n$-action is intrinsic, and so is the count of its finite-orbit elements: $$i(\Gamma_n) \;=\; \bigl|\{a \in A_n : \text{the } (\Gamma_n/E_n)\text{-orbit of } a \text{ is finite}\}\bigr|.$$ We now compute $i(\Gamma_n) = 2^{4n}$, through three lemmas that make the doubling map completely explicit.

Lemma 6.6. There is a unique $K$-equivariant surjective $\mathbb F_2$-linear map $d : B \to V$ with $d(b(v)) = v$ for all $v$.

Fix an ordered basis $(u_i)$ of $V$ and define $d$ on the basis of $B$ from Lemma I: $u_i\otimes u_i \mapsto u_i$ and $u_i\otimes u_j + u_j\otimes u_i \mapsto 0$ ($i \lt j$). Then the expansion of Lemma I(2) gives $d(b(v)) = \sum_i x_iu_i = v$ (the quadratic terms die). Since the $b(v)$ span $B$, the property $d(b(v)) = v$ determines $d$ on a spanning set, giving uniqueness; surjectivity is clear; and equivariance follows on the spanning set: $d(k\cdot b(v)) = d(b(k v)) = kv = k\cdot d(b(v))$. ∎

Lemma 6.7 (the doubling formula). For every $n \ge 0$ and $\eta \in E_n$, $$2\eta \;=\; \iota_n\bigl(t^n\, d(\sigma_n(\eta))\bigr). \tag{6.10}$$ Consequently $$2E_n = \iota_n(t^nV) \qquad\text{and}\qquad E_n[2] = \sigma_n^{-1}(\ker d). \tag{6.11}$$

Both sides of (6.10) are homomorphisms in $\eta$ (composites of the homomorphisms $\sigma_n$, $d$, multiplication by $t^n$, $\iota_n$, and doubling). They agree on the generators of $E_n$: on $\varepsilon^{(n)}_v$, the left side is $\iota_n(t^nv)$ by (6.8) and the right side is $\iota_n(t^n d(b(v))) = \iota_n(t^nv)$; on $\iota_n(v)$, both sides vanish ($\iota_n(v)$ has order $\le 2$; and $\sigma_n\circ\iota_n = 0$ by exactness). These elements generate: the $\sigma_n$-images $b(v)$ of the $\varepsilon$'s span $B$, and $\ker\sigma_n = \iota_n(V)$. This proves (6.10). Then $2E_n = \iota_n(t^n\,d(\sigma_n(E_n))) = \iota_n(t^nV)$ ($\sigma_n$ and $d$ are onto), and $2\eta = 0 \iff t^nd(\sigma_n\eta) = 0 \iff d(\sigma_n\eta) = 0$ (multiplication by $t^n$ on $V$ and $\iota_n$ are injective), i.e. $E_n[2] = \sigma_n^{-1}(\ker d)$. ∎

Lemma 6.8. There is a $K$-equivariant exact sequence $$0 \longrightarrow V/t^nV \longrightarrow A_n \longrightarrow \ker d \longrightarrow 0, \tag{6.13}$$ with maps $v + t^nV \mapsto \iota_n(v) + 2E_n$ and $\eta + 2E_n \mapsto \sigma_n(\eta)$. Under the resulting identification of $V/t^nV$ with its image, $$A_n^{\mathrm{fin}} \;=\; \{a \in A_n : K\cdot a \text{ finite}\} \;=\; V/t^nV, \qquad |A_n^{\mathrm{fin}}| = 2^{4n}.$$

Restrict $\sigma_n$ to $E_n[2] = \sigma_n^{-1}(\ker d)$: it maps onto $\ker d$ (any preimage $\eta$ of $w \in \ker d$ has $2\eta = \iota_n(t^nd(w)) = 0$) with kernel $\iota_n(V)$; quotient the resulting sequence $0 \to V \to E_n[2] \to \ker d \to 0$ by $2E_n = \iota_n(t^nV) \subseteq \iota_n(V)$ to get (6.13).

Finite orbits: $V/t^nV$ is a finite set ($2^{4n}$ elements), so all its orbits are finite. Conversely, if $a \in A_n$ has nonzero image $w \in \ker d \subseteq B$, then $w$ has an infinite $K$-orbit (Lemma 5.2 via $K \twoheadrightarrow Q$), and the equivariant map $A_n \to \ker d$ sends the orbit of $a$ onto the orbit of $w$: the orbit of $a$ is infinite. So the finite-orbit part is exactly $V/t^nV \cong (R/(t^n))^4$, of order $2^{4n}$. ∎

Proposition 6.9. The groups $(\Gamma_n)_{n \ge 0}$ are pairwise nonisomorphic.

An abstract isomorphism $\Gamma_n \cong \Gamma_m$ carries $\mathrm{Tor}(\Gamma_n) = E_n$ onto $\mathrm{Tor}(\Gamma_m) = E_m$, hence induces an isomorphism $A_n \cong A_m$ intertwining the intrinsic quotient-group actions, hence a bijection of finite-orbit parts. Lemma 6.8 gives $2^{4n} = i(\Gamma_n) = i(\Gamma_m) = 2^{4m}$, so $n = m$. ∎
