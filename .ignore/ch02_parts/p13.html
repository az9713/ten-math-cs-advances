13. The asymptotic spherical bound (Theorem 1.2)

Scale everything with $n$: fix the level $r$, let the stabilizer rows grow as $\mu_m = b_mn+o(n)$ and cap the ambient rows at $\lambda_\ell \le a_\ell n + o(n)$, demanding strict interlacing of the limit slopes:

$$a_1\gt b_1\gt a_2\gt\cdots\gt b_r\gt a_{r+1}\ge 0. \tag{80}$$

(For $r=0$: just $a_1\ge0$.) Two changes of variable organize every formula. The quadratic coordinates and the level-zero weight function:

$$A(u) = u(1+u),\qquad q(u) = \frac{\sqrt{u(1+u)}}{1+2u},\qquad x_\ell = A(a_\ell),\quad y_m = A(b_m),$$

(A is strictly increasing on $[0,\infty)$, so the $x$'s and $y$'s interlace strictly too), and the interpolation residues, spectral strength, and dimension exponent (paper's (81)):

$$R_\ell = \frac{\displaystyle\prod_{m=1}^{r}(x_\ell-y_m)}{\displaystyle\prod_{q\ne\ell}(x_\ell-x_q)}, \qquad \Gamma_r(a,b) = \sum_{\ell=1}^{r+1}R_\ell\,q(a_\ell), \qquad \Phi_r(a,b) = \sum_{\ell=1}^{r+1}H_{\mathrm{sph}}(a_\ell)-\sum_{m=1}^{r}H_{\mathrm{sph}}(b_m). \tag{81}$$

The $R_\ell$ are the residues of the rational function $m(z) = \prod_m(z-y_m)/\prod_\ell(z-x_\ell)$ at its poles $x_\ell$: strict interlacing makes every $R_\ell\gt0$ (numerator factors alternate in sign in step with the denominator's), and comparing $z\,m(z)\to1$ as $z\to\infty$ with the partial-fraction expansion $m(z) = \sum_\ell R_\ell/(z-x_\ell)$ gives $\sum_\ell R_\ell = 1$: the residues are a probability distribution on the ambient rows. This innocuous-looking fact is the load-bearing wall of §§15–16.

13.1 Local spectral geometry: the box and the sines

Lemma 7.1 (limiting weights). Under (80) with $\lambda_\ell/n\to a_\ell$, $\mu_m/n\to b_m$, every existing transition satisfies $$p_{\ell,\pm}(\lambda;\mu)\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{R_\ell\,\bigl(2a_\ell+1\pm1\bigr)}{2(1+2a_\ell)}, \qquad\text{hence}\qquad J_{\lambda,\lambda+e_\ell}\;\longrightarrow\;R_\ell\,q(a_\ell), \tag{82, 83}$$ uniformly over weights with $\max_\ell|\lambda_\ell/n - a_\ell| + \max_m|\mu_m/n-b_m| = o(1)$.
Substitute the shifts (73) into (74) and track leading orders. Each shifted coordinate scales as $\widehat\lambda_\ell = n(a_\ell+\tfrac12)+o(n)$, $\widehat\mu_m = n(b_m+\tfrac12)+o(n)$. The identity $(u+\tfrac12)^2-(v+\tfrac12)^2 = A(u)-A(v)$ turns every quadratic difference into a quadratic-coordinate difference: $$\bigl(\widehat\lambda_\ell\pm\tfrac12\bigr)^2-\widehat\mu_m^2 = n^2\bigl(x_\ell-y_m\bigr)+o(n^2), \qquad \widehat\lambda_\ell^2-\widehat\lambda_q^2 = n^2\bigl(x_\ell-x_q\bigr)+o(n^2),$$ while the linear factors give $\widehat\lambda_\ell\pm\rho_{n,r} = n\bigl(a_\ell+\tfrac12\pm\tfrac12\bigr)+o(n)$ and $2\widehat\lambda_\ell = n(1+2a_\ell)+o(n)$. The $n$-powers cancel ($n^{2r}$ up and down, one linear factor each way), leaving exactly $R_\ell\,(2a_\ell+1\pm1)/(2(1+2a_\ell))$. The symmetric weight (77) is the geometric mean of the $+$ limit at $\lambda$ and the $-$ limit at $\lambda+e_\ell$ (same limiting slopes), i.e. $R_\ell\sqrt{(1+a_\ell)a_\ell}/(1+2a_\ell) = R_\ell q(a_\ell)$. Uniformity: the limiting denominators $x_\ell - x_q$, $x_\ell - y_m$ are bounded away from $0$ by strict interlacing, so the rational convergence is uniform on shrinking neighborhoods. ∎

Now build the vertex set. Pick integral rows $\mu_m = b_mn+o(n)$ and ambient caps $N_{\ell,n} = a_\ell n+o(n)$ ($N_{r+1,n}=0$ if $a_{r+1}=0$), and let $\Omega_n$ = all ambient weights $\lambda$ interlacing $\mu$ with $\mu_\ell\le\lambda_\ell\le N_{\ell,n}$ (and $0\le\lambda_{r+1}\le N_{r+1,n}$). Its upper corner is $\lambda^{*}_n = (N_{1,n},\dots,N_{r+1,n})$.

Lemma 7.2 (spectral lower bound). $\;\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{\max}\bigl(J_{\Omega_n}\bigr)\;\ge\;2\,\Gamma_r(a,b)$.
Choose $m_n\to\infty$, $m_n = o(n)$. Strict interlacing lets every positive coordinate of $\lambda^{*}_n$ decrease independently by $0,1,\dots,m_n-1$ while staying in $\Omega_n$ (a zero last coordinate simply contributes no direction): a rectangular sub-box $\mathcal B$ with $m_n$ vertices per active direction, hugging the upper corner. All $\mathcal B$-vertices share the limit slopes $a$, so by Lemma 7.1 every edge of $\mathcal B$ in direction $\ell$ has weight $w_\ell + o(1)$ with $w_\ell = R_\ell q(a_\ell)$, uniformly. Take the product test vector $$f(\lambda^{*}_n - c) \;=\; \prod_{\ell\ \mathrm{active}}\ \sin\frac{\pi (c_\ell+1)}{m_n+1},\qquad c\in\{0,\dots,m_n-1\}^{\mathrm{active}},$$ zero outside $\mathcal B$. On a constant-weight box, this is precisely the ground state of the associated lattice operator: each direction contributes $2w_\ell\cos\frac{\pi}{m_n+1}$ to the Rayleigh quotient (the one-dimensional sine identity of Lemma 2.2, once per tensor factor), and the contributions add because the operator is a sum over directions. With the $o(1)$ weight perturbations: $$\frac{\langle f, J_{\Omega_n}f\rangle}{\langle f,f\rangle} \;=\; 2\sum_{\ell}R_\ell\,q(a_\ell)\cos\frac{\pi}{m_n+1}+o(1)\;\longrightarrow\;2\,\Gamma_r(a,b),$$ where a vanished direction ($a_{r+1}=0$) is harmless since its omitted term $R_{r+1}q(0) = 0$. Rayleigh–Ritz (26) finishes. ∎

13.2 Dimension asymptotics

Lemma A.1 (uniform dimension exponents). Fix $r$ and $A_{\max}\lt\infty$, and assume $n\ge2r+4$. Uniformly over dominant integral weights with all rows $\le A_{\max}n$, $$\frac1n\log_2 D_n(\lambda) = \sum_{i=1}^{r+1}H_{\mathrm{sph}}\Bigl(\frac{\lambda_i}{n}\Bigr) + O_{r,A_{\max}}\Bigl(\frac{\log (n+2)}{n}\Bigr),$$ and the same for $\dim E_\mu$ in dimension $n-1$ with $r$ rows.
In Weyl's formula (111), the factor carrying exponential size is the factorial ratio, which is, up to the polynomially bounded factors, the binomial $\binom{\lambda_i+n-i-r-2}{\lambda_i}$: its $\frac1n\log_2$ is $H_{\mathrm{sph}}(\lambda_i/n)+O(\log(n+2)/n)$ uniformly for $\lambda_i\le A_{\max}n$ by the Stirling computation of §10.1 (the shift of the top argument by the constant $i+r+2$ moves the exponent by $O(\log n/n)$). Every remaining factor of (111) — the $(2\lambda_i+n-2i)/(n-2i)$ terms, the $\binom{r+1}2$ pair factors, and the $(\lambda_i+r+1-i)!/(r+1-i)!$ corrections of bounded index — is a product of $O_r(1)$ terms each of size polynomial in $n$, contributing $O_r(\log(n+2)/n)$ in rate; this covers chamber walls (equal or zero rows) as well, where individual factors may vanish in the formula's numerator—the formula remains exact and the bounds still apply to the surviving factors. ∎

Two corollaries, both uniform over $\Omega_n$: the corner dominates, $\frac1n\log_2\sum_{\lambda\in\Omega_n}D_\lambda = \sum_\ell H_{\mathrm{sph}}(a_\ell)+o(1)$ (86) — $H_{\mathrm{sph}}$ is increasing, so the summand exponent is maximized at $\lambda^{*}_n$, and $|\Omega_n| = O(n^{r+1})$ is a subexponential multiplicity — and the fiber exponent is $\frac1n\log_2\dim E_\mu = \sum_mH_{\mathrm{sph}}(b_m)+o(1)$ (85). Their difference is exactly $\Phi_r(a,b)$.

13.3 Proof of Theorem 1.2

Theorem 1.2 (restated precisely). Fix $r\ge0$, $0\lt s\lt1$, and vectors $a,b$ with $a_\ell\gt b_\ell\gt a_{\ell+1}$ for all $\ell$ (and $a_{r+1}\ge0$). Then $$2\Gamma_r(a,b)\gt s \;\Longrightarrow\; A(n,s)\le 2^{(\Phi_r(a,b)+o(1))n} \qquad(n\to\infty,\ r,s,a,b\ \text{fixed}).$$ Consequently, with the non-strict feasibility closure, $$R_{\mathrm{sph}}(s)\;\le\;\kappa_r(s) := \inf_{\substack{a_1\gt b_1\gt\cdots\gt b_r\gt a_{r+1}\ge0\\ 2\Gamma_r(a,b)\ge s}}\Phi_r(a,b) \qquad\text{for every } r, \qquad R_{\mathrm{sph}}(s)\le\kappa_\infty(s) := \inf_r\kappa_r(s).$$
Strict case. If $2\Gamma_r(a,b)\gt s$: Lemma 7.2 keeps $\Lambda_{\Omega_n}-s$ uniformly positive for large $n$, so the prefactor of (78) is $O(1)$; (85)–(86) make the dimension ratio $2^{(\Phi_r+o(1))n}$; Theorem 6.2 delivers the display.

Boundary closure. Tuples with $2\Gamma_r = s\gt0$ are limits of strictly feasible ones with the same limiting exponent: apply the quadratic scaling $S_c(u) = A^{-1}(cA(u))$, $c\gt1$, to every coordinate. It multiplies all $x_\ell, y_m$ by $c$, hence preserves interlacing and every residue $R_\ell$ (each is a ratio of differences of quadratic coordinates, homogeneous of degree $0$); and it strictly increases each $q(a_\ell)$: in quadratic coordinates $2q(A^{-1}(x)) = \sqrt{x/(x+1/4)}$ (square both sides, using $(1+2u)^2 = 1+4x$), which is strictly increasing in $x$ — an identity reused centrally in §15. So $2\Gamma_r$ strictly exceeds $s$ after scaling. As $c\downarrow1$ the exponents $\Phi_r$ converge ($H_{\mathrm{sph}}\circ A^{-1}$ is continuous). Taking infima over the closed feasibility region and then over $r$ gives the two consequences. ∎

Level $r=0$ recovers the direct KL bound ($\Gamma_0 = q(a_1)$, $\Phi_0 = H_{\mathrm{sph}}(a_1)$, boundary $2q(a_0(s))=s$); level 1 with $a_2 = 0$ recovers the one-row bound (11): setting $x_2 = 0$ in (81) gives $R_1 = (x_1-y_1)/x_1$, $R_2q(0) = 0$, and the identity $\Gamma_1((a,0),b) = \Gamma_{\mathrm{row}}(a,b)$ follows by algebra (checked numerically in §18, check 13). The cap-optimized $\overline\kappa_r$ of (87) then bounds $R_{\mathrm{sph}}$ as well. What is not yet proved is that the rungs are strictly ordered — that is §14.
