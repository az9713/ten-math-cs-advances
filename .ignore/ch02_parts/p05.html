5. The whole-cube bound (Theorem 2.1)

We now assemble §4's parts into the first new certificate. The plan, in machine terms: pick a window of Fourier levels $k \le i \le L$, each carrying its copy $\varphi_{i,x}E_k$ of the harmonic fiber; symmetrize the directed transition weights (4.b) into a tridiagonal matrix; use its top eigenvector to weld the copies into one moving projection $P_x$; and let Proposition 4.1 do the rest.

5.1 The symmetric transition matrix

Fix $0\le k \lt L \le n-k$ and retain the levels $V^{\square}_k,\dots,V^{\square}_L$. The whole-cube transition matrix $J_{\mathrm{H}} = J_{\mathrm{H}}(n,k,L)$ is the $(L-k+1)\times(L-k+1)$ symmetric tridiagonal matrix, indexed by Fourier degrees, with zero diagonal and off-diagonal entries

$$\bigl(J_{\mathrm{H}}\bigr)_{i,i+1} = \bigl(J_{\mathrm{H}}\bigr)_{i+1,i} = c^{(k)}_{i,\mathrm{H}} := \frac{(i-k+1)(n-i-k)}{n\sqrt{(i+1)(n-i)}}. \tag{20}$$

This entry is precisely the geometric mean $\sqrt{p_{i,i+1}\,p_{i+1,i}}$ of the two directed weights (4.b) — the symmetrization licensed by the dimension balance (19): a directed matrix $T$ satisfying $D_ip_{i,j} = D_jp_{j,i}$ is conjugate, via the diagonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{D_i})$, to the symmetric matrix with entries $\sqrt{p_{i,j}p_{j,i}}$, so both have the same spectrum. For $k=0$, $J_{\mathrm{H}}$ is the classical Krawtchouk-recurrence matrix that underlies the first MRRW bound; for $k \gt 0$ it has the same shape but every vertex now carries the $d^{\square}_k$-dimensional fiber.

Since all off-diagonal entries are strictly positive ($k\le i\lt L\le n-k$ makes $Q_i \gt 0$), the Perron–Frobenius theorem applies: the largest eigenvalue $\lambda = \lambda_{\max}(J_{\mathrm{H}}) \gt 0$ is simple and has a strictly positive unit eigenvector $v = (v_i)_{i=k}^{L}$. (For a symmetric matrix with nonnegative entries and a connected support graph, the top eigenvector can be chosen nonnegative by the Rayleigh principle — replacing entries of a candidate by absolute values never lowers the quotient — and positivity then propagates along edges.)

5.2 The finite bound

Theorem 2.1. Let $n,d,k,L$ be integers with $1\le d\le n$ and $0\le k\lt L\le n-k$. Set $s = 1-2d/n$ and $\lambda = \lambda_{\max}\bigl(J_{\mathrm{H}}(n,k,L)\bigr)$. If $\lambda \gt s$, then $$A_2(n,d) \;\le\; \frac{1-s}{\,d^{\square}_k\,(\lambda-s)\,}\;\sum_{i=k}^{L}\binom{n}{i}. \tag{21}$$
We construct the inputs of Proposition 4.1 on the ambient space $$\mathcal V = \bigoplus_{i\in I}V^{\square}_i, \qquad I = \{k,\dots,L\},\qquad D_{\mathrm{amb}} = \dim\mathcal V = \sum_{i\in I}\binom ni,$$ with $W = \mathbb{R}^n$ and $\ell_x = x/\sqrt n$ as in §4.1.

The moving projection. Let $v$ be the positive unit Perron eigenvector of $J_{\mathrm{H}}$ and put $$w_i = \sqrt{\binom ni}\;v_i \;\gt\;0, \qquad Z = \sum_{i\in I}w_i.$$ Unwinding the diagonal conjugation, the eigenvalue equation $J_{\mathrm H}v=\lambda v$ becomes, in the $w$-coordinates, a statement about the directed weights: $$\sum_{\substack{i\in I\\ |i-j|=1}} p_{i,j}\,w_i \;=\; \lambda\,w_j \qquad (j\in I). \tag{22}$$ Indeed, by (19), $p_{i,j}\sqrt{D^{\square}_i} = \sqrt{p_{i,j}\,p_{j,i}}\,\sqrt{D^{\square}_j}$ (square both sides to check), so the left side of (22) is $\sqrt{D^{\square}_j}\sum_i (J_{\mathrm H})_{j,i}v_i = \sqrt{D^{\square}_j}\,\lambda v_j = \lambda w_j$. For each word $x$, weld the staircase copies of the fiber into one isometric embedding $$\Psi_x : E_k \longrightarrow \mathcal V,\qquad \Psi_xh = \bigoplus_{i\in I}\sqrt{\frac{w_i}{Z}}\;\varphi_{i,x}h,$$ an isometry because each $\varphi_{i,x}$ is and the weights $w_i/Z$ sum to $1$. Let $P_x = \Psi_x\Psi_x^{*}$: the orthogonal projection onto $\operatorname{im}\Psi_x$, of rank $d^{\square}_k$.

The isometry $B$. Using the coordinate maps (15)–(16), define $B:\mathcal V\to\mathbb{R}^n\otimes\mathcal V$ on $f = \bigoplus_{j\in I}f_j$ by $$Bf = \bigoplus_{i\in I}\;\sum_{\substack{j\in I\\|i-j|=1}}\sqrt{\frac{p_{i,j}\,w_i}{\lambda\,w_j}}\;C_{i,j}f_j .$$ (Read: the block of $Bf$ in $\mathbb{R}^n\otimes V^{\square}_i$ collects contributions from the neighbors $j = i\pm1$, each routed through the corresponding coordinate isometry and scaled by a Perron-normalized weight.) Two facts make $B$ an isometry. First, for fixed $i$ the images of $C_{i,i-1}$ and $C_{i,i+1}$ in $\mathbb{R}^n\otimes V^{\square}_i$ are orthogonal (deletion tensors have $r\notin S$, addition tensors $r\in S$ — end of §4.5). Second, each $C_{i,j}$ is an isometry. Hence $$\|Bf\|^2 = \sum_{i\in I}\sum_{\substack{j: |i-j|=1}}\frac{p_{i,j}w_i}{\lambda w_j}\|f_j\|^2 = \sum_{j\in I}\frac{\|f_j\|^2}{\lambda w_j}\underbrace{\sum_{\substack{i\in I\\ |i-j|=1}}p_{i,j}w_i}_{=\;\lambda w_j \text{ by }(22)} = \|f\|^2 .$$

The alignment (49). Orient the coordinate maps as $C_{i,j}: V^{\square}_j\to\mathbb{R}^n\otimes V^{\square}_i$, so that the block of $Bf$ living over level $i$ is $\sum_j\sqrt{p_{i,j}w_i/(\lambda w_j)}\,C_{i,j}f_j$; the adjoint therefore acts on a tensor $g = \bigoplus_i g_i$ by $(B^{*}g)_j = \sum_i \sqrt{p_{i,j}w_i/(\lambda w_j)}\;C_{i,j}^{*}g_i$. In this orientation the contractions (17)–(18) read uniformly: $C_{i,j}^{*}(\ell_x\otimes\varphi_{i,x}h) = \sqrt{p_{i,j}}\,\varphi_{j,x}h$ — the coefficient of the transition from $i$ to $j$ (e.g. $C_{i,i+1}^{*}$ carries the level-$i$ copy to the level-$(i{+}1)$ copy with coefficient $\sqrt{Q_i/(n(i+1))} = \sqrt{p_{i,i+1}}$). Now take $g = \ell_x\otimes\Psi_xh$, whose level-$i$ block is $\ell_x\otimes\sqrt{w_i/Z}\,\varphi_{i,x}h$: $$(B^{*}g)_j \;=\; \sum_{\substack{i\in I\\|i-j|=1}}\sqrt{\frac{p_{i,j}\,w_i}{\lambda\,w_j}}\,\sqrt{\frac{w_i}{Z}}\;\sqrt{p_{i,j}}\;\varphi_{j,x}h \;=\; \varphi_{j,x}h\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda w_j Z}}\sum_{\substack{i\in I\\|i-j|=1}} p_{i,j}\,w_i \;=\; \varphi_{j,x}h\,\sqrt{\frac{\lambda w_j}{Z}}$$ by (22) again. Assembling blocks: $B^{*}(\ell_x\otimes\Psi_xh) = \sqrt\lambda\,\Psi_xh$ — exactly hypothesis (49) with $\Lambda = \lambda$, for every $u = \Psi_xh \in\operatorname{im}P_x$.

Conclusion. Proposition 4.1 with $D = D_{\mathrm{amb}}$, $d = d^{\square}_k$, threshold $s = 1-2d/n$: every code with minimum distance $\ge d$ (equivalently $t \le s$ pairwise) has size at most $\frac{1-s}{\lambda-s}\cdot\frac{1}{d^{\square}_k}\sum_{i=k}^L\binom ni$, which is (21). ∎

Where each hypothesis of Prop. 4.1 got its teeth. The isometry of $B$ needed (a) orthogonality of addition/deletion images and (b) the Perron identity (22) — that is, the top eigenvector is exactly the right welding weight. The alignment (49) needed the exact contraction coefficients (17)–(18), i.e. the ladder algebra of §4. And the balance (19) is what made the directed/symmetric translation legitimate. Nothing else about the cube was used — which is why §9 can rerun this proof on an arbitrary weighted graph.
Toy 5.1 (the machine on real numbers: $n=10$, $d=4$). Take the window $k=1$, $L=4$. The matrix $J_{\mathrm H}(10,1,4)$ has edge weights $c^{(1)}_{i,\mathrm H} = \frac{i(9-i)}{10\sqrt{(i+1)(10-i)}}$ for $i=1,2,3$, numerically $(0.18856,\,0.28577,\,0.34017)$, and largest eigenvalue $\lambda = 0.46226$. The threshold is $s = 1-8/10 = 0.2 \lt \lambda$: feasible. The fiber dimension is $d^{\square}_1 = \binom{10}1 - \binom{10}0 = 9$ and the ambient dimension is $\sum_{i=1}^4\binom{10}i = 385$. Bound (21): $$A_2(10,4) \;\le\; \frac{1-0.2}{9\,(0.46226-0.2)}\cdot385 \;=\; 130.49\ldots \;\Longrightarrow\; A_2(10,4)\le 130 .$$ The same window with the classical fiber $E_0$ ($k=0$, $L=4$, $d=1$) has $\lambda = 0.8$, giving the much weaker $\frac{0.8}{0.6}\cdot 386 = 514.7$. The harmonic fiber's rank-$9$ division beats the classical certificate by a factor $\approx4$ at $n$ as small as ten. (For calibration, the true value is $A_2(10,4) = 40$; no two-point LP is tight here, and the asymptotic comparison — the honest one — is the next section's business.) §18, check 5, reconstructs this entire example in exact arithmetic, including the isometry and alignment identities.
