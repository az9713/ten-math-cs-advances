8. Beating the optimized MRRW bound: $\kappa_{\mathrm{bin}} \lt M_2$

Scaling the layer construction is the same drill as §6, with four normalized parameters instead of two: the layer weight $\alpha = w/n$, the two fiber degrees $\beta = p/n$ and $\gamma = q/n$, and the top Johnson degree $u = L/n$. Their admissible ranges (the paper's (4)–(5)), forced by the branching window (31) and $w\lt n/2$, are

$$\frac\delta2\lt\alpha\lt\frac12,\qquad 0\le\beta\lt\frac\alpha2,\qquad 0\le\gamma\lt\frac{1-\alpha}2, \tag{4}$$ $$\beta+\gamma \;\lt\; u \;\lt\; \min\{\alpha,\;\alpha-\beta+\gamma,\;1-\alpha+\beta-\gamma\}. \tag{5}$$

Package the limits in the affine coordinates $z = 1-2u$, $m = 1-2\alpha$, $\zeta = 1-2\beta-2\gamma$, $\xi = 1-2\alpha+2\beta-2\gamma$, and define the layer's limiting spectral function (the paper's (6)):

$$\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u) \;=\; \underbrace{\frac{(\zeta\xi-mz^2)^2}{z^2(1-m^2)(1-z^2)}}_{=:B\ \text{(diagonal limit)}} \;+\;2\cdot\underbrace{\frac{(z^2-\xi^2)(\zeta^2-z^2)}{2z^2(1-m^2)\sqrt{1-z^2}}}_{=:C\ \text{(edge limit)}}. \tag{6}$$
Lemma 3.5 (spectral limit of the layer matrix). Suppose $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,u)$ satisfies (4)–(5), and choose integer sequences $w/n\to\alpha$, $p/n\to\beta$, $q/n\to\gamma$, $L/n\to u$. Then $$\liminf_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{\max}\bigl(\widehat J(n,w,p,q,L)\bigr) \;\ge\; \Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u). \tag{46}$$
Substitute the parameter scalings into (33)–(35), uniformly for degrees $j$ with $j/n\to u$. The shifted parameters scale as $\jmath_1/n \to \frac\alpha2-\beta$, $\jmath_2/n\to\frac{1-\alpha}2-\gamma$, $\jmath/n\to\frac z2$, $m_0/n\to\frac m2$, $\Sigma/n\to\frac\zeta2$, $\Delta_{\mathrm J}/n\to\frac\xi2$. Keeping leading orders (quadratic differences like $\jmath_2(\jmath_2{+}1)-\jmath_1(\jmath_1{+}1) = (\jmath_2-\jmath_1)(\jmath_2+\jmath_1+1)$ scale as $n^2\frac{\xi\zeta}4$): $$b^{p,q}_j \to \frac{m(\zeta\xi-mz^2)}{z^2(1-m^2)},\quad b^{0}_j \to \frac{m^2(1-z^2)}{z^2(1-m^2)},\quad c^{p,q}_j \to \frac{\sqrt{(z^2-m^2)(z^2-\xi^2)(\zeta^2-z^2)}}{2z^2(1-m^2)},\quad c^{0}_j \to \frac{(z^2-m^2)\sqrt{1-z^2}}{2z^2(1-m^2)},$$ using $wN/n^2\to\frac{1-m^2}4$ throughout. The strict inequalities (4)–(5) keep every denominator away from zero and put the top degree strictly inside the branching window ($j_-\lt L\lt j_+$ for large $n$). Squaring-over-classical, (41)–(42), the retained matrix's diagonal entries near degree $L$ converge uniformly to $B = (b^{p,q})^2/b^0$ and its edge weights to $C = (c^{p,q})^2/c^0$, which are exactly the two displayed pieces of (6). Now ride the terminal window with the discrete sine vector of Lemma 2.2, supported on the last $m_n\to\infty$, $m_n = o(n)$ degrees: its Rayleigh quotient is $B + 2C\cos\frac{\pi}{m_n+1}+o(1)$ (the diagonal contributes its constant, the edges their doubled cosine), and since $B+2C = \Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u)$, the Rayleigh–Ritz principle (26) gives (46). ∎
Why only a lower bound this time? On the cube, monotone entries made the row-sum upper bound match. Here the diagonal entries need not be monotone, and we do not need an upper bound: Lemma 3.5 feeds feasibility (“the gatekeeper clears the threshold”), and for that, $\liminf \ge \Lambda$ suffices. §18, check 9, confirms the convergence numerically at $n=4000$.
Theorem 3.6 (constant-weight rate bound). Fix $0\lt\delta\lt\frac12$. If $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,u)$ satisfies (4)–(5) and the feasibility condition $$\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u) \;\gt\; 1-\frac{\delta}{2\alpha(1-\alpha)}, \tag{7}$$ then $$R_2(\delta) \;\le\; 1 - H_2(\alpha) + H_2(u) - \alpha H_2\!\Bigl(\frac\beta\alpha\Bigr) - (1-\alpha)H_2\!\Bigl(\frac{\gamma}{1-\alpha}\Bigr). \tag{47}$$
Choose integer sequences as in Lemma 3.5 and let $d_n = 2\lfloor\lceil\delta n\rceil/2\rfloor$ (largest even integer $\le\lceil\delta n\rceil$; layer distances are even, and $A_2(n,\lceil\delta n\rceil)\le A_2(n,d_n)$ since requiring less separation only enlarges codes; $d_n/n\to\delta$). Since $\alpha\gt\delta/2$, the hypotheses $2\le d_n\le 2w$ of Theorem 3.4 hold eventually. The finite threshold $s_n = 1-\frac{nd_n}{2w(n-w)}$ converges to the right side of (7), so Lemma 3.5 makes $\lambda-s_n$ eventually bounded below by half the (7)-gap: the prefactor in (44) is $O(1)$. Rates of the two dimension factors: the ambient sum telescopes, $$\sum_{j=p+q}^{L}\Bigl[\binom nj-\binom n{j-1}\Bigr] = \binom nL - \binom n{p+q-1} = 2^{nH_2(u)+o(n)},$$ by Lemma B (the subtracted term has strictly smaller exponent since $p+q\lt L\le w\lt n/2$); the fiber has rate $\frac1n\log_2\bigl[d_p(w)d_q(N)\bigr] = \alpha H_2(\beta/\alpha)+(1-\alpha)H_2(\gamma/(1-\alpha))+o(1)$ (Lemma B applied inside each part, with the scaling $p/w\to\beta/\alpha$ etc.). Taking $\frac1n\log_2$ of (44) and adding the Bassalygo–Elias cost $1-H_2(\alpha)$ from Lemma C gives (47). ∎

Let $\mathcal D_\delta$ be the set of parameters satisfying (4), (5), (7). Taking the infimum defines the constant-weight exponent (the paper's (8)):

$$\kappa_{\mathrm{CW}}(\delta) \;=\; \inf_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,u)\in\mathcal D_\delta} \Bigl\{1 - H_2(\alpha) + H_2(u) - \alpha H_2\!\Bigl(\frac\beta\alpha\Bigr) - (1-\alpha)H_2\!\Bigl(\frac\gamma{1-\alpha}\Bigr)\Bigr\}, \qquad R_2(\delta)\le\kappa_{\mathrm{CW}}(\delta). \tag{8}$$

The four terms are, in order: the Bassalygo–Elias toll, the ambient Johnson entropy, and the two fiber rebates. Every quantity is now explicit, and the remaining work is comparison with the classical records.

8.1 The classical objective, recovered inside the new one

Set $\beta=\gamma=0$ (trivial fiber). Writing $A = \alpha(1-\alpha)$, $U = u(1-u)$, formula (6) collapses (substitute $\zeta = 1$, $\xi = m$, use $1-m^2 = 4A$, $1-z^2 = 4U$) to the paper's (48):

$$1-\Lambda_{\alpha,0,0}(u) \;=\; \frac{A-U}{A\,(1+2\sqrt U)}. \tag{48}$$

On the spectral boundary — where (7) holds with equality — the substitution $\tau = 2\sqrt U$ turns the constraint into $4A = \tau^2+2\delta\tau+2\delta$ and the objective into the classical one: $u = \frac{1-\sqrt{1-\tau^2}}2$ gives $H_2(u) = g(\tau^2)$, and $\alpha = \frac{1-\sqrt{1-\tau^2-2\delta\tau-2\delta}}2$ gives $H_2(\alpha) = g(\tau^2+2\delta\tau+2\delta)$, so $1-H_2(\alpha)+H_2(u) = F_\delta(\tau)$ exactly. As $\tau$ sweeps $(0,1-2\delta)$, the pair $(u,\alpha)$ sweeps the fixed-line boundary, with the endpoints $\tau=0$ and $\tau=1-2\delta$ reached in the limits $u\downarrow0$ and $\alpha\uparrow\frac12$. Approximating boundary points from the strictly feasible side, $\kappa_{\mathrm{CW}}(\delta) \le M_2(\delta)$. The classical optimized bound is, once again, a boundary sub-family of the enlarged variational problem — the layer version of what §6.2 found on the cube.

Proposition 3.7 (interior minimizers strictly improve). Fix $0\lt\delta\lt\frac12$, and suppose $0\lt u\lt\alpha\lt\frac12$ sits on the fixed-line spectral boundary, $\Lambda_{\alpha,0,0}(u) = 1-\frac{\delta}{2\alpha(1-\alpha)}$. Then there exist $\beta=\gamma=\varepsilon\gt0$ and $u'$ with $(\alpha,\varepsilon,\varepsilon,u')\in\mathcal D_\delta$ whose objective in (47) is strictly smaller than $1-H_2(\alpha)+H_2(u)$.
From (48), differentiate in $u$ (with $U = u(1-u)$, $U' = 1-2u \gt 0$): $$\partial_u\Lambda_{\alpha,0,0}(u) = (1-2u)\left[\frac{1}{A(1+2\sqrt U)} + \frac{A-U}{A\sqrt U\,(1+2\sqrt U)^2}\right] \gt 0,$$ both bracketed terms positive since $U\lt A$ on the relevant range (that is (48) with $\Lambda\lt1$). So the spectral value responds at a definite positive rate to increases of $u$. Now perturb: set $\beta=\gamma=\varepsilon$ and $u_\varepsilon = u + c\varepsilon$. The function $\Lambda$ is smooth in all four arguments at this interior point, so the spectral value changes by $c\,\partial_u\Lambda\cdot\varepsilon + O(\varepsilon)$, and choosing $c$ large enough fixed makes $(\alpha,\varepsilon,\varepsilon,u_\varepsilon)$ satisfy (7) strictly, along with (4)–(5), for all small $\varepsilon$. The objective moves by $$\underbrace{H_2(u_\varepsilon)-H_2(u)}_{O(\varepsilon)} \;-\; \underbrace{\alpha H_2\!\Bigl(\frac\varepsilon\alpha\Bigr) - (1-\alpha)H_2\!\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\alpha}\Bigr)}_{=\,2\varepsilon\log_2\frac1\varepsilon + O(\varepsilon)} \;\lt\; 0$$ for small $\varepsilon$: the double fiber rebate $2\varepsilon\log_2\frac1\varepsilon$ crushes every linear cost. ∎
Theorem 3.8 (= Theorem 1.1). For every fixed $0\lt\delta\lt\frac12$: $\;\kappa_{\mathrm{bin}}(\delta) = \min\{\kappa_{\mathrm H}(\delta),\kappa_{\mathrm{CW}}(\delta)\}\lt M_2(\delta)$.
Two cases on where the minimum of $F_\delta$ sits.

Case $M_2(\delta)\lt M_1(\delta)$. The endpoint $\tau = 1-2\delta$ has value $M_1\gt M_2$, so it is not a minimizer. Neither is $\tau=0$: since $g(\tau^2) = O(\tau^2\log\frac1\tau) = o(\tau)$, the one-sided derivative is $F'_\delta(0^+) = -2\delta g'(2\delta)\lt0$ ($g$ is strictly increasing). So every minimizer is interior, corresponds under the $\tau$-to-$(u,\alpha)$ dictionary to an interior fixed-line boundary point, and Proposition 3.7 strictly undercuts it: $\kappa_{\mathrm{CW}}(\delta)\lt M_2(\delta)$.

Case $M_2(\delta) = M_1(\delta)$. Theorem 2.3 already gives $\kappa_{\mathrm H}(\delta)\lt M_1(\delta) = M_2(\delta)$.

In both cases $\kappa_{\mathrm{bin}}\lt M_2$. Combined with $R_2\le\kappa_{\mathrm H}$ (Theorem 2.3) and $R_2\le\kappa_{\mathrm{CW}}$ (Theorem 3.6), this is Theorem 1.1. ∎
