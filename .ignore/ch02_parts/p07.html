7. Constant-weight layers and the associated Hahn matrix

The whole-cube certificate will turn out to beat $M_1$ but not always $M_2$: when the minimum of $F_\delta$ is interior, the classical constant-weight machinery is genuinely stronger than anything living on the full cube. So the chapter refines that machinery too. This section rebuilds the constant-weight world — the Johnson scheme — and runs the moving-subspace program inside it. The template is §§4–5; what changes is the cast: Johnson degrees replace Fourier degrees, a pair of harmonic spaces (one on the support, one on the complement) replaces $E_k$, and the transition matrix acquires diagonal entries.

7.1 Reduction to a layer

Lemma C (Bassalygo–Elias inequality (27)). For any $0\le w\le n$, writing $A_{\mathrm J}(n,w,d)$ for the largest code of minimum distance $\ge d$ inside the weight-$w$ layer $X_w = \{x \in\{0,1\}^n : |x| = w\}$, $$A_2(n,d) \;\le\; \frac{2^n}{\binom nw}\,A_{\mathrm J}(n,w,d).$$
Let $C$ attain $A_2(n,d)$. For $z\in\{0,1\}^n$ let $C_z = \{x\in C : x\oplus z \in X_w\}$, the part of $C$ that lands in the layer after translation by $z$. Each fixed $x$ belongs to $C_z$ for exactly $\binom nw$ translates $z$ (choose on which $w$ coordinates $z$ differs from $x$). Summing over all $2^n$ translates: $\sum_z |C_z| = |C|\binom nw$, so some $z^{*}$ has $|C_{z^{*}}| \ge |C|\binom nw/2^n$. Translation preserves Hamming distances, so $C_{z^{*}}\oplus z^{*}$ is a constant-weight code with minimum distance $\ge d$ and size $\ge |C|\binom nw/2^n$. ∎

When $w/n\to\alpha$, the prefactor costs $1-H_2(\alpha)$ in the exponent (Lemma B). The strategy: bound layer codes so well that even after paying $1-H_2(\alpha)$, the total beats $M_2$. Complementation (replacing $w$ by $n-w$) preserves distances, so assume $1\le w\lt n/2$ throughout, with the middle layer reached in the limit $\alpha\uparrow\frac12$.

7.2 The layer as a homogeneous space

Identify a weight-$w$ word with its support, so $X_w = \binom{[n]}{w}$, acted on transitively by the permutation group $S_n$. The stabilizer of a base word $x$ is $S_x\times S_{x^c}$: permutations of the $w$ support coordinates times permutations of the $N := n-w$ complement coordinates. Two distance encodings coexist and we need both: the intersection deficit $r(x,y) = w - |x\cap y| = \tfrac12 d_H(x,y)$ (distances in a layer are even), and the inner-product form demanded by the projection machine,

$$t(x,y) \;=\; 1 - \frac{n\,r(x,y)}{wN}, \qquad \ell_x = \sqrt{\frac{n}{wN}}\Bigl(\mathbf 1_x - \frac wn\,\mathbf 1\Bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^n, \tag{28}$$

where $\mathbf 1_x$ is the 0/1 indicator of the support. A direct expansion shows $\|\ell_x\| = 1$ and $\langle\ell_x,\ell_y\rangle = t(x,y)$: $\langle \mathbf 1_x-\frac wn\mathbf1, \mathbf 1_y - \frac wn\mathbf 1\rangle = |x\cap y| - \frac{w^2}{n} = \frac{wN}{n} - r(x,y)$. Note the centering: $\ell_x$ lives in the hyperplane $W = \mathbf 1^\perp \subset\mathbb{R}^n$, which will be the coordinate space of the layer machine.

Functions on $X_w$ (uniform inner product) decompose under $S_n$. Order functions by junta degree: let $\mathcal F_j$ be the span of the indicator monomials $y\mapsto\prod_{r\in T}\mathbf 1\{r\in y\}$ over sets $|T|\le j$, for $0\le j\le w$. Define the Johnson harmonic spaces as the increments

$$V^{\mathrm J}_j := \mathcal F_j \ominus \mathcal F_{j-1} \quad (\mathcal F_{-1}=0), \qquad\text{so}\qquad \mathbb{R}^{X_w} = \bigoplus_{j=0}^{w} V^{\mathrm J}_j. \tag{29}$$
Import B (Johnson scheme representation facts). For $1\le w\le n/2$ and $0\le j\le w$:
  1. $\dim V^{\mathrm J}_j = \binom nj - \binom n{j-1}$ (equivalently: the inclusion matrix of $j$-subsets vs. $w$-subsets has full rank $\binom nj$ — Gottlieb's theorem);
  2. each $V^{\mathrm J}_j$ is an irreducible representation of $S_n$ — the Specht module of the two-row partition $(n-j,j)$ — and distinct $j$ give pairwise inequivalent representations;
  3. (two-row branching / Littlewood–Richardson restriction) restricted to $S_w\times S_N$, the module $V^{\mathrm J}_j$ decomposes multiplicity-freely, and it contains the outer tensor product of the Specht modules $(w-p,p)\boxtimes(N-q,q)$ exactly when $p+q \le j \le \min\{w,\; w-p+q,\; N+p-q\}$;
  4. (associated Hahn recurrence, normalization of [BCV23, (90)–(92)] and [Koo81, §4]) the scalar coefficients in Proposition 3.2 below are the explicit expressions (33)–(35).
Citations: Delsarte 1973; Srinivasan 2011; Goodman–Wallach §9.3.5; Bernard–Crampé–Vinet 2023; Koornwinder 1981. Item 4 is verified independently by exact arithmetic in §18 (checks 6–8): the $p=q=0$ specialization is doubly stochastic on the full path, the claimed symmetrization identities hold exactly, and the asymptotic limits reproduce the closed form (6).

Two payoffs of the junta filtration are immediate and fully elementary. First, the closeness function $y\mapsto t(x,y)$ has junta degree $1$ (it is an affine function of the indicators $\mathbf 1\{r \in y\}$). Second, this gives the product rule for free:

Product rule (32). Multiplication $\mathsf M_x$ by $y\mapsto t(x,y)$ maps $V^{\mathrm J}_j$ into $V^{\mathrm J}_{j-1}\oplus V^{\mathrm J}_j\oplus V^{\mathrm J}_{j+1}$.
Multiplying a junta-degree-$\le j$ function by a degree-$\le1$ function lands in degree $\le j+1$: $\mathsf M_x\mathcal F_j \subseteq \mathcal F_{j+1}$. So $\mathsf M_xV^{\mathrm J}_j \perp$ nothing above level $j+1$ is needed; for the lower cutoff use self-adjointness of $\mathsf M_x$ (it is multiplication by a real function): for $g\in V^{\mathrm J}_{j'}$ with $j'\le j-2$, $\langle \mathsf M_xf, g\rangle = \langle f, \mathsf M_xg\rangle = 0$ since $\mathsf M_xg \in \mathcal F_{j-1}\perp V^{\mathrm J}_j$. ∎

7.3 The moving fiber: a harmonic pair

Fix a base support $x$. Boolean harmonics generalize verbatim to an abstract finite set $A$: on functions of $p$-subsets of $A$, let $\partial$ be the deletion-sum (the map $D$ of §4 with ground set $A$), and put $E_p(A) = \ker\partial$, of dimension $d_p(|A|) = \binom{|A|}p - \binom{|A|}{p-1}$ (the proof of §4.3 transfers unchanged). The attached fiber at $x$ is the tensor pair

$$E^{p,q}_x \;=\; E_p(x)\otimes E_q(x^{c}), \qquad d^{\mathrm J}_{p,q} = d_p(w)\,d_q(N),$$

for harmonic degrees $0\le p\le w/2$ on the support and $0\le q\le N/2$ on the complement. This is a representation of the stabilizer $S_x\times S_{x^c}$ — the Specht pair $(w-p,p)\boxtimes(N-q,q)$ of Import B.3 — and moving $x$ moves the fiber. Import B.3 answers exactly the question the machine asks: which ambient Johnson degrees contain a copy of the fiber?

Lemma 3.1 (branching window). Let $1\le w\lt n/2$, $0\le p\le w/2$, $0\le q\le N/2$, $x\in X_w$. The Johnson space $V^{\mathrm J}_j$ contains a copy of $E^{p,q}_x$ preserved by the stabilizer of $x$ precisely when $$j_- := p+q \;\le\; j \;\le\; j_+ := \min\{\,w,\; w-p+q,\; N+p-q\,\}, \tag{31}$$ the copy is unique, and its isometric stabilizer-equivariant embedding $\varphi_{j,x}: E^{p,q}_x\to V^{\mathrm J}_j$ is unique up to sign. [= Import B.3 restated at the base point; uniqueness up to sign because the copy is a single irreducible summand and, the Specht pair being absolutely irreducible over $\mathbb R$, its equivariant endomorphisms are scalars.]

7.4 The scalar recurrence on the fiber

By the product rule, $\mathsf M_x$ ties neighboring Johnson degrees; by multiplicity one, its action between the fiber copies is forced to be scalar. The scalars are the associated Hahn coefficients. Package the parameters (paper's (33)):

$$\jmath_1 = \tfrac w2 - p,\quad \jmath_2 = \tfrac N2 - q,\quad \jmath = \tfrac n2 - j,\quad m_0 = \tfrac n2 - w,\quad \Sigma = \jmath_1+\jmath_2,\quad \Delta_{\mathrm J} = \jmath_2-\jmath_1, \tag{33}$$

and define (paper's (34)–(35)):

$$\mu^{p,q}_j = \frac{m_0}{2}\,\frac{\jmath_2(\jmath_2+1)-\jmath_1(\jmath_1+1)}{\jmath(\jmath+1)}, \qquad \nu^{p,q}_j = \frac{\sqrt{(\jmath^2-m_0^2)(\jmath^2-\Delta_{\mathrm J}^2)\bigl((\Sigma+1)^2-\jmath^2\bigr)}}{2\jmath\sqrt{(2\jmath-1)(2\jmath+1)}}, \tag{34}$$ $$b^{p,q}_j = \frac{n\,\mu^{p,q}_j - m_0^2}{wN}, \qquad c^{p,q}_j = \frac{n\,\nu^{p,q}_j}{wN}. \tag{35}$$
Proposition 3.2 (three-term recurrence on the fiber). In the setting of Lemma 3.1 with $j_-\le j_+$, the embeddings can be sign-normalized so that for every $x\in X_w$, $Y\in E^{p,q}_x$, and $j_-\le j\le j_+$, $$t(x,\cdot)\,\varphi_{j,x}Y \;=\; c^{p,q}_{j-1}\,\varphi_{j-1,x}Y \;+\; b^{p,q}_j\,\varphi_{j,x}Y \;+\; c^{p,q}_j\,\varphi_{j+1,x}Y, \tag{37}$$ with the convention that the $j\mp1$ term is dropped at the window ends. Moreover $c^{p,q}_j \gt 0$ strictly for $j_-\le j\lt j_+$.
Fix $x$; write $H_x = S_x\times S_{x^c}$. The composite $\Pi^{\mathrm J}_i\,\mathsf M_x\,\varphi_{j,x} : E^{p,q}_x\to V^{\mathrm J}_i$ ($\Pi^{\mathrm J}_i$ = orthogonal projection onto $V^{\mathrm J}_i$) is $H_x$-equivariant, because $\mathsf M_x$ and each $\Pi^{\mathrm J}_i$ commute with the stabilizer. By Lemma 3.1 its image is $0$ unless $j_-\le i\le j_+$, in which case it lands in the unique copy $\varphi_{i,x}E^{p,q}_x$; the product rule further forces $i\in\{j-1,j,j+1\}$. Since $E^{p,q}_x$ is absolutely irreducible, each surviving composite is a real scalar times $\varphi_{i,x}$ (Schur). Import B.4 identifies the diagonal scalar as $b^{p,q}_j$ and the magnitude of the up-scalar as $c^{p,q}_j$. Positivity in the open window: for $j_-\le j\lt j_+$ one checks $0\lt\max\{m_0,|\Delta_{\mathrm J}|\}\lt\jmath\le\Sigma$ — indeed $j\ge j_-$ gives $\jmath\le\Sigma$, and $\frac n2 - j_+ = \max\{m_0,|\Delta_{\mathrm J}|\}$ by taking the max over the three arguments of the min in (31) — so all three radicand factors of $\nu^{p,q}_j$ are positive, as is the denominator; hence $c^{p,q}_j\gt0$. Signs: choose a reference support $x_0$ and any isometric equivariant $\varphi_{j_-,x_0}$; inductively fix the sign of $\varphi_{j+1,x_0}$ so that the up-coefficient is $+c^{p,q}_j$ (possible since it is nonzero in the open window); transport to all $x = gx_0$ by $\varphi_{j,x} = g\varphi_{j,x_0}g^{-1}$, well-defined because two transporting permutations differ by an element of the stabilizer, with which every $\varphi_{j,x_0}$ commutes. Down-coefficient: self-adjointness of $\mathsf M_x$ and isometry of the embeddings give, for $Y,Z\in E^{p,q}_x$, $\langle\varphi_{j-1,x}Z,\,\mathsf M_x\varphi_{j,x}Y\rangle = \langle\mathsf M_x\varphi_{j-1,x}Z,\,\varphi_{j,x}Y\rangle = c^{p,q}_{j-1}\langle Z,Y\rangle$, so the $j-1$ component is $c^{p,q}_{j-1}\varphi_{j-1,x}Y$. At the window ends the dropped coefficient indeed vanishes: at $j=j_-$ the formal factor $(\Sigma+1)^2-\jmath^2$ of $\nu_{j_- - 1}$ is zero, and at $j=j_+$ either $\jmath^2 = m_0^2$ (if $j_+=w$) or $\jmath^2 = \Delta_{\mathrm J}^2$ (the other two cases). ∎

The classical fixed-line coefficients are the $p=q=0$ case; write $b^{0}_j,\ c^{0}_j$. There $b^0_0 = 0$ and, for $j\ge1$, $b^0_j = \frac{m_0^2\,j(n-j+1)}{wN\,\jmath(\jmath+1)} \gt 0$ and $c^0_j\gt0$ for $0\le j\lt w$: the classical path has all its transitions alive on the whole degree range.

7.5 Symmetrizing, and the layer bound

As on the cube, the projection machine wants isometric coordinate maps $V^{\mathrm J}_j \to W\otimes V^{\mathrm J}_i$ (recall $W = \mathbf 1^\perp$), not the bare recurrence. Their normalization is fixed by the classical case, and this is where the “square over classical” signature of the whole chapter first appears. Define, for window degrees $i,j$ with $|i-j|\le1$ and $(i,j)\ne(0,0)$:

$$r^{p,q}_{i,j} = \begin{cases} b^{p,q}_i, & i=j,\\ c^{p,q}_{\min\{i,j\}}, & |i-j|=1,\end{cases} \qquad r^{0}_{i,j} \text{ likewise with } (p,q)=(0,0).$$
Lemma 3.3 (normalized coordinate isometries). For $|i-j|\le1$, $(i,j)\ne(0,0)$, there is an $S_n$-equivariant isometry $C_{i,j}: V^{\mathrm J}_j\to W\otimes V^{\mathrm J}_i$ whose contraction on the fibers is $$C^{*}_{i,j}\bigl(\ell_x\otimes\varphi_{i,x}Y\bigr) = \sqrt{p_{i,j}}\;\varphi_{j,x}Y, \qquad p_{i,j} = \frac{\bigl(r^{p,q}_{i,j}\bigr)^2}{r^{0}_{i,j}}\sqrt{\frac{D^{\mathrm J}_j}{D^{\mathrm J}_i}}, \tag{38}$$ for every $x\in X_w$, $Y\in E^{p,q}_x$. Consequently $$D^{\mathrm J}_i\,p_{i,j} = D^{\mathrm J}_j\,p_{j,i}, \qquad \sqrt{p_{i,j}\,p_{j,i}} = \frac{\bigl(r^{p,q}_{i,j}\bigr)^2}{r^{0}_{i,j}}. \tag{39}$$
Let $K_i,K_j$ be the reproducing kernels of $V^{\mathrm J}_i, V^{\mathrm J}_j$ (i.e. $K_i(x,\cdot)$ is the unique vector representing evaluation at $x$). Transitivity of $S_n$ on $X_w$ makes the diagonal constant: $K_i(x,x) = D^{\mathrm J}_i$ (trace of the projection onto $V^{\mathrm J}_i$ spread evenly over $|X_w|$ points, normalized by the probability inner product). So $z_{i,x} = K_i(x,\cdot)/\sqrt{D^{\mathrm J}_i}$ is the unit stabilizer-fixed vector of $V^{\mathrm J}_i$ positive at $x$, and the classical ($p=q=0$) case of (37) reads $\Pi^{\mathrm J}_j(t(x,\cdot)z_{i,x}) = r^0_{i,j}z_{j,x}$ (40). Define the coordinate-multiplication map $$M_{i,j}: W\otimes V^{\mathrm J}_i\to V^{\mathrm J}_j,\qquad M_{i,j}(v\otimes f) = \Pi^{\mathrm J}_j\bigl(\langle v,\ell_{\,\cdot}\rangle\,f\bigr),$$ i.e. multiply $f$ by the linear function $y\mapsto\langle v,\ell_y\rangle$ and project. It is $S_n$-equivariant, so the positive operator $M_{i,j}M^{*}_{i,j}$ on the irreducible $V^{\mathrm J}_j$ is a scalar $m_{i,j}\,\mathrm{id}$. Compute the scalar by the Hilbert–Schmidt norm: expanding in orthonormal bases, using $\sum_a \langle v_a, \ell_y\rangle\langle v_a,\ell_z\rangle = \langle \ell_y,\ell_z\rangle = t(y,z)$ over an orthonormal basis $(v_a)$ of $W$, and the reproducing property, $$\|M_{i,j}\|^2_{\mathrm{HS}} = \iint t(y,z)K_i(y,z)K_j(y,z)\,d\sigma(y)\,d\sigma(z) = \int t(x,y)K_i(x,y)K_j(x,y)\,d\sigma(y) \cdot 1$$ (by transitivity the inner integral is independent of $z=x$), and by (40) this is $r^0_{i,j}\sqrt{D^{\mathrm J}_iD^{\mathrm J}_j}$. Dividing by the output dimension: $m_{i,j} = r^0_{i,j}\sqrt{D^{\mathrm J}_i/D^{\mathrm J}_j}$. Then $C_{i,j} := \varepsilon_{i,j}\,M^{*}_{i,j}/\sqrt{m_{i,j}}$ is an equivariant isometry ($\varepsilon_{i,j}=\pm1$ chosen so the contraction coefficient below is $\ge0$). Its contraction on the fiber copies: $C^{*}_{i,j}(\ell_x\otimes\varphi_{i,x}Y) = \varepsilon_{i,j}M_{i,j}(\ell_x\otimes\varphi_{i,x}Y)/\sqrt{m_{i,j}} = \varepsilon_{i,j}\,\Pi^{\mathrm J}_j(t(x,\cdot)\varphi_{i,x}Y)/\sqrt{m_{i,j}} = |r^{p,q}_{i,j}|\,\varphi_{j,x}Y/\sqrt{m_{i,j}}$ by (37). Squaring gives (38); symmetry $r^{p,q}_{i,j}=r^{p,q}_{j,i}$, $r^0_{i,j}=r^0_{j,i}$ gives (39). ∎

Now fix a top degree $j_-\lt L\le j_+$ and retain the Johnson degrees $j_-,\dots,L$. The symmetrized layer matrix $\widehat J = \widehat J(n,w,p,q,L)$ is tridiagonal-with-diagonal:

$$\widehat J_{j,j} = \widehat b^{\,p,q}_j := \begin{cases}\bigl(b^{p,q}_j\bigr)^2/b^0_j, & j\ge1,\\[2pt] 0,& j=0,\end{cases} \qquad \widehat J_{j,j+1} = \widehat J_{j+1,j} = \widehat c^{\,p,q}_j := \frac{\bigl(c^{p,q}_j\bigr)^2}{c^0_j}. \tag{41, 42}$$

These are exactly the geometric means $\sqrt{p_{i,j}p_{j,i}}$ from (39) — the “associated coefficient squared over classical” pattern, which will recur verbatim on the sphere in (68). Note the sanity check at the classical point: if $p=q=0$ then $\widehat J$ has entries $b^0_j$ and $c^0_j$ — the ordinary Hahn recurrence matrix.

Theorem 3.4 (finite layer bound). Let $n,w,p,q,L,d$ be integers with $1\le w\lt n/2$, $0\le p\le w/2$, $0\le q\le(n-w)/2$, $p+q\lt L\le\min\{w,\,w-p+q,\,n-w+p-q\}$, $d$ even, $2\le d\le 2w$. Put $N = n-w$, $s = 1-\frac{nd}{2wN}$, $\lambda = \lambda_{\max}\bigl(\widehat J(n,w,p,q,L)\bigr)$. If $\lambda\gt s$, then $$A_{\mathrm J}(n,w,d) \;\le\; \frac{1-s}{\lambda-s}\;\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j=p+q}^{L}\Bigl[\binom nj - \binom n{j-1}\Bigr]}{\Bigl[\binom wp-\binom w{p-1}\Bigr]\Bigl[\binom Nq-\binom N{q-1}\Bigr]}. \tag{44}$$
Rerun the proof of Theorem 2.1 with the layer cast; only two details change. Setting $I = \{p+q,\dots,L\}$, $\omega_j = \sqrt{D^{\mathrm J}_j}\,v_j$ for the positive unit Perron eigenvector $v$ of $\widehat J$ (its off-diagonals are positive, diagonal nonnegative — Perron applies to $\widehat J + c\,\mathrm{id}$ and shifts back), the dimension balance (39) converts $\widehat Jv = \lambda v$ into the directed identity $\sum_{|i-j|\le1} p_{i,j}\omega_i = \lambda\omega_j$ (45) — now including $i=j$ terms. Build $\Psi_xY = \bigoplus_j\sqrt{\omega_j/Z}\,\varphi_{j,x}Y$ and $B = \bigoplus$-assembly of the $C_{i,j}$ of Lemma 3.3 with weights $\sqrt{p_{i,j}\omega_i/(\lambda\omega_j)}$, setting $C_{0,0}=0$ (consistently: $p_{0,0} = 0$ since $b^0$-normalization degenerates there and the classical $j=0$ diagonal vanishes). First changed detail: orthogonality of the images of $C_{i,j}$ for fixed $i$, distinct $j$: they are invariant irreducible subspaces of $W\otimes V^{\mathrm J}_i$ carrying the pairwise inequivalent representations $V^{\mathrm J}_j$ (Import B.2), hence orthogonal — the cube's hands-on tensor-support argument is replaced by pure representation theory, and the argument now also covers the $i=j$ loops. Second changed detail: the rank of $P_x = \Psi_x\Psi_x^*$ is $d^{\mathrm J}_{p,q} = d_p(w)d_q(N)$ and the ambient dimension telescopes, $\sum_{j\in I}D^{\mathrm J}_j = \binom nL - \binom n{p+q-1}$. With these, $B$ is an isometry by (45), the alignment $B^{*}(\ell_x\otimes\Psi_xY) = \sqrt\lambda\,\Psi_xY$ holds by Lemma 3.3's contractions plus (45), and Proposition 4.1 delivers (44). ∎
Why diagonal entries appear here and not on the cube. On the whole cube, $t(x,\cdot)$ is a pure degree-one character combination, whose multiplication shifts Fourier degree by exactly $\pm1$. On a layer, the centering in (28) makes $t(x,\cdot)$ an affine degree-one junta — and the layer's harmonic decomposition is not graded by a group character, so a same-degree component survives. The machine is indifferent: Proposition 4.1 never assumed the transition graph was bipartite or loop-free.
