17. What the certificates capture, and what they miss

Honest bookkeeping, in both directions. The results are records — and they are also small, two-point, asymptotic, and upper-bound-only. Each of those qualifiers is a precise limitation with a name.

17.1 Captured

  • Every fixed distance and angle, strictly. Not an improvement at some parameters: $\kappa_{\mathrm{bin}}(\delta)\lt M_2(\delta)$ for every $\delta\in(0,\frac12)$ and $\overline\kappa_{r+1}(s)\lt\overline\kappa_r(s)$ for every $s\in(0,1)$ and $r$. The 1977 and 1978 records fall everywhere at once.
  • Within Delsarte's own feasible region. The new certificates are ordinary scalar two-point Delsarte certificates (Theorem 4.2 exhibits the psd decomposition explicitly). No new LP, no SDP hierarchy, no computer search: the classical program always contained these certificates, and nobody had built them.
  • A sharp endpoint. The $s\to1$ hierarchy limit $\lambda^* = \frac12\log_2\frac{2\pi}e$ is not merely an improvement but optimal within the LP framework: Chapter 1's lower bound shows no Cohn–Elkies auxiliary function beats it, and Cohn–Zhao transference (§16.1) closes the loop between the two frameworks.

17.2 Missed, with names

  • The gap to the truth remains enormous. At $\delta = 0.2$: Gilbert–Varshamov gives $R_2\ge0.2781$, the new bound $R_2\le0.4597$ (old: $0.4614$). The improvement covers well under $1\%$ of the unknown interval. Nobody knows which side is closer to the truth; the paper takes no position.
  • Two-point ceiling (the analogy's break-point, again). The certificates see only $\operatorname{tr}(P_xP_y)$ per pair. Known lower bounds on the optimum of Delsarte's two-point LP — Samorodnitsky 2001 for binary (the LP value at some rates provably exceeds the average of GV and MRRW), Samorodnitsky 2004 for spherical, and the recent quantitative versions (Navon–Samorodnitsky 2005; Chailloux–Debris-Alazard 2025; Samorodnitsky 2025 on the difficulty of even beating the first LP bound by spectral means) — put a floor under every method of this shape, far above GV. Reaching the truth, if the truth is near GV, will require genuinely multi-point information. The chapter's certificates dodge the published spectral-limitation theorems (that is why the improvement was possible) but remain subject to the LP-optimum floors.
  • Asymptotic only. The $o(1)$'s are real: at any concrete $(n,d)$, tables of best known bounds (SDP-based, dimension-specific) are untouched. Semidefinite hierarchies (Schrijver 2005 binary; Bachoc–Vallentin 2008 spherical — whose tangent-harmonic kernels this chapter repurposes, with the crucial change that the base point moves) remain the state of the art at fixed small $n$, and settled the kissing number in dimension 4. Conversely, no SDP result had improved the general exponents this chapter improves.
  • Upper bounds only. Nothing here constructs codes. The lower-bound side ($\mathrm{GV}$, algebraic-geometry codes over large alphabets, etc.) is unchanged.
  • Neighboring regimes, other tools. The near-half-distance regime $d = \frac n2-\Theta(\sqrt n)$ saw its own recent improvement by different methods (Pang–Mahdavifar–Pradhan 2023) — disjoint from the fixed-$\delta$ regime here. Restricted families (linear codes: Loyfer–Linial 2023, Coregliano et al. 2022–26 complete LP hierarchies) and fixed dimensions ($8$, $24$: Viazovska theory, where LP is exactly tight) are separate stories. Constant-factor (non-exponent) spherical improvements at fixed angle: Sardari–Zargar 2024.
  • Binary fibers are hand-picked. The Hamming and Johnson pairs are not strong Gelfand pairs (§9.3): more ambitious binary fibers hit multiplicity $\ge2$ and would need matrix-valued transition machinery that the chapter deliberately avoids. Whether such an extension buys more exponent is open.
Perspective. The result's significance is best stated in method terms: a construction gap inside a 50-year-old convex program, invisible to every limitation theorem aimed at the program, closed by importing one idea (moving subspaces with scalar overlaps) from representation theory. The direct payoff is a set of slightly better constants; the structural payoff is that the classical certificates are now known to be non-optimal within their own game — and the game's true optimum value, at least at the packing endpoint, is now known exactly.
