10. Spherical harmonics from zero

The spherical half of the chapter now begins, and it follows the binary half beat for beat: this section builds the ambient spaces and the classical bound (the analogue of §4's Fourier levels), §11 attaches a moving fiber and beats the classical bound (the analogue of §§5–6), and §§12–14 iterate the upgrade into an infinite hierarchy — something the cube's structure never offered. Throughout the spherical sections $n\ge3$, and the measure on $S^{n-1}$ is the rotation-invariant probability measure $\sigma$.

10.1 Harmonic levels and their dimensions

Definition (spherical harmonics). A polynomial on $\mathbb{R}^n$ is homogeneous of degree $m$ if scaling the argument by $c$ scales the value by $c^m$, and harmonic if its Laplacian $\Delta = \sum_r\partial_r^2$ vanishes. Let $V^{\mathrm S}_m = \mathcal H_m(\mathbb{R}^n)$ denote the restrictions of degree-$m$ homogeneous harmonic polynomials to $S^{n-1}$. Rotations act by composition, $(\rho\cdot f)(u) = f(\rho^{-1}u)$, preserving each $V^{\mathrm S}_m$. Examples: $V^{\mathrm S}_0$ = constants; $V^{\mathrm S}_1$ = linear functionals $u\mapsto\langle v,u\rangle$; $V^{\mathrm S}_2$ = quadratic forms with zero trace.
Dimension (59). Let $P_m$ denote all degree-$m$ homogeneous polynomials in $n$ variables, $\dim P_m = \binom{m+n-1}{m}$. Then $\Delta: P_m\to P_{m-2}$ is surjective, whence $$D^{\mathrm S}_m = \dim V^{\mathrm S}_m = \binom{m+n-1}{m}-\binom{m+n-3}{m-2} \;=\; \frac{2m+n-2}{n-2}\binom{m+n-3}{m},$$ and restriction to the sphere is injective on harmonics. Moreover distinct levels are orthogonal in $L^2(\sigma)$, and $\bigoplus_{m\ge0}V^{\mathrm S}_m$ is dense.
Surjectivity. Endow each $P_m$ with the apolar pairing $\langle f,g\rangle_\partial := f(\partial)\,g$ (substitute $\partial_r$ for $x_r$ in $f$ and apply the resulting differential operator to $g$): monomials satisfy $\langle x^a, x^b\rangle_\partial = a!\,\delta_{ab}$, so this is an inner product. For $f\in P_{m-2}$, $g\in P_m$, $$\langle \|x\|^2 f,\; g\rangle_\partial = \Bigl(\sum_r\partial_r^2\Bigr)\!\circ f(\partial)\,g = f(\partial)\,\Delta g = \langle f, \Delta g\rangle_\partial,$$ since the operators $\partial_r$ commute: multiplication by $\|x\|^2$ and $\Delta$ are mutually adjoint. Multiplication by $\|x\|^2$ is injective (polynomials form an integral domain), so its adjoint $\Delta: P_m \to P_{m-2}$ is surjective; rank–nullity gives $\dim\mathcal H_m = \binom{m+n-1}{m}-\binom{m+n-3}{m-2}$. The closed form on the right is elementary binomial algebra, and §18 (check 10) verifies the two expressions agree in exact integers for all $m\le200$, $n\le60$. Injectivity of restriction & orthogonality. Iterating the (easily checked) decomposition $P_m = \mathcal H_m\oplus\|x\|^2P_{m-2}$ shows every polynomial restricted to the sphere equals a sum of harmonics of degrees $\le m$; if a degree-$m$ harmonic vanished on the sphere it would equal $\|x\|^2q$ there, contradicting the directness of that decomposition. Orthogonality of distinct levels: harmonics of distinct degree are eigenfunctions of the spherical Laplacian with distinct eigenvalues — or, avoiding that operator: $\int_{S^{n-1}} fg\,d\sigma$ for $f\in V_m, g\in V_{m'}$ with $m\gt m'$ vanishes by Green's identity applied to the harmonic extensions on the ball, $\int_S (f\partial_rg - g\partial_rf) = 0$ with $\partial_r = $ radial derivative scaling as degree. Density: polynomials are dense (Stone–Weierstrass) and every polynomial restricts to a finite sum of harmonics. ∎

The growth exponent. With $m = un + o(n)$, $u\gt0$ fixed, Stirling on $\binom{m+n-3}{m}$ gives (the same computation as Lemma B, with $n+m$ in place of $n$): $$\frac1n\log_2 D^{\mathrm S}_{m} \;=\; (1+u)\log_2(1+u) - u\log_2 u + o(1) \;=\; H_{\mathrm{sph}}(u)+o(1),$$ the function announced in (9). Unlike $H_2$, it is unbounded — degree has no ceiling — and strictly increasing, with $H_{\mathrm{sph}}(u)\sim\log_2 u$ growth. This is the ambient entropy of the spherical machine.

10.2 Zonal vectors and the addition formula

Zonal line and addition formula (60). Fix $x\in S^{n-1}$. Inside each $V^{\mathrm S}_m$, the functions invariant under all rotations fixing $x$ form exactly one line, spanned by a polynomial $P_m(\langle x,\cdot\rangle)$ in the “latitude” $t = \langle x,u\rangle$, normalized by $P_m(1)=1$ ($P_m$ = the normalized Gegenbauer polynomial $C^{(n-2)/2}_m(t)/C^{(n-2)/2}_m(1)$). The reproducing kernel $K^{\mathrm S}_m(u,v) = \sum_a Y_{m,a}(u)Y_{m,a}(v)$ (any orthonormal basis) satisfies $$K^{\mathrm S}_m(u,v) \;=\; D^{\mathrm S}_m\,P_m(\langle u,v\rangle),$$ and consequently $(u,v)\mapsto P_m(\langle u,v\rangle)$ is a psd kernel on the sphere.
A function invariant under the stabilizer of $x$ is constant on each latitude sphere $\{u: \langle x,u\rangle = t\}$ (the stabilizer acts transitively there), hence a function of $t$ alone; and the restriction of a degree-$m$ polynomial is then a polynomial of degree $\le m$ in $t$ (average the polynomial over the stabilizer: the average is again a degree-$\le m$ polynomial restriction and equals the function of $t$). Count the invariant dimensions across levels. On one hand, the stabilizer-invariant functions in $V^{\mathrm S}_0\oplus\cdots\oplus V^{\mathrm S}_m$ all lie in the $(m{+}1)$-dimensional space of polynomials of degree $\le m$ in $t$. On the other, each level $V^{\mathrm S}_{m'}$, $m'\le m$, contains at least one invariant line — the kernel section $K^{\mathrm S}_{m'}(x,\cdot)$, which is invariant by basis-independence and nonzero since its value at $x$ is $D^{\mathrm S}_{m'}\gt0$ (see below). Since the levels are orthogonal, the invariant dimensions add up to at most $m+1$ over $m+1$ levels, so each is exactly one: the invariant line is unique. The kernel: $K^{\mathrm S}_m$ is basis-independent and rotation-invariant, $K^{\mathrm S}_m(\rho u,\rho v) = K^{\mathrm S}_m(u,v)$, so $K^{\mathrm S}_m(x,\cdot)\in V^{\mathrm S}_m$ is stabilizer-invariant: $K^{\mathrm S}_m(x,\cdot) = c\,P_m(\langle x,\cdot\rangle)$. Evaluate the constant on the diagonal: $K^{\mathrm S}_m(x,x) = \sum_a Y_{m,a}(x)^2$ integrates over the sphere to $D^{\mathrm S}_m$, and is rotation-invariant hence constant, so $K^{\mathrm S}_m(x,x) = D^{\mathrm S}_m = c\,P_m(1) = c$. Positive-definiteness: $K^{\mathrm S}_m$ is a Gram kernel of the vectors $(Y_{m,a}(u))_a$. ∎

Lemma A now gives the classical spherical LP: if $f = \sum_{m=0}^{M}f_mP_m$ has $f_0\gt0$, $f_m\ge0$, and $f(t)\le0$ on $[-1,s]$, then $A(n,s)\le f(1)/f_0$. What the classical spectral construction feeds it is the zonal path: unit vectors $z_{m,x} = K^{\mathrm S}_m(x,\cdot)/\sqrt{D^{\mathrm S}_m}$ — one pinned vector per level, the sphere's twin of §4's $v_{i,x}$.

10.3 The recurrence and the classical exponent

Multiplication by the latitude $t$ is the sphere's coordinate transition. On the zonal profiles it acts by the classical Gegenbauer three-term recurrence, which we import once, in the form covering all the fibers we will ever use (its $k=0$ case is the textbook identity):

Import D (classical spherical analysis). Let $\eta = k+(n-2)/2$. (i) Gegenbauer recurrence: for $j\ge0$, with $C^\eta_{-1} = 0$, $$2(j+\eta)\,t\,C^{\eta}_j(t) \;=\; (j+1)\,C^{\eta}_{j+1}(t) + (j+2\eta-1)\,C^{\eta}_{j-1}(t). \tag{64}$$ (ii) Branching rule $SO(n)\downarrow SO(n-1)$, harmonic case: for each $x$, restricting $V^{\mathrm S}_i$ to the rotations fixing $x$ decomposes multiplicity-freely as $$V^{\mathrm S}_i\big\downarrow_{SO(n-1)} \;\simeq\; \bigoplus_{k=0}^{i}\mathcal H_k(x^{\perp}), \tag{62}$$ where $\mathcal H_k(x^\perp)$ is the space of degree-$k$ harmonics on the unit sphere of the hyperplane $x^\perp$. In particular $V^{\mathrm S}_i$ contains exactly one copy of $\mathcal H_k(x^\perp)$ when $i\ge k$, none when $i\lt k$. Citations: (i) any orthogonal-polynomials reference (Szegő); (ii) Goodman–Wallach, Thms. 8.1.3–8.1.4. Both are verified numerically in §18: (64) at random arguments against explicitly computed Gegenbauer coefficients, and the dimension count of (62), $D^{\mathrm S}_i(n) = \sum_{k\le i}\dim\mathcal H_k(x^\perp)$, in exact integers (check 10).

The classical construction is now a two-line instance of Theorem 4.2: fiber $E$ = trivial ($d=1$, the zonal lines), graph = the path on levels $k=0\le i\le L$, edge weights computed in §11 (Lemma 5.1 below with $k=0$) with limit: for $i/n\to a$, the weight tends to $$q(a) = \frac{\sqrt{a(1+a)}}{1+2a},$$ so the path's top eigenvalue tends to $2q(a)$ (the sine-vector argument of Lemma 2.2, rerun verbatim — done in general as Lemma 5.2). Feasibility $2q(a)\gt s$ plus ambient entropy $H_{\mathrm{sph}}(a)$ give the direct Kabatianskii–Levenshtein exponent: solving the boundary $2q(a_0) = s$, $$4a_0(1+a_0) = s^2(1+2a_0)^2 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; (1+2a_0)^2(1-s^2) = 1 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; a_0 = a_0(s) = \tfrac12\bigl((1-s^2)^{-1/2}-1\bigr),$$ recovering exactly the constant of §1.2, and $A(n,s)\le 2^{(H_{\mathrm{sph}}(a_0(s))+o(1))n}$. The stronger classical record $B_{\mathrm{KL}}$ adds the spherical-cap reduction, which we take up (and import precisely) in §11.4.
