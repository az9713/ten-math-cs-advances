11. Tangent harmonics: the one-row spherical improvement

Now the sphere gets its moving fiber. The classical construction pinned one zonal vector per level; we replace it by the space of degree-$k$ harmonics on the directions orthogonal to the code point — a subspace of dimension exponential in $n$ once $k\propto n$, transported by rotations, sitting once inside every ambient level $i\ge k$. This is the exact spherical twin of §§5–6, and it already beats the 1978 record.

11.1 The fiber $\mathcal H_k(x^\perp)$

For $x\in S^{n-1}$, the tangent sphere at $x$ is $S(x^\perp) = x^\perp\cap S^{n-1}$, an $(n-2)$-sphere. Define the fiber

$$E_x = \mathcal H_k(x^{\perp}), \qquad d^{\mathrm S}_k = \dim E_x = \binom{n+k-2}{k}-\binom{n+k-4}{k-2}, \tag{61}$$

the degree-$k$ spherical harmonics of the $(n-1)$-dimensional Euclidean space $x^\perp$ (dimension by the Lemma of §10.1 applied in dimension $n-1$). Equivalently: symmetric $k$-tensors on $x^\perp$ with all self-contractions zero. For $k=0$ it is the constants (the classical case); for $k=1$ it is $x^\perp$ itself; for $k=2$, traceless quadratic forms on $x^\perp$. Keep the two integers straight: $k$ is a degree, fixed per construction; $d^{\mathrm S}_k$ is a dimension, and $\frac1n\log_2 d^{\mathrm S}_{bn}\to H_{\mathrm{sph}}(b)$ — the fiber entropy. A rotation carrying $x$ to $y$ carries $x^\perp$ to $y^\perp$ and $E_x$ to $E_y$: the fiber moves. For $k\ge1$ we assume $n\ge4$ (so that $SO(n-1)$ acting on $\mathcal H_k(x^\perp)$ is absolutely irreducible — part of Import D's package — giving the Schur property the machine needs).

The branching rule (62) of Import D says: $V^{\mathrm S}_i$ contains exactly one copy of $E_x$ when $i\ge k$, none otherwise. The copy even has an explicit form. Away from the poles, every $u\in S^{n-1}$ decomposes uniquely as

$$u = t\,x + \sqrt{1-t^2}\,\xi, \qquad t = \langle x,u\rangle\in[-1,1],\quad \xi\in S(x^{\perp}),$$

— latitude $t$ and tangential direction $\xi$ — and the copy of $E_x$ inside $V^{\mathrm S}_i$ is spanned by the separated functions (Import D(iii), the explicit intertwiner of Bachoc–Vallentin):

$$(\varphi_{i,x}Y)(u) \;=\; p_{i-k}(t)\,\bigl(1-t^2\bigr)^{k/2}\,Y(\xi), \qquad Y\in E_x, \tag{63}$$

with radial profile $p_{i-k}$ proportional to the Gegenbauer polynomial $C^{\,k+(n-2)/2}_{\,i-k}$, the constant fixed (sign: positive leading coefficient) so that $\varphi_{i,x}$ is an isometry. The tangential factor $Y$ is untouched by multiplication by $t$ — so the Gegenbauer recurrence (64), applied to the radial factor with $\eta = k+\frac{n-2}2$ and $j = i-k$, immediately gives the fiber recurrence

$$t\,\varphi_{i,x} = \alpha^{(k)}_i\,\varphi_{i+1,x} + \beta^{(k)}_i\,\varphi_{i-1,x}, \tag{65}$$

with squared coefficients (after the isometric normalization; Import D(iii), verified numerically to $10^{-12}$ by quadrature in §18, check 11)

$$\bigl(\alpha^{(k)}_i\bigr)^2 = \frac{(i-k+1)(i+k+n-2)}{(2i+n-2)(2i+n)}, \qquad \bigl(\beta^{(k)}_i\bigr)^2 = \frac{(i-k)(i+k+n-3)}{(2i+n-4)(2i+n-2)}, \tag{66}$$

so that $\beta^{(k)}_{i+1} = \alpha^{(k)}_i$ (shift $i\mapsto i+1$ in the second formula and compare). Note the boundary behavior doing the bookkeeping for us: $\alpha^{(k)}$ carries the factor $i-k+1$ and $\beta^{(k)}$ the factor $i-k$, so the recurrence never exits the branching range $i\ge k$.

11.2 Normalized coordinate maps (Lemma 5.1)

As always, the machine wants isometries into $\mathbb{R}^n\otimes V^{\mathrm S}_i$, normalized by the classical case. The spherical version of §7.5's computation:

Lemma 5.1. Let $i\ge k$, abbreviate $D_j = D^{\mathrm S}_j$, and let $\Pi_j$ project onto $V^{\mathrm S}_j$. Define coordinate-multiplication maps $$M^{\uparrow}_i:\mathbb{R}^n\otimes V^{\mathrm S}_i\to V^{\mathrm S}_{i+1},\quad M^{\uparrow}_i(v\otimes f) = \Pi_{i+1}\bigl(\langle v,\cdot\rangle f\bigr); \qquad M^{\downarrow}_i:\mathbb{R}^n\otimes V^{\mathrm S}_{i+1}\to V^{\mathrm S}_i \text{ likewise.}$$ Then $M^{\uparrow}_i(M^{\uparrow}_i)^{*} = m^{\uparrow}_i\,\mathrm{id}$ and $M^{\downarrow}_i(M^{\downarrow}_i)^{*} = m^{\downarrow}_i\,\mathrm{id}$ with $$m^{\uparrow}_i = \alpha^{(0)}_i\sqrt{\frac{D_i}{D_{i+1}}} = \frac{i+1}{2i+n}, \qquad m^{\downarrow}_i = \alpha^{(0)}_i\sqrt{\frac{D_{i+1}}{D_i}} = \frac{i+n-2}{2i+n-2}. \tag{67}$$ Hence $C^{\uparrow}_i = (M^{\uparrow}_i)^{*}/\sqrt{m^{\uparrow}_i}$ and $C^{\downarrow}_i = (M^{\downarrow}_i)^{*}/\sqrt{m^{\downarrow}_i}$ are equivariant isometries, with fiber contractions $$(C^{\uparrow}_i)^{*}(x\otimes\varphi_{i,x}Y) = \frac{\alpha^{(k)}_i}{\sqrt{m^{\uparrow}_i}}\varphi_{i+1,x}Y, \qquad (C^{\downarrow}_i)^{*}(x\otimes\varphi_{i+1,x}Y) = \frac{\alpha^{(k)}_i}{\sqrt{m^{\downarrow}_i}}\varphi_{i,x}Y,$$ directed squared weights $p_{i,i+1} = (\alpha^{(k)}_i)^2/m^{\uparrow}_i$, $p_{i+1,i} = (\alpha^{(k)}_i)^2/m^{\downarrow}_i$ satisfying the reciprocity $D_i\,p_{i,i+1} = D_{i+1}\,p_{i+1,i}$, and symmetric edge weight $$J_{i,i+1} = \sqrt{p_{i,i+1}p_{i+1,i}} = \frac{\bigl(\alpha^{(k)}_i\bigr)^2}{\alpha^{(0)}_i}. \tag{68}$$
Equivariance of $M^{\uparrow}_i$ is clear (rotations respect multiplication by $\langle v,\cdot\rangle$ jointly in $v$ and the function), so $M^{\uparrow}_i(M^{\uparrow}_i)^{*}$, a positive equivariant operator on the irreducible $V^{\mathrm S}_{i+1}$, is scalar. Trace it via the Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Summing over an orthonormal basis $(e_r)$ of $\mathbb{R}^n$ and orthonormal bases of the harmonic levels, and using $\sum_r\langle e_r, u\rangle\langle e_r, v\rangle = \langle u,v\rangle$ together with the addition formula (60) twice: $$\|M^{\uparrow}_i\|^2_{\mathrm{HS}} = \iint_{(S^{n-1})^2}\langle u,v\rangle\,K^{\mathrm S}_i(u,v)\,K^{\mathrm S}_{i+1}(u,v)\,d\sigma(u)\,d\sigma(v).$$ Rotation-invariance collapses one integral: the inner integral is independent of $v = x$, and equals $\sqrt{D_iD_{i+1}}\,\langle t_x z_{i,x},\,z_{i+1,x}\rangle$ in terms of the unit zonal vectors $z_{j,x} = K^{\mathrm S}_j(x,\cdot)/\sqrt{D_j}$ and $t_x(u) = \langle x,u\rangle$. The $k=0$ recurrence (65) evaluates the bracket: $\langle t_xz_{i,x}, z_{i+1,x}\rangle = \alpha^{(0)}_i$. So $\|M^{\uparrow}_i\|^2_{\mathrm{HS}} = \alpha^{(0)}_i\sqrt{D_iD_{i+1}}$, and dividing by $\dim V^{\mathrm S}_{i+1}$ gives $m^{\uparrow}_i = \alpha^{(0)}_i\sqrt{D_i/D_{i+1}}$. The twin computation (or the observation that $M^{\downarrow}$ and $M^{\uparrow}$ have equal HS norms, being mutual adjoints up to the tensor shuffle) gives $m^{\downarrow}_i = \alpha^{(0)}_i\sqrt{D_{i+1}/D_i}$. The explicit fractions in (67): substitute (59) and (66)-with-$k=0$; §18, check 11, verifies them in exact rationals for all $i\le300$, $n\le40$. The contractions restate (65) applied inside $(M^{\uparrow})^{*}$, noting the downward coefficient of $t\varphi_{i+1,x}$ is $\beta^{(k)}_{i+1} = \alpha^{(k)}_i$. Reciprocity: $D_ip_{i,i+1} = D_i(\alpha^{(k)}_i)^2\frac{2i+n}{i+1}$ versus $D_{i+1}p_{i+1,i} = D_{i+1}(\alpha^{(k)}_i)^2\frac{2i+n-2}{i+n-2}$; equality is the identity $m^{\uparrow}_im^{\downarrow}_i = (\alpha^{(0)}_i)^2$ read backwards through (67). The edge weight: $\sqrt{p_{i,i+1}p_{i+1,i}} = (\alpha^{(k)}_i)^2/\sqrt{m^{\uparrow}_im^{\downarrow}_i} = (\alpha^{(k)}_i)^2/\alpha^{(0)}_i$. ∎

Note the signature again: fiber coefficient squared over classical coefficient, exactly matching the cube's (20) (where the classical normalizer was hidden in the $\sqrt{(i+1)(n-i)}$) and the layer's (41)–(42).

11.3 The one-row path and its exponent

Fix $k\lt L$ and retain the levels $k\le i\le L$: a path graph with the fiber $E_x$ at every vertex and edge weights, substituting (66) into (68),

$$c^{(k)}_i = \frac{(i-k+1)(i+k+n-2)}{\sqrt{(i+1)(i+n-2)(2i+n-2)(2i+n)}}. \tag{71}$$
Lemma 5.2. Fix $0\le b\lt a$, and suppose $k/n\to b$, $L/n\to a$. Then the path's top eigenvalue satisfies $$\lambda_{k,L} \;\longrightarrow\; 2\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{row}}(a,b) = \frac{2(a-b)(1+a+b)}{(1+2a)\sqrt{a(1+a)}}.$$
For $i/n\to u\gt0$, (71) converges to $$\Gamma_{\mathrm{row}}(u,b) = \frac{(u-b)(1+u+b)}{(1+2u)\sqrt{u(1+u)}} = \frac{\sqrt{u(1+u)}}{1+2u} - \frac{b(1+b)}{(1+2u)\sqrt{u(1+u)}},$$ the second form by the expansion $(u-b)(1+u+b) = u(1+u)-b(1+b)$. It increases in $u\ge b$: the first summand $q(u)$ increases (its square is $\frac{u(1+u)}{(1+2u)^2} = \frac14\bigl(1-\frac1{(1+2u)^2}\bigr)$), the subtracted term decreases. If $b\gt0$ the entrywise convergence is uniform along the whole path (all retained $i\ge k$, $i/n\ge b - o(1) \gt 0$), so the maximum edge weight converges to $\Gamma_{\mathrm{row}}(a,b)$ and the row-sum bound gives $\limsup\lambda_{k,L}\le2\Gamma_{\mathrm{row}}(a,b)$. If $b=0$ the early entries are not uniformly controlled by the limit function; instead bound them directly: $$0\le c^{(k)}_i\le c^{(0)}_i = \sqrt{\frac{(i+1)(i+n-2)}{(2i+n-2)(2i+n)}}\le\sqrt{\frac{i+1}{n-2}},$$ the first inequality because $(i-k+1)(i+k+n-2) = (i+1)(i+n-2)-k(k+n-3)\le(i+1)(i+n-2)$ termwise under the square root pattern of (71). So edges with $i\le\varepsilon n$ have weight $O(\sqrt\varepsilon)$: splitting the path at $\varepsilon n$ and letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ recovers the same $\limsup$. The matching $\liminf$: the discrete sine vector on the last $m_n = o(n)\to\infty$ vertices, exactly as in Lemma 2.2. ∎

Theorem 4.2 on this path (fiber dimension $d^{\mathrm S}_k$, ambient $\sum_{i=k}^LD^{\mathrm S}_i$, both with known entropies) now gives: whenever $2\Gamma_{\mathrm{row}}(a,b)\gt s$,

$$A(n,s) \;\le\; 2^{\,(\Phi_{\mathrm{row}}(a,b)+o(1))\,n}, \qquad \Phi_{\mathrm{row}}(a,b) = H_{\mathrm{sph}}(a) - H_{\mathrm{sph}}(b). \tag{11}$$

At $b=0$ this is the direct KL bound of §10.3. For $b\gt0$: at the classical boundary $a = a_0(s)$ the same infinite-marginal-value trade as on the cube — the fiber rebate $H_{\mathrm{sph}}(b)\sim b\log_2\frac1b$ against a linear spectral cost — strictly improves the exponent; the full one-parameter optimization is (88) in §14, where the strict inequality is proved as part of Lemma 7.4.

11.4 The spherical-cap reduction

The classical record $B_{\mathrm{KL}}$ is not the direct bound but its cap-optimized form, so we must be able to combine the new certificates with the same reduction. It is a geometric preprocessing step, independent of everything above:

Import C (Sidelnikov's spherical-slice inequality). Fix $0\le t\le s\lt1$. In the exponent form actually used: if $A(m,t)\le2^{(B+o(1))m}$ as $m\to\infty$, then $$A(n,s)\le 2^{\bigl(B + \frac12\log_2\frac{1-t}{1-s}+o(1)\bigr)n}.$$ Geometry: among the spherical caps of a fixed angular radius $\theta$, some cap contains at least the average share of the code; centrally project that cap onto the equatorial sphere of its axis — a sphere of one dimension less. Projection can only increase normalized inner products of cap points, but by an explicitly bounded amount depending on $\theta$: tuning $\theta$ realizes any target threshold $t\le s$, and the cap-share bookkeeping costs exactly the displayed exponent. Iterating shrinks $n$ by $o(n)$ only. Citation: Sidelnikov 1974; Kabatianskii–Levenshtein 1978, Thm. 4. [Verified at the level of consequences: check 14 of §18 reproduces $B_{\mathrm{KL}}(1/2) = 0.4009442$ from this reduction plus the direct KL exponent.]

Applying Import C to the level-$r$ hierarchy exponents $\kappa_r$ of §13 (of which the one-row bound is the case $r=1$ with second row zero) defines the cap-optimized exponents

$$\overline{\kappa}_r(s) \;=\; \inf_{0\le t\le s}\Bigl\{\kappa_r(t) + \tfrac12\log_2\frac{1-t}{1-s}\Bigr\}, \tag{87}$$

with $\overline{\kappa}_0 = B_{\mathrm{KL}}$, and likewise $\overline{\kappa}_{\mathrm{row}}$ from the one-row family. At the kissing threshold the computed values (§18): $\overline{\kappa}_{\mathrm{row}}(1/2) = 0.39731$, already below the classical $0.400944$ — the 1978 record falls at the very first rung of the new machinery. The remaining spherical sections build the rungs above.
