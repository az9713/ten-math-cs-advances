16. The spherical→Euclidean dictionary

Chapter 1 and this chapter prove the same packing exponent by unrelated arguments. The paper's Appendix B shows the coincidence runs deeper: the two optimality analyses single out the same probability measure and the same weight function, once translated into a common coordinate. This section is that translation, condensed to its three identifications; it is a “sister document” section — the Euclidean objects referenced here are constructed in full in the Chapter 1 explainer (its §§6–11), and we quote them by name.

16.1 Certificates transfer

Cohn–Zhao transference (imported with E). Let $g_s(t)$ be any spherical certificate on $S^d$ — positive-definite, $g_s(t)\le0$ for $t\le s$, constant coefficient $g_{s,0}\gt0$. Convolving it over an upper hemisphere against a radial cutoff supported in the ball of radius $L_s = \sqrt{2/(1-s)}$ produces a Euclidean function $f_s$ that is a feasible Cohn–Elkies auxiliary function ($\widehat{f_s}\ge0$ from positive-definiteness; $f_s(x)\le0$ for $\|x\|\ge1$ because the hemispherical inner products then fall below $s$), with value ratio controlled by $$\mathrm{LP}_d \;\le\; \Bigl(\frac{1-s}2\Bigr)^{d/2}\frac{g_s(1)}{g_{s,0}}.$$ So every certificate of this chapter feeds the machine of Chapter 1, and the $s\uparrow1$ limit of our hierarchy pushes into exactly the regime the Euclidean program lives in ($L_s\to\infty$). Citation: Cohn–Zhao 2014, Thm. 3.4 and the discussion following it (which covers the $s\gt\frac12$ range needed here).

16.2 One measure, two proofs

Change the spectral variable of §15 to the hyperbolic coordinate $a\gt0$:

$$t = \tfrac14\operatorname{sech}^2a \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \rho(dt) = \tfrac2\pi\operatorname{sech}a\,da, \qquad \nu(dt) = \operatorname{sech}^2a\,da, \tag{112, 113}$$

(both by direct differentiation: $dt = -\tfrac12\operatorname{sech}^2a\tanh a\,da$ and $\sqrt{t(c-t)} = \tfrac14\operatorname{sech}^2a\tanh a$, $\sqrt{c-t} = \tfrac12\operatorname{sech}a\tanh a$). Under the Chebyshev limit of Theorem 8.3 at cutoff $T$, the tilted measure becomes

$$\rho_{K_T}(da) = \frac{2}{\pi Z_T}\,\frac{\operatorname{sech}a}{\sqrt{1+4T\cosh^2a}}\,da \;\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{}\;\operatorname{sech}^2a\,da \;=\;\nu(da),$$

in total variation (dominated convergence plus $\pi Z_T\sqrt T\to1$ from (106)). So at the optimum, the tilt reproduces the target $\nu$ — the relative entropy of (99) drains to zero — and the optimal spectral measure of the spherical hierarchy is $\operatorname{sech}^2a\,da$. Now the other side: Chapter 1's lower-bound argument (its strip obstruction, §7 there) produces from the Poisson kernel of a complex strip the limiting Mellin-frequency measure $p(u)\,du = \frac\pi4\operatorname{sech}^2\bigl(\frac{\pi u}2\bigr)du$ on $u\in\mathbb{R}$. The pushforward of $p(u)\,du$ under $a = \pi|u|/2$ is exactly $\operatorname{sech}^2a\,da$. The two optimality analyses — one a relative-entropy projection on the half-line, one a contour argument in a strip — converge on the same distribution. As a byproduct, the constant of Proposition 8.1 reappears: $\frac{d\nu}{d\rho}(a) = \frac\pi2\operatorname{sech}a$ and $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\nu\|\rho) = \log\pi-1$, using $\int_0^\infty\operatorname{sech}^2a\,\log\cosh a\,da = 1-\log2$.

16.3 One weight, and Wallis's product

The correspondence extends from the measure to the actual construction. Subtracting its divergent constant, the Chebyshev potential has a hyperbolic limit:

$$\widetilde K_T(a) = K_T\bigl(\tfrac14\operatorname{sech}^2a\bigr)-\tfrac12\log(4T) = \tfrac12\log\Bigl(\cosh^2a+\tfrac1{4T}\Bigr) \;\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{}\; K_\infty(a) = \log\cosh a. \tag{114}$$

Chapter 1's optimal construction modifies the logarithm of its Mellin profile by $h_\epsilon(z) = \int_0^\infty w_\epsilon(a)(\cos(az)-1)\,da$, whose principal negative weight is (in its notation, on the scale window $I_\epsilon = [\epsilon^2,\log\frac1\epsilon]$, with $b_\epsilon(a) = 1-2\epsilon(1+a)$)

$$w_{\epsilon,-}(a) = -\,b_\epsilon(a)\,\frac{e^{-2a}}{2a^2\cosh a}\,\mathbf 1_{I_\epsilon}(a) \;=\; -\,b_\epsilon(a)\,\frac{e^{-2a}}{2a^2}\,e^{-K_\infty(a)}\,\mathbf 1_{I_\epsilon}(a):$$

an exponential tilt by the limiting spherical potential. (The Euclidean construction also carries a second, exponentially smaller positive weight on a distant scale interval, needed there for global Fourier estimates; it has no spherical counterpart and vanishes from every limit below.) The final consistency check is quantitative. In Chapter 1's radius formula, the principal weight contributes $\int_0^\infty w_{*}(a)\,a\sinh a\,da = -\frac12\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-2a}\tanh a}{a}\,da$ (using $e^{-K_\infty}\sinh a = \tanh a$), and this integral is a Wallis product in disguise:

The Wallis integral. $\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-2a}\tanh a}{a}\,da \;=\; \log\frac\pi2$.
With $u = e^{-2a}$: $\tanh a = \frac{1-u}{1+u} = (1-u)^2\sum_{j\ge0}u^{2j}$, so $e^{-2a}\tanh a = \sum_{j\ge0}e^{-(4j+2)a}\bigl(1-e^{-2a}\bigr)^2$, all summands nonnegative — Tonelli permits term-by-term integration. Each term, by the Frullani integral $\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-ua}-e^{-va}}a\,da = \log\frac vu$: $$\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-(4j+2)a}-2e^{-(4j+4)a}+e^{-(4j+6)a}}{a}\,da = \log\frac{(4j+4)^2}{(4j+2)(4j+6)} = \log\frac{(2j+2)^2}{(2j+1)(2j+3)}.$$ Summing telescopes into Wallis's product: $\prod_{j\ge0}\frac{(2j+2)^2}{(2j+1)(2j+3)} = \frac\pi2$. ∎ (Numerical confirmation in §18, check 18.)

Feeding $-\frac12\log\frac\pi2$ into Chapter 1's radius formula gives the limiting sign radius $\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\lim_{d\to\infty}R_{\epsilon,d}/\sqrt d = \frac1\pi$ and hence density rate $v_d^{1/d}R_{\epsilon,d}/2\to\sqrt{e/2\pi} = 2^{-\lambda^*}$ — the same constant, now derived through the spherical weight. The identification is of limiting objects: it does not claim finite-dimensional spherical certificates converge to a particular Euclidean function, and the order of limits ($n$, then $r$, then $T$, forcing $s_T\uparrow1$) is essential. But it settles that the two chapters' optima are one mathematical structure seen from two sides — the strongest kind of independent confirmation two 25-page proofs can give each other.
