4. Boolean harmonics on the cube

We now build the raw material for the binary constructions: the function spaces on the cube, the ladder operators connecting them, and the harmonic subspaces that will become the moving fibers of §3. Everything here is elementary and fully proved; the section's product is a small table of exact identities — (12) through (19) — that §5 will consume whole.

4.1 Fourier levels

Identify the binary alphabet with $\{\pm1\}$ and write $X_n = \{\pm1\}^n$ for the cube. The normalized coordinate vector of a word and the resulting closeness function are

$$\ell_x = \frac{x}{\sqrt n} \in \mathbb{R}^n, \qquad t(x,y) = \langle \ell_x,\ell_y\rangle = 1 - \frac{2d_H(x,y)}{n},$$

since each agreeing coordinate contributes $+\frac1n$ and each disagreeing one $-\frac1n$. So Hamming distance and the inner-product encoding of §3 are the same data, and a minimum-distance-$d$ code is exactly a code with threshold $s = 1-2d/n$.

Give real-valued functions on $X_n$ the uniform-probability inner product $\langle f,g\rangle = 2^{-n}\sum_{x} f(x)g(x)$. The Fourier characters $\chi_S(x) = \prod_{r\in S}x_r$, one for each subset $S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$, form an orthonormal basis (distinct characters differ by some $\chi_{S\triangle T}\ne 1$, which averages to zero). Write $e_S = \chi_S$ for the basis vectors, and collect them by degree:

$$V^{\square}_i = \operatorname{span}\{e_S : |S| = i\}, \qquad D^{\square}_i = \dim V^{\square}_i = \binom{n}{i}.$$

(The superscript $\square$ tags whole-cube objects; later sections use $\mathrm{J}$ for Johnson-layer and $\mathrm{S}$ for spherical.) The cube's symmetry group — coordinate permutations and sign flips — permutes each level's basis up to signs, so each $V_i^\square$ is symmetry-invariant. At a word $x$, the classical construction of §2.3 uses the single unit vector

$$v_{i,x} = \binom{n}{i}^{-1/2}\sum_{|S|=i}\chi_S(x)\,e_S,$$

the normalized “level-$i$ average pinned at $x$”: it is the unique (up to sign) unit vector in $V_i^\square$ fixed by every symmetry fixing $x$. The pairwise inner products $\langle v_{i,x},v_{i,y}\rangle$ are the normalized Krawtchouk polynomials of the classical theory — but we will never need their formulas, only the ladder identities below.

4.2 The addition and deletion ladder

Define the Boolean addition and deletion operators on the Fourier basis, extended linearly:

$$U e_S = \sum_{r\notin S} e_{S\cup\{r\}}, \qquad D e_S = \sum_{r\in S} e_{S\setminus\{r\}}.$$

$U$ raises degree by one, $D$ lowers it. They are adjoint: $\langle Ue_S, e_T\rangle$ and $\langle e_S, De_T\rangle$ both equal $1$ exactly when $T = S\cup\{r\}$ for some $r \notin S$, else $0$; hence $U^{*} = D$. Their commutator is diagonal:

Identity (12). On each level, $\;(DU - UD)\big|_{V^{\square}_i} = (n-2i)\,\mathrm{id}$.
Expand both orders on a basis vector with $|S| = i$. Adding $r\notin S$ then deleting from $S\cup\{r\}$ gives $DUe_S = (n-i)\,e_S + \sum_{r\notin S,\, r''\in S} e_{(S\cup r)\setminus r''}$ (the first term from deleting the just-added $r$, which can happen $n-i$ ways). Deleting $r \in S$ then adding to $S\setminus\{r\}$ gives $UDe_S = i\,e_S + \sum_{r\in S,\, r'\notin S} e_{(S\setminus r)\cup r'}$ (re-adding the just-deleted $r$, $i$ ways). The two double sums range over identical pairs (one coordinate leaves $S$, another enters), so they cancel in the difference, leaving $(n-i) - i = n-2i$ times $e_S$. ∎

4.3 Harmonic spaces and their dimension

Definition (Boolean harmonics). For $0\le k\le n/2$, the degree-$k$ Boolean harmonic space is $$E_k = \ker\bigl(D : V^{\square}_k \to V^{\square}_{k-1}\bigr), \qquad V^{\square}_{-1} = 0 .$$ Concretely, a coefficient vector on $k$-element subsets is harmonic when its coefficients sum to zero over all $k$-sets containing any fixed $(k{-}1)$-set. $E_0$ is the constant line; $E_1 = \{\sum_r a_r e_{\{r\}} : \sum_r a_r = 0\}$ has dimension $n-1$.
Dimension of $E_k$. For $1\le k\le n/2$: $U: V^{\square}_{k-1}\to V^{\square}_k$ is injective, $D: V^{\square}_k\to V^{\square}_{k-1}$ is surjective, and $$d^{\square}_k = \dim E_k = \binom nk - \binom n{k-1}.$$
For $f \in V^{\square}_{k-1}$, identity (12) at level $k-1$ gives $$\|Uf\|^2 - \|Df\|^2 = \langle (DU-UD)f, f\rangle = (n-2k+2)\|f\|^2. \tag{4.a}$$ Since $k \le n/2$, the factor $n-2k+2 \ge 2$ is positive, so $\|Uf\| \gt 0$ whenever $f\neq0$: $U$ is injective on level $k-1$. Its adjoint $D$ (from level $k$) is therefore surjective onto level $k-1$, and rank–nullity gives $\dim\ker D = \binom nk - \binom n{k-1}$. ∎

Two consequences deserve emphasis. First, iterating the same one-line argument decomposes every level into harmonic strata, $V_k^\square = E_k \oplus UE_{k-1}\oplus U^2E_{k-2}\oplus\cdots$ — $E_k$ is precisely the new content appearing at degree $k$. Second, and centrally for us: when $k = bn$ grows proportionally with $n$, the dimension $d^\square_k$ is exponentially large. The following Stirling estimate, used silently everywhere in asymptotic coding theory, we prove once and cite forever.

Lemma B (entropy counting). Fix $0 \lt u \lt 1$ and integers $k_n$ with $k_n/n \to u$. Then $$\frac1n\log_2\binom{n}{k_n} \;\longrightarrow\; H_2(u), \qquad\text{and for } 0\lt u \lt \tfrac12,\quad \frac1n\log_2\Bigl[\binom{n}{k_n}-\binom{n}{k_n-1}\Bigr] \;\longrightarrow\; H_2(u).$$
Stirling's formula $\log_2 m! = m\log_2 m - m\log_2 e + O(\log m)$ gives $\log_2\binom{n}{k} = n\log_2 n - k\log_2 k - (n-k)\log_2(n-k) + O(\log n)$; dividing by $n$ and substituting $k = un + o(n)$ yields $H_2(u) + o(1)$ (the linear $-\log_2 e$ terms cancel: $n = k + (n-k)$). For the harmonic difference, factor $$\binom nk - \binom n{k-1} = \binom nk\Bigl(1 - \frac{k}{\,n-k+1\,}\Bigr),$$ using $\binom n{k-1}/\binom nk = k/(n-k+1)$. For $u \lt \frac12$ the bracket tends to $1 - \frac{u}{1-u} \gt 0$, a constant, which vanishes at rate scale. ∎

So attaching $E_{k}$, $k = bn$, to a code point buys fiber entropy $H_2(b)$ — exactly the quantity that will be subtracted from the classical exponent in Theorem 2.3.

4.4 One harmonic space, embedded at every level

The moving-subspace plan needs the same space $E_k$ to live inside every retained Fourier level, coherently. The ladder does this for free. For $h\in E_k$, define its iterated raises $U^rh$; the key computational identities are:

Identities (13). Let $h\in E_k$ and write $m = n-2k$. For $1\le r\le n-2k$ and $0\le r\le n-2k$ respectively, $$D U^{r}h = r\,(m - r + 1)\,U^{r-1}h, \qquad \|U^{r}h\|^2 = r!\;\frac{m!}{(m-r)!}\;\|h\|^2 .$$
Induction on $r$ for the first identity. Base $r=1$: $DUh = UDh + (n-2k)h = (n-2k)h$ since $Dh=0$, matching $1\cdot m$. Step: assuming it at $r-1$, and noting $U^{r-1}h$ lives in level $i = k+r-1$, $$DU^{r}h = (UD + (n-2i)\,\mathrm{id})\,U^{r-1}h = U\bigl[(r{-}1)(m{-}r{+}2)U^{r-2}h\bigr] + (m - 2r + 2)\,U^{r-1}h,$$ using (12) at level $i$ (where $n - 2i = m - 2r + 2$) and the inductive hypothesis. The coefficient is $(r-1)(m-r+2) + (m-2r+2) = r(m-r+1)$, an algebra check. For the norms: $\|U^rh\|^2 = \langle DU^rh, U^{r-1}h\rangle = r(m-r+1)\|U^{r-1}h\|^2$, and the product $\prod_{j=1}^{r} j(m-j+1) = r!\,m!/(m-r)!$ telescopes. ∎

Consequently, for every $k \le i \le n-k$ the normalized addition map

$$\varphi_i h = \frac{U^{\,i-k}h}{\sqrt{(i-k)!\,(n-2k)!/(n-i-k)!}} \qquad (h\in E_k) \tag{14}$$

is an isometric embedding $E_k \hookrightarrow V^{\square}_i$ — a copy of the same harmonic space at every level from $k$ up to $n-k$ (the norm factor is $(13)$ with $r = i-k$; it is positive exactly in this range). These copies are pinned at the all-ones word. To move them to an arbitrary word $x$, use the sign-twist unitary $R_x e_S = \chi_S(x)e_S$ and set $\varphi_{i,x} = R_x\varphi_i$. For $k=0$ (so $h$ = the constant), $\varphi_{i,x}h$ is exactly the classical pinned vector $v_{i,x}$: the classical construction is the $1$-dimensional floor of this staircase.

4.5 Coordinate transitions between neighboring levels

The last ingredient is the operator $B$ demanded by Proposition 4.1 — and it must live on $\mathbb{R}^n\otimes\mathcal V$, tensoring with the coordinate space. The natural building blocks are two elementary maps that split off one coordinate index. For $|T| = i+1$ and $|T'| = i$ define

$$C_{i,i+1}\,e_T = \frac1{\sqrt{i+1}}\sum_{r\in T} e_r\otimes e_{T\setminus\{r\}}, \qquad C_{i+1,i}\,e_{T'} = \frac1{\sqrt{n-i}}\sum_{r\notin T'} e_r\otimes e_{T'\cup\{r\}}, \tag{15, 16}$$

where $e_r$ is the $r$th standard basis vector of $\mathbb{R}^n$; so $C_{i,i+1}:V^{\square}_{i+1}\to\mathbb{R}^n\otimes V^{\square}_i$ (“delete, and remember what you deleted”) and $C_{i+1,i}: V^{\square}_i\to\mathbb{R}^n\otimes V^{\square}_{i+1}$ (“add, and remember what you added”). Each is an isometry: its output is a sum of distinct orthonormal tensors, $i+1$ resp. $n-i$ of them, with matching normalization. Moreover their images inside a common $\mathbb{R}^n\otimes V^{\square}_i$ are orthogonal: deletion outputs live on tensors $e_r\otimes e_S$ with $r\notin S$, addition outputs on $r\in S$.

Now contract with a code point's coordinate vector. Set $Q_i = (i-k+1)(n-i-k)$.

Contractions (17)–(18). For every word $x$, $h \in E_k$, and $k\le i\lt n-k$, $$C_{i,i+1}^{*}\bigl(\ell_x\otimes\varphi_{i,x}h\bigr) = \sqrt{\frac{Q_i}{n(i+1)}}\,\varphi_{i+1,x}h, \qquad C_{i+1,i}^{*}\bigl(\ell_x\otimes\varphi_{i+1,x}h\bigr) = \sqrt{\frac{Q_i}{n(n-i)}}\,\varphi_{i,x}h.$$
First take $x = \mathbf 1$ (the all-ones word), so $\ell_x = \frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_r e_r$ and $R_x = \mathrm{id}$. For $f\in V^{\square}_i$ and any basis vector $e_T$ of $V^{\square}_{i+1}$, $$\bigl\langle C_{i,i+1}^{*}(\ell_{\mathbf 1}\otimes f),\, e_T\bigr\rangle = \bigl\langle \ell_{\mathbf 1}\otimes f,\, C_{i,i+1}e_T\bigr\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{i+1}}\sum_{r\in T}\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\,\langle f, e_{T\setminus r}\rangle = \frac{\langle Uf, e_T\rangle}{\sqrt{n(i+1)}},$$ because $\langle Uf,e_T\rangle$ sums $\langle f, e_{T\setminus r}\rangle$ over exactly the same $r\in T$. Hence $C^{*}_{i,i+1}(\ell_{\mathbf 1}\otimes f) = Uf/\sqrt{n(i+1)}$, and the twin computation gives $C^{*}_{i+1,i}(\ell_{\mathbf 1}\otimes g) = Dg/\sqrt{n(n-i)}$ for $g\in V^{\square}_{i+1}$. On harmonic staircase vectors, (13)–(14) convert $U$ and $D$ into the normalized steps $$U\varphi_ih = \sqrt{Q_i}\,\varphi_{i+1}h,\qquad D\varphi_{i+1}h = \sqrt{Q_i}\,\varphi_ih.$$ To see these: the normalizer in (14) grows by the factor $\sqrt{(i+1-k)(n-i-k)} = \sqrt{Q_i}$ from level $i$ to $i+1$, which gives the $U$ identity directly; and (13) with $r = i+1-k$, $m=n-2k$ gives $DU^{r}h = (i+1-k)(n-k-i)\,U^{r-1}h = Q_i\,U^{r-1}h$, so dividing by the level-$(i{+}1)$ normalizer and converting to the level-$i$ one costs $\sqrt{Q_i}$, leaving $D\varphi_{i+1}h = (Q_i/\sqrt{Q_i})\varphi_ih = \sqrt{Q_i}\,\varphi_ih$. This proves the claim at $x=\mathbf 1$. For general $x$: the twist $R_x$ acts on $V^\square_\bullet$, and $x$ itself twists the coordinate factor via the diagonal sign matrix $\sigma_x = \mathrm{diag}(x_1,\dots,x_n)$, which satisfies $\ell_x = \sigma_x\ell_{\mathbf 1}$ and the equivariance $(\sigma_x\otimes R_x)\,C_{i,j} = C_{i,j}\,R_x$ (adding or deleting coordinate $r$ from $S$ changes $\chi_S(x)$ by the factor $x_r$, which is what $\sigma_x$ supplies). Conjugating the $x=\mathbf 1$ identities by $\sigma_x\otimes R_x$ transports them verbatim. ∎

Squaring the two contraction coefficients gives the directed transition weights between neighboring levels along the harmonic staircase:

$$p_{i,i+1} = \frac{Q_i}{n(i+1)}, \qquad p_{i+1,i} = \frac{Q_i}{n(n-i)}, \qquad Q_i = (i-k+1)(n-i-k). \tag{4.b}$$

For $k=0$ these weights sum to $1$ at each level of the full path: $p_{i,i+1}+p_{i,i-1} = \frac{n-i}{n} + \frac{i}{n} = 1$ (check 3 of §18 verifies this exactly), reflecting that coordinate multiplication moves level-$i$ content only to levels $i\pm1$. For $k \gt 0$ they sum to less than $1$: multiplication also leaks into symmetry types outside the staircase, and the leak is simply discarded — the machinery never needs the weights to be probabilities. What it does need is the exact dimension-weighted balance

$$\binom{n}{i}\,p_{i,i+1} \;=\; \binom{n}{i+1}\,p_{i+1,i}, \tag{19}$$

which holds because $\binom ni\frac{Q_i}{n(i+1)} = \binom n{i+1}\frac{Q_i}{n(n-i)}$ reduces to the Pascal identity $\binom ni(n-i) = \binom n{i+1}(i+1)$. Identity (19) is what lets a directed, unequal-weight transition system be symmetrized into an honest symmetric matrix — the move that §5 performs first.
