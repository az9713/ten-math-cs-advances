3. The Cohn–Elkies certificate: one function bounds every packing

This section proves inequality (4): every admissible $f$ is a certificate that no packing anywhere in $\mathbb{R}^d$ can beat the density $v_d f(0) / (2^d\widehat f(0))$. The proof is a double count of pair interactions — one count using the sign of $f$, one using the sign of $\widehat f$ — glued by Poisson summation. We first handle periodic packings, where the count is finite, then show periodic packings capture $\Delta_d$.

3.1 The periodic case: a double count

A periodic packing is a finite union of lattice translates: fix a lattice $L$ and points $t_1, \dots, t_N$, distinct modulo $L$, and take $\mathcal{P} = \bigcup_{j=1}^N (t_j + L)$, assumed $1$-separated. Its density is exact (no $\limsup$ needed): each fundamental cell of volume $\operatorname{covol}(L)$ contains exactly $N$ centers, so the fraction of space covered by the radius-$\tfrac12$ balls is $$\bar\delta(\mathcal{P}) \;=\; \frac{N}{\operatorname{covol}(L)}\cdot\frac{v_d}{2^d}.$$

Proposition C (certificate bound, periodic case). Let $f \in \mathcal{A}_d$ and let $\mathcal{P} = \bigcup_j (t_j+L)$ be a periodic packing as above. Then $$\frac{N}{\operatorname{covol}(L)} \;\le\; \frac{f(0)}{\widehat f(0)}, \qquad\text{hence}\qquad \bar\delta(\mathcal{P}) \;\le\; \frac{v_d}{2^d}\,\frac{f(0)}{\widehat f(0)}.$$

Consider the finite-plus-lattice pair sum $$S \;=\; \sum_{j=1}^N \sum_{k=1}^N \sum_{\ell \in L} f(t_j - t_k + \ell),$$ absolutely convergent by Schwartz decay. We bound $S$ from above and below.

Above (real side, using $f \le 0$ outside radius 1). Split off the terms with $j = k$ and $\ell = 0$, which contribute $N f(0)$. Every other term evaluates $f$ at $t_j - t_k + \ell$ with either $j \ne k$ or $\ell \ne 0$; in both cases $t_j + \ell$ and $t_k$ are two distinct points of the packing $\mathcal{P}$, so $|t_j - t_k + \ell| \ge 1$ by $1$-separation, and admissibility gives $f(t_j-t_k+\ell) \le 0$. Hence $$S \;\le\; N f(0).$$

Below (Fourier side, using $\widehat f \ge 0$). Apply Poisson summation (Theorem 2.8) to the inner sum at the point $x = t_j - t_k$: $$S = \sum_{j,k}\frac{1}{\operatorname{covol}(L)}\sum_{m \in L^*}\widehat f(m)\, e^{2\pi i m\cdot(t_j-t_k)} = \frac{1}{\operatorname{covol}(L)}\sum_{m \in L^*}\widehat f(m)\, \Bigl|\sum_{j=1}^N e^{2\pi i m\cdot t_j}\Bigr|^2,$$ where we swapped the finite sums with the absolutely convergent $m$-sum and factored $\sum_{j,k} e^{2\pi i m \cdot t_j}\overline{e^{2\pi i m\cdot t_k}} = |\sum_j e^{2\pi i m\cdot t_j}|^2$. Every summand is $\ge 0$ (this is where $\widehat f \ge 0$ acts), so keeping only $m = 0$, where the exponential sum is $N$: $$S \;\ge\; \frac{\widehat f(0)\, N^2}{\operatorname{covol}(L)}.$$

Chaining the two bounds (Fig. 9), $\widehat f(0)N^2/\operatorname{covol}(L) \le S \le Nf(0)$; divide by $N \widehat f(0) \gt 0$. ∎

What would break. Drop "$f \le 0$ outside radius $1$" and the upper count fails: distant pairs could contribute positively and $S \le Nf(0)$ dies. Drop "$\widehat f \ge 0$" and the lower count fails: negative Fourier modes could cancel the $m=0$ term. Drop "$\widehat f(0) \gt 0$" and the final division is illegal (and the bound trivializes). The three conditions of (2) are exactly the three joints of this proof — nothing is decorative.

3.2 Reduction to periodic packings

Lemma D (periodic packings suffice). For every packing $\mathcal{P}$ and every $\varepsilon \gt 0$ there is a periodic packing $\mathcal{P}'$ with $\bar\delta(\mathcal{P}') \ge \bar\delta(\mathcal{P}) - \varepsilon$. Consequently $\Delta_d = \sup\{\bar\delta(\mathcal{P}') : \mathcal{P}' \text{ periodic}\}$.

Choose radii $R_n \to \infty$ realizing the $\limsup$: the covered fraction $\delta_n$ of $Q_{R_n}$ tends to $\bar\delta(\mathcal{P})$. Fix $n$, write $R = R_n$. Every point of $Q_R$ covered by a packing ball is covered by a ball whose center lies in $Q_{R+1}$, so the finite set $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{P} \cap Q_{R+1}$ (finite: its points are $1$-separated, so the balls $B(x,\tfrac12)$, $x \in \mathcal F$, are disjoint and all fit in $Q_{R+2}$, bounding $|\mathcal F| \le \operatorname{vol}(Q_{R+2})2^d/v_d$) satisfies $$|\mathcal{F}|\cdot \frac{v_d}{2^d} \;\ge\; \operatorname{vol}\Bigl(Q_R \cap \bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{P}} B(x,\tfrac12)\Bigr) \;=\; \delta_n \operatorname{vol}(Q_R).$$ Now periodize $\mathcal{F}$ with the lattice $L = (2R+3)\,\mathbb{Z}^d$: let $\mathcal{P}' = \mathcal{F} + L$. This is a packing: two points in the same translate of $\mathcal{F}$ are $1$-separated already; two points in different translates differ, in some coordinate, by at least $(2R+3) - 2(R+1) = 1$. Its density is exact: $$\bar\delta(\mathcal{P}') = \frac{|\mathcal{F}|}{(2R+3)^d}\cdot\frac{v_d}{2^d} \;\ge\; \delta_n\,\frac{\operatorname{vol}(Q_R)}{(2R+3)^d} = \delta_n \Bigl(\frac{2R}{2R+3}\Bigr)^{\!d}.$$ As $n \to \infty$, $\delta_n \to \bar\delta(\mathcal{P})$ and the volume ratio tends to $1$; choose $n$ large enough. ∎

Theorem E (Gorbachev, Cohn–Elkies; eq. (4)). For every $f \in \mathcal{A}_d$, $$\Delta_d \;\le\; \frac{v_d}{2^d}\,\frac{f(0)}{\widehat f(0)}, \qquad\text{and therefore}\qquad \Delta_d \le \mathrm{LP}_d.$$

By Lemma D it suffices to bound periodic packings, and Proposition C does exactly that, uniformly. Taking the infimum over $f \in \mathcal{A}_d$ gives (4) with $\mathrm{LP}_d$ as in (3). ∎

3.3 The whole method in dimension one

Here is the entire machine, run by hand where every step is visible. In $d = 1$ take the tent function and compute its transform by writing it as a convolution of two box functions $\chi = \mathbf{1}_{[-1/2,1/2]}$:

$$f(x) = \max(1 - |x|,\, 0) = (\chi * \chi)(x), \qquad \widehat f(\xi) = \widehat\chi(\xi)^2 = \Bigl(\frac{\sin \pi\xi}{\pi\xi}\Bigr)^{\!2},$$

using Lemma 2.2(iv) and the elementary integral $\widehat\chi(\xi) = \int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e^{-2\pi i x\xi}dx = \frac{\sin\pi\xi}{\pi\xi}$. Check the three certificate conditions against Fig. 10: $f \le 0$ (indeed $= 0$) for $|x| \ge 1$; $\widehat f = \mathrm{sinc}^2 \ge 0$ everywhere; $\widehat f(0) = 1 \gt 0$. And $f(0) = 1$, so the certified bound is $$\Delta_1 \;\le\; \frac{v_1}{2^1}\cdot\frac{f(0)}{\widehat f(0)} \;=\; \frac{2}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{1} \;=\; 1,$$

which is exactly $\Delta_1 = 1$: the machine is tight in dimension one, with the tent as its magic function. (The tent is continuous but not Schwartz; the double-count proof of Proposition C never used more than continuity, decay $|f(x)| \le C(1+|x|)^{-2}$, and $\sum_m|\widehat f(m)| \lt \infty$ — all true for the tent, since $\widehat f(m) = O(m^{-2})$, so the certificate is legitimate as stated. With extra mollification care one checks the Schwartz infimum in (3) is also exactly $1$; we never need that refinement.)

Dimension one also reveals what tightness requires. Where must equality hold in the double count? Every inequality we discarded must be an equality: $f$ must vanish at every nonzero inter-point distance of the optimal packing (the tent vanishes at $\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$ — the distances of the integer packing), and $\widehat f$ must vanish at every nonzero dual-lattice point (here $\mathrm{sinc}^2$ vanishes on $\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$). This "forced interpolation" of zeros is exactly the structure Viazovska engineered in $d = 8$ with modular forms. In high dimensions, Theorem 1.1 says no function — magic or otherwise — can push the machine below $(\sqrt{e/2\pi}+o(1))^d$.
