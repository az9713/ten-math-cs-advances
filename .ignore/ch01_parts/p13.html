Appendix A: the two uncertainty constants never tie ($A_+(d) \lt A_-(d)$)

Theorem 1.2 makes $A_+(d)$ and $A_-(d)$ asymptotically indistinguishable. This appendix proves they are nonetheless strictly ordered in every dimension, by an explicit operator: integrating the radial tail of a $(-1)$-eigenfunction produces a $(+1)$-eigenfunction whose last sign change happens strictly earlier (Fig. 32). One imported fact is needed — that the $(-1)$-problem has an honest extremizer:

Import 4 (attained extremizer) [CG19, Thm. 1.4]. For every $d \ge 1$ the infimum $A_-(d)$ of definition (6) is attained: there exists a nonzero radial $g \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^d;\mathbb{R})$ with $\widehat g = -g$, $g(0) = 0$, and $r(g) = A_-(d) \lt \infty$.
Proposition A.1. Let $d \ge 1$, $\lambda = d/2$, and let $0 \ne g \in L^1_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^d;\mathbb{R})$ satisfy $\widehat g = -g$ and $g(0) = 0$ (continuous representative). Define $T_dg(0) = 0$ and, for $x \ne 0$ (eq. (87)), $$(T_dg)(x) \;=\; \frac\lambda2\int_1^\infty t^{\lambda-1}\,g(tx)\,dt.$$ Then $T_dg$ is nonzero, continuous, integrable, with $$\widehat{T_dg} = T_dg, \qquad (T_dg)(0) = 0, \qquad \|T_dg\|_1 \le \tfrac12\|g\|_1.$$ If $r(g) \lt \infty$ then $r(T_dg) \lt r(g)$; if $g$ is Schwartz, so is $T_dg$. Consequently $A_+(d) \lt A_-(d)$ for every $d \ge 1$.

Step 0: regularity of $g$. Since $g = -\widehat g$ with $g \in L^1$, inversion (Theorem 2.4) makes $g$ bounded and continuous, with $\|g\|_\infty \le \|g\|_1$. In particular $\int|g(x)|\,|x|^{-\lambda}dx \lt \infty$: split at $|x| = 1$; inside, $|x|^{-\lambda}$ is integrable in $\mathbb{R}^d$ ($\lambda = d/2 \lt d$) against the bounded $g$; outside, $|x|^{-\lambda} \le 1$ against $g \in L^1$.

Step 1: the central Mellin cancellation (eq. (88)). We claim $\mathcal Mg(\lambda) = \int_0^\infty g(r)r^{\lambda-1}dr = 0$ — the $L^1$ analogue of what Theorem S would give at the self-dual point $z = \lambda$, proved here directly since $g$ need not be Schwartz. Set $J(t) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^d}g(x)\,e^{-\pi t|x|^2}dx$ for $t \gt 0$. The multiplication formula (Lemma 2.2(iii)) with the Gaussian pair (Proposition 2.3) and $\widehat g = -g$ gives Gaussian duality: $$-J(t) = \int \widehat g\,(x)e^{-\pi t|x|^2}dx = \int g(\xi)\,t^{-\lambda}e^{-\pi|\xi|^2/t}\,d\xi = t^{-\lambda}J(1/t), \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad J(t) = -t^{-\lambda}J(1/t).$$ Moreover $\int_0^\infty t^{\lambda/2-1}|J(t)|\,dt \le \int_0^\infty t^{\lambda/2-1}\int|g(x)|e^{-\pi t|x|^2}dx\,dt = \frac{\Gamma(\lambda/2)}{\pi^{\lambda/2}}\int|g(x)|\,|x|^{-\lambda}dx \lt \infty$ by Tonelli and Step 0. Now substitute $t \mapsto 1/t$ in $\mathcal I = \int_0^\infty t^{\lambda/2-1}J(t)\,dt$: the measure $t^{\lambda/2-1}dt$ maps to $t^{-\lambda/2-1}dt$, and the duality relation converts the integrand so that $\mathcal I = -\mathcal I$, forcing $\mathcal I = 0$. On the other hand, computing $\mathcal I$ by Fubini (now legal) and polar integration: $$0 = \mathcal I = \int g(x)\,\frac{\Gamma(\lambda/2)}{(\pi|x|^2)^{\lambda/2}}\,dx = \frac{\Gamma(\lambda/2)}{\pi^{\lambda/2}}\,S_d\int_0^\infty g(r)\,r^{d-\lambda-1}dr = \frac{\Gamma(\lambda/2)S_d}{\pi^{\lambda/2}}\;\mathcal Mg(\lambda),$$ using $d - \lambda = \lambda$. Hence $\mathcal Mg(\lambda) = 0$.

Step 2: basic properties of $T_dg$. For $x \ne 0$, absolute convergence: substituting $s = t|x|$, the defining integral is $\frac\lambda2|x|^{-\lambda}\int_{|x|}^\infty s^{\lambda-1}g_{\mathrm{prof}}(s)\,ds$ (radial profile), finite since $s^{\lambda-1} \le s^{d-1}/|x|^{\lambda}$ for $s \ge |x|$ and $g \in L^1$. Tonelli with $d = 2\lambda$: $$\|T_dg\|_1 \le \frac\lambda2\int_1^\infty t^{\lambda-1}\Bigl(\int|g(tx)|dx\Bigr)dt = \frac\lambda2\,\|g\|_1\int_1^\infty t^{\lambda-1-d}\,dt = \frac\lambda2\,\|g\|_1\cdot\frac1\lambda = \frac{\|g\|_1}{2},$$ which also licenses transforming under the integral sign. By the dilation rule, $\widehat{g(t\,\cdot)} = t^{-d}\widehat g(\cdot/t) = -t^{-d}g(\cdot/t)$, so (eq. (89)) $$\widehat{T_dg}(\xi) = -\frac\lambda2\int_1^\infty t^{\lambda-d-1}g(\xi/t)\,dt = -\frac\lambda2\int_0^1 s^{\lambda-1}g(s\xi)\,ds = \frac\lambda2\int_1^\infty s^{\lambda-1}g(s\xi)\,ds = (T_dg)(\xi),$$ substituting $s = 1/t$ (with $t^{\lambda - d - 1}dt = -s^{\lambda-1}ds$, using $d = 2\lambda$) in the middle step and the central cancellation of Step 1 — in the form $\int_0^\infty s^{\lambda-1}g(s\xi)\,ds = |\xi|^{-\lambda}\mathcal Mg(\lambda) = 0$ — in the last. The small-scale representation $-\frac\lambda2\int_0^1s^{\lambda-1}g(s\xi)ds$ extends continuously to $\xi = 0$ with value $-\frac\lambda2 g(0)\int_0^1s^{\lambda-1}ds = -g(0)/2 = 0$, matching the defined value $T_dg(0) = 0$ and giving continuity everywhere.

Step 3: $T_dg \ne 0$, and Schwartz preservation. In profile form the large-scale representation reads $$(T_dg)(r) = \frac\lambda2\,r^{-\lambda}\int_r^\infty s^{\lambda-1}g(s)\,ds,$$ so $\frac{d}{dr}\bigl(r^\lambda\,T_dg(r)\bigr) = -\frac\lambda2 r^{\lambda-1}g(r)$, i.e. $\bigl(r\frac{d}{dr} + \lambda\bigr)T_dg = -\frac\lambda2\,g$. If $T_dg \equiv 0$, the left side vanishes and $g \equiv 0$ — contradiction. If $g$ is Schwartz: differentiating the small-scale representation shows smoothness at the origin (each $\xi$-derivative brings down powers of $s \le 1$, all integrals converging uniformly), and differentiating the large-scale representation shows rapid decay of all derivatives (the tail integral of a Schwartz profile decays faster than any power); so $T_dg$ is Schwartz.

Step 4: the sign radius strictly shrinks. Let $R = r(g) \lt \infty$. First, $R \gt 0$: otherwise $g \ge 0$ everywhere with $\int g = \widehat g(0) = -g(0) = 0$, forcing $g \equiv 0$. For $r \ge R$ the profile satisfies $g(s) \ge 0$ for all $s \ge r$, so $$(T_dg)(r) = \frac\lambda2 r^{-\lambda}\int_r^\infty s^{\lambda-1}g(s)\,ds \;\ge\; 0,$$ and in fact $\gt 0$: equality would force $g = 0$ on $[r,\infty)$, making both $g$ and $\widehat g = -g$ compactly supported, which contradicts the analyticity argument of Proposition F (a nonzero compactly supported function cannot have compactly supported transform). In particular $(T_dg)(R) \gt 0$; since $T_dg$ is continuous, positivity extends to an interval $[R-\varepsilon, R]$ for some $\varepsilon \gt 0$, and combined with strict positivity on $[R,\infty)$ this gives $r(T_dg) \le R - \varepsilon \lt R$.

Step 5: conclusion. Apply Steps 1–4 to the extremizer $g$ of Import 4: $T_dg$ is a nonzero integrable $(+1)$-eigenfunction vanishing at the origin, so by definition (6), $$A_+(d) \;\le\; r(T_dg) \;\lt\; r(g) \;=\; A_-(d).$$ ∎
