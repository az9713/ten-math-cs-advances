12. Assembling Theorems 1.1 and 1.2

All the pieces are on the table; the endgame is bookkeeping.

Proof of Theorem 1.1. Upper bound. Fix $\epsilon \in (0, \epsilon_0)$ and let $f_-, f_+, R_{\epsilon,d}$ be as in Theorem 4.1. By Bridge two (Proposition G(i)), $F_{\epsilon,d}(x) = f_-(R_{\epsilon,d}\,x)$ is admissible with $F_{\epsilon,d}(0)/\widehat{F_{\epsilon,d}}(0) = R_{\epsilon,d}^d$, so definition (3) gives (eq. (86)) $$\mathrm{LP}_d \;\le\; \frac{v_d}{2^d}\,R_{\epsilon,d}^{\,d}.$$ Taking $d$-th roots and using $v_d^{1/d} = (1+o(1))\sqrt{2\pi e/d}$ (Import 2): $$\limsup_{d\to\infty}\mathrm{LP}_d^{1/d} \;\le\; \lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{\sqrt{2\pi e/d}}{2}\,R_{\epsilon,d} \;=\; \frac{\sqrt{2\pi e}}{2}\,\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{R_{\epsilon,d}}{\sqrt d},$$ and letting $\epsilon \downarrow 0$, Theorem 4.1's double limit turns the right side into $\frac{\sqrt{2\pi e}}{2}\cdot\frac1\pi = \sqrt{e/2\pi}$.

Lower bound. Theorem 3.8: $\liminf_d \mathrm{LP}_d^{1/d} \ge \sqrt{e/2\pi}$. The two bounds squeeze: $$\sqrt{\frac{e}{2\pi}} \;\le\; \liminf_{d\to\infty}\mathrm{LP}_d^{1/d} \;\le\; \limsup_{d\to\infty}\mathrm{LP}_d^{1/d} \;\le\; \sqrt{\frac{e}{2\pi}}.$$ ∎

Proof of Theorem 1.2. Lower bound: Proposition 3.7 gives $A_\varsigma(d) \ge c\sqrt d$ for every $c \lt 1/\pi$ and large $d$, i.e. $\liminf A_\varsigma(d)/\sqrt d \ge 1/\pi$, both signs. Upper bound: with $f_\pm, f_0$ from Theorem 4.1, set $g_{\epsilon,d,-} = f_+ - f_-$ and $g_{\epsilon,d,+} = f_0$. By Bridge two (Proposition G(ii)–(iii)) these are nonzero eigenfunctions of eigenvalues $-1$ and $+1$, vanishing at the origin, strictly positive outside $R_{\epsilon,d}$; definition (6) gives $A_\varsigma(d) \le R_{\epsilon,d}$ for both signs, hence $$\limsup_{d\to\infty}\frac{A_\varsigma(d)}{\sqrt d} \;\le\; \lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{R_{\epsilon,d}}{\sqrt d} \;\xrightarrow[\epsilon\downarrow0]{}\; \frac1\pi.$$ ∎

The chapter's results, assembled. $$\lim_{d\to\infty}\mathrm{LP}_d^{1/d} = \sqrt{\frac{e}{2\pi}} = 0.657744\ldots, \qquad \Delta_d \le 2^{-(0.604400\ldots + o(1))d}, \qquad \lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{A_\pm(d)}{\sqrt d} = \frac1\pi.$$ One constant threads all three: the sign radius $\sqrt d/\pi$, squeezed between the strip obstruction (§7–8) and the two-shell construction (§9–11).

def g(r):
    return r*r*mp.e**(-mp.pi*r*r)/(1 + r*r)

def limit_eps(eps):
    a0, big_a = eps*eps, mp.log(1/eps)
    u0 = 1 + eps/4

14. Scope — what is proved, what is missed

What you now own. Reading from §2 to §12 you have complete proofs, from the graduate core plus four precisely-stated imports (gamma reflection, the Stirling package, the half-plane Poisson bound, and — only for Appendix A — an extremizer-existence theorem), of: the Cohn–Elkies certificate theorem; the exact asymptotic rate $\mathrm{LP}_d^{1/d} \to \sqrt{e/2\pi}$; the packing exponent record $\Delta_d \le 2^{-(0.6044\ldots+o(1))d}$; and the sign-uncertainty asymptotics $A_\pm(d) \sim \sqrt d/\pi$. You can, with the tools installed here, re-derive the $d=1$ theory by hand, run the construction numerically in any dimension, and read the original chapter (and [CE03], [CG19], [AJCHLT20]) fluently.

What the theorem does not say — each limitation an honest signpost:

  • It does not find better packings. Both theorems live on the upper-bound side. The best known packings still have density $\approx 2^{-d}$ (Proposition B plus polynomial-factor refinements), and the true exponent of $\Delta_d$ remains unknown anywhere in $[\,0.6044\ldots,\ 1\,]$ — the blue interval of Fig. 5 is untouched.
  • It closes a method, not the problem. The exact rate is a ceiling on the Cohn–Elkies LP itself: improving the sphere-packing exponent now provably requires strictly stronger machinery — e.g. three-point (semidefinite) bounds, whose high-dimensional exponent is an open problem. Post-1978 refinements within the two-point framework ([CZ14, SZ24, Z24]) improved only lower-order factors; this chapter explains why that was forced.
  • Asymptotic, not effective in low dimensions. The statement is $d \to \infty$ with $\epsilon$ fixed first. Our own numerics make the cost vivid: the shell construction's constants scale like $e^{1/8\epsilon^2}$, so at $\epsilon = 0.1$ the asymptotic regime near the target contour opens only around $d \sim 10^6$ (Fig. 30's caption). Nothing here competes with the exact magic functions at $d = 8, 24$, where $\mathrm{LP}_d = \Delta_d$ exactly.
  • The two uncertainty constants are asymptotically equal but never equal. Appendix A proves $A_+(d) \lt A_-(d)$ for every $d$ — the $o(\sqrt d)$ gap is real and its true size is open.
  • The physics connection is one-directional here. The modular bootstrap ([HMR19, AJCHLT20]) predicted these constants from conformal-field-theory considerations; the chapter proves the flat-space (sphere-packing) statements. The bootstrap's own spectral-gap questions remain open.
