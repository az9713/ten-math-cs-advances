13. Proof III: the entropy budget and postselection stability (Lemmas 4.5–4.7)

This section proves the two state inequalities (8) of Lemma 3.3 — the quantum heart of the chapter, and the place where the factor $p$ cancels exactly.

13.1 From normalized states to unnormalized reveal costs

The targets are posterior averages of normalized-state distances. On every positive-posterior branch, Bayes' rule gives the exact posterior weight from the ingredients of §11.2: $$\mathcal{Q}(i,t,z,x,y) \;=\; \mathcal{P}_0(i,t,x,y)\;\mathbf{1}_{W_D}(t,z)\;\frac{\|\phi_{r,x,y}\|^2}{p}, \tag{29}$$ since given the questions and history the word $Z=z$ appears with probability $p_r(x,y) = \|\phi_{r,x,y}\|^2$ (equation (4)), the acceptance $W_D$ is decided by $(t,z)$, and conditioning divides by $p = \mathcal{P}(W_D)$. Now apply normalization stability (Proposition C) with $u = \phi_{r,x,y}$ and $v = \phi^B_{r,y}$: $$\big\|\Psi_{r,x,y}-\Psi^B_{r,y}\big\|^2 \;\le\; \frac{4\,\|\phi_{r,x,y}-\phi^B_{r,y}\|^2}{\|\phi_{r,x,y}\|^2}.$$ The denominator $\|\phi_{r,x,y}\|^2$ is exactly the factor carried by the posterior weight (29) — so in the posterior expectation the two cancel branchwise, leaving the prior-weighted, unnormalized quantity $$I_A \;:=\; \mathbb{E}_{(i,t,x,y)\sim\mathcal{P}_0}\ \sum_{z:\ \mathbf{1}_{W_D}(t,z)=1} \big\|\phi_{r,x,y}-\phi^B_{r,y}\big\|^2 = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}_0}\sum_{z\ \mathrm{accepted}}\Big\|\big(\Gamma(H_{r,x})-\Gamma(\bar H_{r,y})\big)\otimes\Gamma(K_{r,y})\,|\psi\rangle\Big\|^2 ,$$ and its mirror $$I_B \;:=\; \mathbb{E}_{(i,t,x,y)\sim\mathcal{P}_0}\ \sum_{z:\ \mathbf{1}_{W_D}(t,z)=1} \big\|\phi_{r,x,y}-\phi^A_{r,x}\big\|^2 ,$$ with the bookkeeping $$\mathbb{E}_{(i,r,x,y)\sim\mathcal{Q}}\big\|\Psi_{r,x,y}-\Psi^B_{r,y}\big\|^2 \;\le\; \frac4p\,I_A, \qquad \mathbb{E}_{(i,r,x,y)\sim\mathcal{Q}}\big\|\Psi_{r,x,y}-\Psi^A_{r,x}\big\|^2 \;\le\; \frac4p\,I_B. \tag{30}$$ Naming: $I_A$ is the Alice-reveal cost — the operator that changes in it is Alice's effect ($H_{r,x}$ versus its average $\bar H_{r,y}$), which by Lemma 4.1 is the live increment of the Alice-reveal martingale $(F_j)$; symmetrically $I_B$ pairs with the Bob-reveal martingale $(G_j)$. It remains to show $I_A, I_B \le 2p(\tau+s)/m$: note the factor $p$ in the bound, waiting for the $1/p$ of (30).

13.2 The accepted-word entropy budget (Lemma 4.5)

Lemma 4.5 (accepted-word entropy budget). For any background information $U$ containing $X_D, Y_D$, let $$p_W(U) = \mathcal{P}(W_D\mid U), \qquad p_z(U) = \mathcal{P}(Z=z\mid U).$$ Then the accepted words satisfy $$\sum_{z:\ \mathbf{1}_{W_D}(U,z)=1} H_1\big(p_z(U)\big) \;\le\; H_1\big(p_W(U)\big) + p_W(U)\,s, \tag{31}$$ and consequently $$\mathbb{E}_{U\sim\mathcal{P}_0}\ \sum_{z:\ \mathbf{1}_{W_D}(U,z)=1} H_1\big(p_z(U)\big) \;\le\; p\,(\tau+s). \tag{32}$$
(Chapter's Appendix A.5.) Since $U$ contains the core questions, acceptance of a word $z$ is determined by $(U,z)$, and the accepted words have total conditional probability $p_W(U)$ and number at most $|A^D\times B^D| = e^{s}$. The counting form of the log-sum inequality (§4.3) applied to their probabilities gives (31): $\sum H_1(p_z) \le H_1(\sum p_z) + (\sum p_z)\log(\text{count})$. For (32), average (31) over $U\sim\mathcal{P}_0$: the second term averages to $\mathbb{E}[p_W(U)]\,s = p\,s$, and for the first, concavity of $H_1$ with Jensen gives $$\mathbb{E}_U\,H_1\big(p_W(U)\big) \;\le\; H_1\big(\mathbb{E}_U\,p_W(U)\big) = H_1(p) = p\log(1/p) = p\,\tau. \qquad $$ ∎

Pause on (32), because it is the postselection stability. The total entropy budget of all accepted branches is not merely finite — it is proportional to $p$. Rare acceptance events have proportionally small budgets: $H_1(p) = p\log(1/p)$, not $\log(1/p)$. The classical intuition "conditioning on a rare event costs $\log(1/p)$ bits" enters only after normalization; while everything stays unnormalized, the cost is $p$ times that. The proof keeps everything unnormalized until the final division by $p$ in (30) — where the two factors annihilate.

13.3 Probability-weighted quantum alignment (Lemma 4.6)

Lemma 4.6 (probability-weighted quantum alignment). $\;I_A,\ I_B \;\le\; \dfrac{2\,p\,(\tau+s)}{m}$.

Consider $I_A$ and the Alice-reveal reverse construction of Lemma 4.1. Fix a partition $M = L_X\sqcup L_Y^+$ with $N = |L_Y^+|\gt0$, the orders, the cut $k_X$, and the background $U$ of (21) (which contains $X_D, Y_D$ as required); fix also an answer word $z$. Conditional on $U$, the word's prior probability is $p_z(U) = \mathcal{P}(Z=z\mid U) = \langle\psi|F_0\otimes K|\psi\rangle$ with $F_0, K$ from (22) — the $j=0$ prediction and the frozen Bob effect. The cut $k_Y$ is uniform on $\{0,\dots,N-1\}$ and independent, so Lemma 4.4 (conditional version, $N$ steps, $p_0 = p_z(U)$) bounds the live increment: $$\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\|\big(\Gamma(F_{k_Y+1})-\Gamma(F_{k_Y})\big)\otimes\Gamma(K)\,|\psi\rangle\big\|^2 \;\Big|\; U\Big] \;\le\; \frac{H_1\big(p_z(U)\big)}{N}.$$ By the boundary identities (23), the vector inside is exactly $\phi_{r,X_i,Y_i}-\phi^B_{r,Y_i}$ — the summand of $I_A$. Sum over the accepted words $z$ (acceptance is $U$-measurable) and average over the residual randomness at fixed partition; by the budget (32), conditioned on the partition (legitimate, since the public randomness is independent of the game), $$\mathbb{E}_U \sum_{z\ \mathrm{accepted}} \frac{H_1(p_z(U))}{N} \;\le\; \frac{p\,(\tau+s)}{N}.$$

Finally average over the size-biased partitions (Fig. 28): $$I_A \;\le\; \sum_{\substack{L_X\sqcup L_Y^+ = M\\ |L_Y^+| \gt 0}} \underbrace{2^{-m}\,\frac{2N}{m}}_{\text{partition weight}}\ \cdot\ \frac{p(\tau+s)}{N} \;=\; \frac{2\,p(\tau+s)}{m}\sum_{\substack{L_X\sqcup L_Y^+ = M\\ |L_Y^+|\gt0}} 2^{-m} \;\le\; \frac{2\,p(\tau+s)}{m},$$ because the block size $N$ cancels between the size-biased weight and the uniform-step bound, and the remaining sum of fair-partition weights is $1 - 2^{-m}\le1$ (machine check c16). For $I_B$, run the mirror Bob-reveal martingale $(G_j)$ with Alice's effect $H_{r,X_i}$ frozen; its partition weight is $2^{-m}\,2|L_X^+|/m$, and the same cancellation gives the same bound. ∎

13.4 The exact cancellation (Lemma 4.7)

Lemma 4.7 (postselection-stable normalized alignment). $$\mathbb{E}_{(i,r,x,y)\sim\mathcal{Q}}\big\|\Psi_{r,x,y}-\Psi^A_{r,x}\big\|^2 \;\le\; 8\eta, \qquad \mathbb{E}_{(i,r,x,y)\sim\mathcal{Q}}\big\|\Psi_{r,x,y}-\Psi^B_{r,y}\big\|^2 \;\le\; 8\eta.$$
On tuples of zero posterior probability, complete any undefined normalized state by a fixed arbitrary unit vector; such branches contribute nothing to a posterior expectation. Combining the reduction (30) with Lemma 4.6: $$\frac4p\,I_A,\ \frac4p\,I_B \;\le\; \frac4p\cdot\frac{2p(\tau+s)}{m} \;=\; \frac{8(\tau+s)}{m} \;=\; 8\eta.$$ The factor $p$ retained by the unnormalized entropy budget cancels the $1/p$ of the posterior weight — no inverse-postselection loss (Fig. 29). This proves both state inequalities (8) of Lemma 3.3. ∎

The quantum half of Lemma 3.3 is done. What remains is its classical half — the total-variation bounds $d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{J}_A), d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{J}_B)\le\kappa$ — which is a purely classical argument about the history distributions, using the same reveal martingale in its classical incarnation.
