11. Proof of the central lemma, I: reveal martingales (Lemma 4.1)

The remaining four sections prove Lemma 3.3, following the chapter's Section 4. Throughout them, fix the repeated-game strategy and the core $D\subsetneq[n]$ with $p = \mathcal{P}(W_D)\gt0$, and abbreviate $$M = [n]\setminus D, \qquad m = |M|, \qquad \tau = \log(1/p), \qquad s = |D|\log(|A||B|), \qquad \eta = \frac{\tau+s}{m}.$$

11.1 What the state difference really is

Start by understanding the quantity $\|\Psi_{r,x,y}-\Psi^A_{r,x}\|$ that (8) bounds. The branches $$\phi_{r,x,y} = \big(\Gamma(H_{r,x})\otimes\Gamma(K_{r,y})\big)|\psi\rangle, \qquad \phi^A_{r,x} = \big(\Gamma(H_{r,x})\otimes\Gamma(\bar K_{r,x})\big)|\psi\rangle$$ differ in exactly one operator: Bob's effect, conditioned on his actual live question $Y_i = y$ in the first, averaged over it in the second ($\bar K_{r,x} = \mathbb{E}_{Y_i\sim\mu(\cdot|x)}K_{r,Y_i}$). So the difference is precisely the change caused by revealing one question, $Y_i$.

A single reveal can be violent — nothing prevents one question from carrying a lot of information about the core answers. The chapter's move: don't study the live reveal in isolation. Imagine keeping Alice's relevant questions fixed and uncovering Bob's remaining questions one at a time, in a random order; somewhere along the way, the live coordinate's turn comes up. After $j$ reveals, the best prediction of Bob's core-answer effect is the conditional expectation $$G_j = \mathbb{E}\big[F^{b_D}_{Y^n}\,\big\vert\, \text{everything fixed at the start},\ \text{first } j \text{ revealed questions}\big],$$ and as $j$ grows these predictions form a Doob martingale under the prior (Fig. 25; §4.4). Immediately before the live step the prediction is the averaged effect $\bar K$; immediately after, the revealed effect $K$. The state difference we care about is therefore one increment of a long martingale, taken at a uniformly random position — and a uniformly random increment can be charged to the total movement budget of the whole path. Making "total movement budget" precise (it will be an operator entropy) is §12's job; this section builds the randomized reveal order and proves its three structural properties.

11.2 The forward reveal construction

Here, finally, is the public randomness $\lambda$ promised in §7.1. Choose $i$ uniformly in $M$; partition $M\setminus\{i\}$ fairly (each element independently, probability $\tfrac12$ each way) into $L_X\sqcup L_Y$; choose independent uniform random orders (permutations) $\pi_{X,-i}$ of $L_X$ and $\pi_{Y,-i}$ of $L_Y$ — the subscript $-i$ records that these orders exclude the live coordinate; and choose independent uniform cuts $$k_X\in\{0,\dots,|L_X|\}, \qquad k_Y\in\{0,\dots,|L_Y|\}.$$ Assemble $$\lambda = (i,\ L_X,\ L_Y,\ \pi_{X,-i},\ \pi_{Y,-i},\ k_X,\ k_Y), \qquad C_X = D\cup L_X\cup \pi_{Y,-i}^{\le k_Y}, \qquad C_Y = D\cup L_Y\cup \pi_{X,-i}^{\le k_X},$$ where $\pi^{\le k}$ denotes the set of the first $k$ elements in the order $\pi$. So Alice's revealed questions are those on $D$, on her side $L_X$ of the partition, and on the first $k_Y$ elements of Bob's side; symmetrically for Bob. This satisfies the coverage condition (2): both core questions revealed, neither live question revealed, and every other coordinate reveals at least one side (its own side of the partition, at minimum). It is a symmetric random-reveal variant of Holenstein's classical dependency-breaking construction; the random orders and cuts exist precisely so that the live question can later be identified with a uniformly random martingale increment.

Write $\mathcal{P}_0$ for the marginal of $\mathcal{P}$ that keeps the public randomness and the questions but discards all answers: $\mathcal{P}_0(\lambda, x^n, y^n) = \mathbb{P}(\lambda)\prod_{j=1}^n\mu(x_j,y_j)$; its induced marginal on $(i, T, X_i, Y_i)$ is denoted $\mathcal{P}_0(i,t,x,y)$. One bookkeeping identity we will need in §13: since $(t,z)$ determines core success, and given $(t,x,y)$ the word $Z = z$ has probability $p_r(x,y) = \|\phi_{r,x,y}\|^2$ by (4), summing over accepted branches recovers the total core-success mass: $$\sum_{i,t,x,y,z}\ \mathcal{P}_0(i,t,x,y)\;\mathbf{1}_{W_D}(t,z)\;p_r(x,y) \;=\; \mathcal{P}(W_D) \;=\; p. $$ This exact factor $p$ is the one that will cancel the posterior's $1/p$.

11.3 The reverse description: the live step is a uniform cut

The forward construction picks $i$ first and the orders afterwards. The proof needs the opposite view: first pick which block of coordinates will be revealed on Bob's side, then realize the live coordinate as a uniformly random position inside that block. The next lemma says the two views produce identically distributed histories, and identifies the martingale's boundary values with the effects of §7–§8. It comes in two mirror-image directions; the "Alice-reveal" direction uncovers Alice's questions one at a time (its martingale tracks Alice's effect, with Bob's effect frozen), and vice versa.

Lemma 4.1 (random live-coordinate martingales). The forward history admits the following equivalent reverse descriptions.

Alice-reveal direction. Sample a partition $M = L_X\sqcup L_Y^+$ with probability $$\mathbb{P}(L_X, L_Y^+) = 2^{-m}\,\frac{2\,|L_Y^+|}{m},$$ a uniform order $\pi_Y$ of $L_Y^+$, and an independent uniform cut $k_Y\in\{0,\dots,|L_Y^+|-1\}$; set $i = \pi_Y[k_Y{+}1]$ (the $(k_Y{+}1)$-st element) and $L_Y = L_Y^+\setminus\{i\}$; then draw $\pi_{X,-i}$ and $k_X$ as in the forward experiment. This reproduces the forward distribution of $\lambda$ exactly. Moreover, with the background $$U = \big(X_D,\ Y_D,\ X_{L_X},\ Y_{L_Y^+},\ Y_{\pi_{X,-i}^{\le k_X}}\big), \tag{21}$$ the effects $$F_j = \mathbb{E}\big[E^{a_D}_{X^n}\,\big\vert\, U,\ X_{\pi_Y^{\le j}}\big] \quad (0\le j\le |L_Y^+|), \qquad K = \mathbb{E}\big[F^{b_D}_{Y^n}\,\big\vert\, U\big] \tag{22}$$ form a prior martingale in $j$ with fixed Bob effect $K$, and at the live cut $$F_{k_Y} = \bar H_{r,Y_i}, \qquad F_{k_Y+1} = H_{r,X_i}, \qquad K = K_{r,Y_i}. \tag{23}$$

Bob-reveal direction (mirror). Sample $M = L_X^+\sqcup L_Y$ with probability $2^{-m}\,2|L_X^+|/m$, a uniform order $\pi_X$ of $L_X^+$ and uniform cut $k_X$, set $i = \pi_X[k_X{+}1]$; with $U = (X_D, Y_D, X_{L_X^+}, Y_{L_Y}, X_{\pi_{Y,-i}^{\le k_Y}})$, the effects $G_j = \mathbb{E}\big[F^{b_D}_{Y^n}\mid U, Y_{\pi_X^{\le j}}\big]$ form a prior martingale with fixed Alice effect $H_{r,X_i}$, and $$G_{k_X} = \bar K_{r,X_i}, \qquad G_{k_X+1} = K_{r,Y_i}.$$

(Chapter's Appendix A.3.) Distributional identity. Consider the Alice-reveal direction and write $N = |L_Y^+|$. A forward outcome $(i, L_X, L_Y, \pi_{X,-i}, \pi_{Y,-i}, k_X, k_Y)$ has probability $$\underbrace{\frac1m}_{i}\;\underbrace{2^{-(m-1)}}_{\text{fair partition of } M\setminus\{i\}}\;\underbrace{\frac{1}{|L_X|!}}_{\pi_{X,-i}}\;\underbrace{\frac{1}{(N-1)!}}_{\pi_{Y,-i}}\;\underbrace{\frac{1}{|L_X|+1}}_{k_X}\;\underbrace{\frac1N}_{k_Y} \;=\; \frac{2^{1-m}}{m\,|L_X|!\,(|L_X|+1)\,(N-1)!\,N},$$ using $|L_Y| = N-1$, so $\pi_{Y,-i}$ has $(N-1)!$ values and $k_Y$ ranges over $\{0,\dots,N-1\}$ — $N$ values. In the reverse experiment, the same outcome arises from: the partition $(L_X, L_Y^+)$ with $L_Y^+ = L_Y\cup\{i\}$, probability $2^{-m}\,2N/m$; the unique order $\pi_Y$ of $L_Y^+$ that puts $i$ in position $k_Y{+}1$ and restricts to $\pi_{Y,-i}$ on the rest, probability $1/N!$; the cut $k_Y$, probability $1/N$; and $(\pi_{X,-i}, k_X)$ as forward. Total: $$2^{-m}\,\frac{2N}{m}\cdot\frac{1}{N!}\cdot\frac1N\cdot\frac{1}{|L_X|!}\cdot\frac{1}{|L_X|+1} = \frac{2^{1-m}}{m\,|L_X|!\,(|L_X|+1)\,(N-1)!\,N}. $$ The two agree outcome by outcome. (Machine checks c14, c14b: exact enumeration at $m=4$, all $480$ realized histories, zero deviation.) Note also that the reverse partition weights are a probability distribution: summing $2^{-m}\,2N/m$ over all $2^m$ partitions is $\mathbb{E}[2N/m]$ under fair coin assignment, and $\mathbb{E}[N] = m/2$.

Martingale property. Fix the partition, the orders, $k_X$, and hence the background $U$ of (21). Under the prior, conditionally on $U$, the still-unrevealed Alice questions $X_j$ for $j\in L_Y^+$ are independent with laws $\mu(\cdot\mid Y_j)$ — their partners $Y_j$ all belong to $Y_{L_Y^+}\subseteq U$, and distinct coordinates are independent (this is the factorization mechanism of §7.2 again). The tower property of conditional expectation (§4.4) applied to the growing conditioning chain $\{U\}\subseteq\{U, X_{\pi_Y^{\le1}}\}\subseteq\cdots$ gives $\mathbb{E}[F_{j+1}\mid U, X_{\pi_Y^{\le j}}] = F_j$: a martingale. The Bob effect $K$ is fixed along the chain because $F^{b_D}_{Y^n}$ depends only on Bob's questions, all of which are either in $U$ or averaged in its definition — revealing Alice's questions never changes it.

Boundary values (23). At $j = k_Y$: the conditioning $(U, X_{\pi_Y^{\le k_Y}})$ carries exactly the question content of $(T, Y_i)$ — Alice's questions on $C_X = D\cup L_X\cup\pi_Y^{\le k_Y}$, Bob's on $C_Y\cup\{i\}$ — and averaging $E^{a_D}_{X^n}$ over the remaining Alice questions (including $X_i\sim\mu(\cdot\mid Y_i)$) yields $\mathbb{E}_{X_i}[H_{r,X_i}\mid Y_i] = \bar H_{r,Y_i}$ by the tower property. At $j = k_Y{+}1$: the reveal adds exactly $X_{\pi_Y[k_Y+1]} = X_i$, giving $H_{r,X_i}$ by definition. And $K = \mathbb{E}[F^{b_D}_{Y^n}\mid U]$ averages Bob's effect over his unrevealed questions given $(T, Y_i)$-equivalent data, which is $K_{r,Y_i}$. Interchanging the roles of Alice and Bob gives the mirror statements. ∎

The lemma delivers exactly the three features the rest of the proof consumes: (a) the live reveal is a uniform step of an $N$-step martingale (the cut $k_Y$ is uniform and independent of the path), (b) the block containing the live coordinate is chosen with the size bias $2N/m$ — a factor that will cancel against the $1/N$ of the uniform step in §13 — and (c) the martingale's values at the cut are exactly the averaged and revealed effects $\bar H, H$ (resp. $\bar K, K$) whose purified difference is the state error. What is still missing is a bound on the increments of a purified martingale; that is the next section's entropy machine.
