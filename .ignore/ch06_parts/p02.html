2. Nonlocal games and the entangled value: CHSH worked in full

This section makes Fig. 1 into mathematics (following the chapter's Section 2), then plays one game completely — CHSH — because it is the smallest example that simultaneously teaches what a strategy is, what entanglement buys, and what a "value" computation looks like on both sides of the classical/quantum divide.

2.1 The definition

Definition 2.1 (finite one-round two-player entangled game). A game is a tuple $$G = (X, Y, A, B, \mu, V), \qquad V : X\times Y\times A\times B \to \{0,1\},$$ where the question sets $X, Y$ and answer alphabets $A, B$ are finite, $A, B$ are nonempty, and $\mu$ is an arbitrary probability distribution on $X\times Y$. A finite-dimensional tensor-product strategy consists of finite Hilbert spaces $\mathcal{H}_A, \mathcal{H}_B$, a unit vector $\psi \in \mathcal{H}_A\otimes\mathcal{H}_B$, and for each question a local POVM: $$E^a_x \succeq 0,\;\; \sum_{a\in A} E^a_x = I_{\mathcal{H}_A} \;\;(x \in X), \qquad F^b_y \succeq 0,\;\; \sum_{b\in B} F^b_y = I_{\mathcal{H}_B} \;\;(y \in Y).$$ Its winning probability is $$\omega^*(G;\psi,E,F) \;=\; \sum_{x,y} \mu(x,y) \sum_{a,b} V(x,y,a,b)\, \langle\psi|\, E^a_x\otimes F^b_y\, |\psi\rangle ,$$ and the entangled value is the supremum over all such strategies, with no bound on the local dimensions: $$\omega^*(G) \;=\; \sup_{\mathcal{H}_A,\mathcal{H}_B,\,\psi,\,E,\,F}\; \omega^*(G;\psi,E,F).$$ No attaining strategy is assumed to exist — the supremum need not be achieved.

Three bookkeeping conventions from the chapter, recorded once. The question marginals are $\mu_X(x) = \sum_y \mu(x,y)$ and $\mu_Y(y) = \sum_x \mu(x,y)$. Questions with zero marginal can be deleted without changing any value, so conditionals $$\mu(x\mid y) = \frac{\mu(x,y)}{\mu_Y(y)}, \qquad \mu(y\mid x) = \frac{\mu(x,y)}{\mu_X(x)}$$ are used only where the denominators are positive. The support $E_\mu = \{(x,y): \mu(x,y) \gt 0\}$ need not be a product set and need not be connected — and, remarkably, nothing in the whole proof will care.

The classical value $\omega(G)$ is the same supremum restricted to strategies without entanglement: the players share a random variable $\lambda$ (agreed beforehand) and answer by functions $a = f_\lambda(x)$, $b = g_\lambda(y)$. It is the special case of Definition 2.1 in which all effects commute (diagonal matrices), so $\omega(G) \le \omega^*(G)$ always.

Proposition D (deterministic strategies suffice classically). $$\omega(G) \;=\; \max_{f: X\to A,\; g: Y\to B}\; \sum_{x,y}\mu(x,y)\, V(x,y,f(x),g(y)).$$
A shared-randomness strategy wins with probability $\mathbb{E}_\lambda[w(\lambda)]$ where $w(\lambda)$ is the winning probability of the deterministic pair $(f_\lambda, g_\lambda)$. An average never exceeds its maximum, so some deterministic pair does at least as well; conversely a deterministic pair is itself a legal strategy. The maximum exists because there are finitely many pairs. ∎

2.2 The CHSH game, both values computed exactly

Toy (CHSH). Questions and answers are single bits: $X = Y = A = B = \{0,1\}$, the questions are independent fair coins ($\mu$ uniform on $\{0,1\}^2$), and the players win iff $$a \oplus b \;=\; x\wedge y,$$ i.e. the answers must agree unless both questions are $1$, in which case they must disagree (Fig. 5, left). We will show $$\omega(\mathrm{CHSH}) = \tfrac34, \qquad \omega^*(\mathrm{CHSH}) = \cos^2(\pi/8) = \tfrac12 + \tfrac{\sqrt2}{4} \approx 0.8536.$$
Proposition E (classical value). $\omega(\mathrm{CHSH}) = 3/4$.
By Proposition D it suffices to consider deterministic $f, g$. The four win conditions, one per question pair, are $$f(0)\oplus g(0) = 0,\quad f(0)\oplus g(1) = 0,\quad f(1)\oplus g(0) = 0,\quad f(1)\oplus g(1) = 1 .$$ XOR-ing all four equations: every value $f(x), g(y)$ appears exactly twice on the left, so the left side XORs to $0$, while the right side XORs to $1$. Hence the four conditions are never simultaneously satisfiable, so at most $3$ of the $4$ equally likely question pairs can be won: $\omega \le 3/4$. Constant answers $f \equiv g \equiv 0$ win exactly the three pairs with $x\wedge y = 0$. (Machine check c01 in §15 confirms the maximum over all $16$ deterministic pairs.) ∎
Proposition F (entangled players reach $\cos^2(\pi/8)$). There is a tensor-product strategy for CHSH with winning probability exactly $\cos^2(\pi/8)$.
Share $|\Phi^+\rangle$ from §1.3, and measure in rotated real bases: Alice uses angle $\theta_A(0) = 0$ or $\theta_A(1) = \pi/4$; Bob uses $\theta_B(0) = \pi/8$ or $\theta_B(1) = -\pi/8$ (Fig. 5, right). By the identity $\mathbb{P}(a = b) = \cos^2(\theta_A - \theta_B)$ proved in §1.3, on the three question pairs requiring agreement the relative angle is $\pm\pi/8$, giving success $\cos^2(\pi/8)$; on the pair $x = y = 1$, requiring disagreement, the relative angle is $\pi/4 - (-\pi/8) = 3\pi/8$, giving success $1 - \cos^2(3\pi/8) = \cos^2(\pi/8)$ again. Every question pair is won with probability exactly $\cos^2(\pi/8)$, hence so is the average. (Machine check c02.) ∎

Could a cleverer entangled strategy do better than $\cos^2(\pi/8)$? No — and the proof, due to Tsirelson, is two lines of operator algebra once the right object is written down. For a two-outcome POVM $\{E^0, E^1\}$ define its observable $M = E^0 - E^1$, a Hermitian operator with $-I \preceq M \preceq I$ (a Hermitian contraction); when the POVM is projective, $M^2 = I$ (call such $M$ a symmetry). Writing $A_x = E^0_x - E^1_x$ and $B_y = F^0_y - F^1_y$, the correlator $\mathbb{E}[(-1)^{a\oplus b}\mid x,y] = \langle\psi|A_x\otimes B_y|\psi\rangle$, and since $\mathbb{P}(a\oplus b = 0 \mid x,y) = \tfrac12(1 + \mathbb{E}[(-1)^{a\oplus b}])$, the CHSH winning probability of any strategy is $$\omega^*(\mathrm{CHSH};\psi,E,F) \;=\; \frac12 + \frac{\langle\psi|\,\mathcal{B}\,|\psi\rangle}{8}, \qquad \mathcal{B} = A_0\otimes B_0 + A_0\otimes B_1 + A_1\otimes B_0 - A_1\otimes B_1 .$$

Proposition G (Tsirelson's bound). For all Hermitian contractions $A_0, A_1$ on $\mathcal{H}_A$ and $B_0, B_1$ on $\mathcal{H}_B$, the Bell operator above satisfies $\|\mathcal{B}\| \le 2\sqrt2$. Consequently $\omega^*(\mathrm{CHSH}) \le \tfrac12 + \tfrac{2\sqrt2}{8} = \cos^2(\pi/8)$, and with Proposition F, $\omega^*(\mathrm{CHSH}) = \cos^2(\pi/8)$.

First reduce to symmetries. $\langle\psi|\mathcal{B}|\psi\rangle$ is affine in each of the four observables separately, and the set of Hermitian contractions is convex and compact, so the supremum is attained at operators that are extreme points of that set. An extreme point must have all eigenvalues in $\{-1, +1\}$: if some eigenvalue $\sigma \in (-1,1)$ with unit eigenvector $v$, then $M \pm (1-|\sigma|)|v\rangle\langle v|$ are distinct Hermitian contractions averaging to $M$. So we may assume $A_x^2 = I$, $B_y^2 = I$. Now group $\mathcal{B} = A_0\otimes(B_0 + B_1) + A_1\otimes(B_0 - B_1)$ and square: $$\mathcal{B}^2 = A_0^2\otimes(B_0+B_1)^2 + A_1^2\otimes(B_0-B_1)^2 + A_0A_1\otimes(B_0+B_1)(B_0-B_1) + A_1A_0\otimes(B_0-B_1)(B_0+B_1).$$ Using $B_y^2 = I$: $(B_0+B_1)^2 + (B_0-B_1)^2 = 4I$, $(B_0+B_1)(B_0-B_1) = -[B_0,B_1]$ and $(B_0-B_1)(B_0+B_1) = [B_0,B_1]$, where $[S,T] = ST - TS$. Hence $$\mathcal{B}^2 \;=\; 4\, I\otimes I \;-\; [A_0,A_1]\otimes[B_0,B_1].$$ Since $\|[S,T]\| \le 2\|S\|\|T\| \le 2$ for contractions, $\|\mathcal{B}^2\| \le 4 + 4 = 8$ and $\|\mathcal{B}\| = \|\mathcal{B}^2\|^{1/2} \le 2\sqrt2$ (the last equality because $\mathcal{B}$ is Hermitian, so $\|\mathcal{B}\|^2 = \|\mathcal{B}^2\|$ by Proposition A). Finally $\cos^2(\pi/8) = \tfrac{1 + \cos(\pi/4)}{2} = \tfrac12 + \tfrac{\sqrt2}{4}$. (Machine checks c03a, c03b: the norm is exactly $2\sqrt2$ at the optimal angles, and $360$ random observable quadruples never exceed it.) ∎

The punchline of the computation is worth internalizing before we move on, because the whole chapter is about what happens when such a game is repeated. Entanglement is a genuine resource: it moved the value from $0.750$ to $0.854$. But it is a bounded resource: the commutator term in $\mathcal{B}^2$ is the exact algebraic measure of how much, and it caps the advantage strictly below $1$. Fig. 6 shows the entangled value of the whole one-parameter family of rotation strategies; the optimum sits at $\beta = 22.5^\circ$, comfortably above the classical line, below the ceiling.

A remark on models, needed for honesty in §16: Definition 2.1 is the tensor-product model of locality. There is a more permissive commuting-operator model (one shared Hilbert space, Alice's and Bob's operators merely commuting); in infinite dimension the two models genuinely differ ($\mathrm{MIP}^* = \mathrm{RE}$ implies as much). Everything in this chapter concerns the tensor-product value with finite-dimensional strategies, as does the open question it answers.
