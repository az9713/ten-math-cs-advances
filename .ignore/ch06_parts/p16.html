16. Scope — what is proved, what is missed

An honest ledger of what Theorem 1.1 does and does not establish.

Proved, in full strength. For every finite two-player one-round game — arbitrary predicate $V$, arbitrary correlated question distribution $\mu$, arbitrary finite answer alphabets — the entangled value of the unchanged repeated game decays exponentially, at a rate depending only on $\varepsilon$ and $\log(|A||B|)$, uniformly over games and over the entanglement dimension of the strategies. The proof is effective: the constant is the explicit $c_{\mathrm{qs}} = \tfrac18(4B_{\mathrm{qs}})^{-12}$ with $B_{\mathrm{qs}}$ from (11), once a concrete $K_{\mathrm{qs}}$ from Import 1 is fixed. Within this document, everything except Import 1 is proved from linear algebra and finite probability.

The exponent is certainly not optimal. Classically the same architecture yields $\varepsilon^3$ (Holenstein); here the embezzlement import's $1/12$-power loss inflates the exponent to $13$. The chapter itself flags this: the exponent "arises from the quantitative loss in a standard quantum correlated-sampling lemma" and is not claimed sharp. Any improvement in the exponent of Import 1 propagates mechanically through (11), (16), and §10. In the other direction, Raz's counterexample already rules out a linear-in-$\varepsilon$ rate classically, so some polynomial loss is necessary; where between $1$ and $13$ the truth lies is open.

The constants are galactic. Fig. 23 was blunt: even at the illustrative floor $K_{\mathrm{qs}} = 1$, the guaranteed rate $\gamma$ at $\varepsilon = 0.3$ is $\sim10^{-31}$ per copy. Theorem 1.1 is a structural fact about the shape of decay — it says nothing usable about, say, $n = 100$ copies of a real Bell test. For practical soundness amplification one still reaches for anchored repetition (Bavarian–Vidick–Yuen), whose modified-game bounds have humane constants.

Model boundaries. Three are built into Definition 2.1. (i) Two players, one round: the dependency-breaking reveal argument is intrinsically two-sided (the coverage condition (2) partitions coordinates between exactly two players); multiplayer quantum parallel repetition remains open in general. (ii) Tensor-product strategies, finite dimension: the theorem bounds the finite-dimensional tensor-product value; the commuting-operator value of infinite-dimensional strategies (the other natural quantum model, strictly larger in general by the resolution of Tsirelson's problem) is not addressed. (iii) Fixed game repeated independently: nothing is claimed about repeating with correlated question tuples across coordinates.

Where this sits in the larger program. Parallel repetition is the standard soundness-amplification tool for multiprover proof systems, and the entangled-game setting is exactly the world of $\mathrm{MIP}^*$ — whose equality with $\mathrm{RE}$ made entangled games a lens on undecidability. Amplification lemmas of this kind slot into that machinery (recent examples in the chapter's bibliography: succinct perfect zero-knowledge for $\mathrm{MIP}^*$, and the co-r.e. characterization of the commuting-operator class $\mathrm{MIP}^{co}$). The specific advance here — postselection-stable quantum conditioning — is plausibly portable: any argument that needs players to re-create a measured-and-conditioned quantum state now has a template that does not pay $1/p$.
