2. What is proved, and the map of both proofs

Here are the chapter's three theorems, verbatim in content. (The chapter's own numbering — Theorems 1.1–1.3, Lemmas 4.1–10.2, Propositions 4.3, 5.1, 7.1, Corollary 5.2 — is preserved throughout this document so you can cross-read with the paper.)

Theorem 1.1 (circuits). For every $n \ge 2^{16}$, $$C(\operatorname{per}_n) \;\ge\; \frac{n^2}{144}\,\bigl(\log_2\log_2 n \;-\; 3\bigr). \tag{1.1}$$ In particular, $C(\operatorname{per}_n) = \Omega(n^2 \log\log n)$ and $C(\operatorname{per}_n)/n^2 \longrightarrow \infty$.
Theorem 1.2 (division-free formulas). If $n \ge 32$ and a division-free arithmetic formula $\Phi$ computes $\operatorname{per}_n$, then $$L_{\mathrm{var}}(\Phi) \;\ge\; \frac{n^4}{128\,\log_2 n}, \qquad G(\Phi) \;\ge\; \frac{n^4}{256\,\log_2 n}.$$ The variable-leaf bound also holds for the total numbers of leaves and vertices.
Theorem 1.3 (formulas with valid division). If $n \ge 32$ and a valid arithmetic formula $\Phi$ with division computes $\operatorname{per}_n$, then $$L_{\mathrm{var}}(\Phi) \;\ge\; \frac{n^4}{192\,\log_2 n}, \qquad G(\Phi) \;\ge\; \frac{n^4}{384\,\log_2 n}.$$ Again, the variable-leaf bound holds for the total numbers of leaves and vertices.

In terms of the $N = n^2$ input variables: the circuit bound is $\Omega(N \log\log N)$, and both formula bounds are $\Omega(N^2/\log N)$. Fig. 3 plots all of them, computed exactly from the displayed constants, against the trivial floor.

Both theorems are proved by the same two-phase pattern: (i) a general-purpose bridge from computation to mathematics (geometry for circuits, field theory for formulas) that holds for any polynomial, followed by (ii) a permanent-specific construction showing the permanent maximizes the mathematical quantity the bridge measures. 2.1 Why each spine needs the permanent (a preview of §13)

It is worth flagging now, so you can watch for it, where each proof consumes a property the determinant lacks.

  • Circuit spine. The bridge (Prop 4.3) needs an affine specialization of the target polynomial whose critical locus — the common zero set of all first partial derivatives — has codimension $\Theta(n^2)$. Every affine specialization of $\det_n$ has critical-locus codimension at most $n - 1$ (proved in §13, using an imported theorem of Alper–Bogart–Velasco), so the bridge can never yield more than $O(n\log n)$ for the determinant. For the permanent, §7–§8 build a specialization achieving $\Theta(n^2)$.
  • Formula spine. The bridge (Lem 8.1) needs small variable groups carrying many algebraically independent coefficients. For the determinant, the Schur complement compresses all the coefficients of a $k$-entry group into $k^2 + 1$ parameters (proved in §13) — log-size groups carry only $O(\log^2 n)$ bits of independence. The permanent has no Schur complement, and Lem 9.1 extracts $\Omega(n^2)$ independence from a group of size $2\lceil \log_2 n\rceil$.
