9. Choosing the degree: proof of Theorem 1.1

All circuit machinery is now on the table. What remains is bookkeeping: pick the block height $t$, block count $b$, and degree $d$ so that (i) the specialization of Lemma 6.1 fits inside $\operatorname{per}_n$, (ii) it retains $\Theta(n^2)$ variables, and (iii) Corollary 5.2's critical-locus bound stays below half the variable count, leaving room for a slice of dimension $k = \Theta(n^2)$.

Definition (the parameter block (7.1)). Given $n$ and a degree $d \ge 3$, set $$t = 4d, \qquad b = \Bigl\lfloor \frac{n}{2t}\Bigr\rfloor, \qquad r = bt, \qquad s = n - r + d, \qquad m = rs, \qquad k = \Bigl\lfloor \frac{m}{4}\Bigr\rfloor.$$ Here $t$ is the rows per block, $b$ the number of blocks, $r = bt$ the total variable rows ($\approx n/2$), $s$ the variable columns chosen so that $B(X)$ is exactly $n \times n$ (indeed $r + s - d = n$), $m = rs$ the number of variable entries, and $k$ the slice dimension fed to Proposition 4.3.
Proposition 7.1. Let $n, d$ be positive integers, define $t,b,r,s,m,k$ by (7.1), and suppose $$d \ge 3, \qquad n \ge 6t, \qquad 4(2^t - 1) \le ts. \tag{7.2}$$ Then every arithmetic circuit of size $L$ computing $\operatorname{per}_n$ satisfies $$L \;\ge\; \frac{n^2 \log_2 (d-1)}{144}. \tag{7.3}$$

First the sanity of the construction. $n \ge 6t$ gives $b \ge \lfloor 3 \rfloor \ge 1$ and, as computed below, $s \ge n/2 \ge 3t \ge d$; also $d \le t$. So $3 \le d \le \min\{t,s\}$, and Lemma 6.1 applies: with $\rho = 2^t - 1$, $$P(X) = \sum_{h=1}^{b}\lambda_h M_{t,s,d}\bigl(X_{R_h,[s]}\bigr)$$ is a nonzero scalar multiple of an affine specialization of $\operatorname{per}_{r+s-d} = \operatorname{per}_n$, homogeneous of degree $d$ in the $m = rs$ variables. All $\lambda_h$ are nonzero and the blocks are variable-disjoint, so Corollary 5.2 gives $$\dim\operatorname{Crit}(P) \;\le\; D := b\bigl(\rho + s(d-2)\bigr).$$

Estimate the two summands of $D$ against $m = bts$. The scale condition $4\rho \le ts$ handles the power sums: $$b\rho \le \frac{bts}{4} = \frac{m}{4}.$$ The choice $t = 4d$ handles the column parameters: $$b s (d-2) \lt bsd = \frac{bts}{4} = \frac{m}{4}.$$ Hence $\dim\operatorname{Crit}(P) \le D \lt m/2$. Since $k = \lfloor m/4 \rfloor \le m/4$, we get $k + D \lt m$, so certainly $\dim\operatorname{Crit}(P) \le D \le m - k$, and $1 \le k \lt m$ (see $m \ge 8$ below). Proposition 4.3 applies: $$k\,\log_2(d-1) \;\le\; 3L. \tag{7.4}$$

It remains to express $k$ in terms of $n$. From $b = \lfloor n/2t\rfloor$, $$\frac{n}{2} - t \;\le\; r = t\Bigl\lfloor\frac{n}{2t}\Bigr\rfloor \;\le\; \frac{n}{2}.$$ The hypothesis $n \ge 6t$ gives $n/2 - t \ge n/2 - n/6 = n/3$. Therefore $$\frac{n}{3} \le r \le \frac{n}{2}, \qquad s = n - r + d \ge \frac{n}{2}, \qquad m = rs \ge \frac{n^2}{6}.$$ In particular $m \ge \tfrac{36 t^2}{6} = 6t^2 \ge 8$, so the floor in $k$ costs at most a factor two: $$k = \Bigl\lfloor \frac{m}{4}\Bigr\rfloor \ge \frac{m}{8} \ge \frac{n^2}{48},$$ i.e. $n^2 \le 48k$. Substituting into (7.4): $$L \ge \frac{k \log_2 (d-1)}{3} \ge \frac{n^2 \log_2(d-1)}{144}.$$ ∎

Finally, let the degree grow with $n$. The tension: larger $d$ strengthens $\log_2(d-1)$, but $t = 4d$ makes the power-sum count $2^t = 2^{4d}$ explode, and the scale condition $4(2^t - 1) \le ts$ caps $2^t$ at roughly $n$. So $d$ can be at most about $\tfrac14 \log_2 n$ — whence, inescapably, a $\log\log$ bound.

Theorem 1.1 (restated). For every $n \ge 2^{16}$, $\;C(\operatorname{per}_n) \ge \dfrac{n^2}{144}\bigl(\log_2\log_2 n - 3\bigr)$.

Put $\ell = \log_2 n \ (\ge 16)$, $d = \lfloor \ell/4 \rfloor$, $t = 4d$. Then $d \ge 4 \ge 3$ and $t \le \ell$. We check the three hypotheses (7.2).

  • $n \ge 6t$: since $t \le \log_2 n$ and $n \ge 2^{16}$, indeed $6\log_2 n \le n$ (at $n = 2^{16}$: $96 \le 65536$; the gap only widens).
  • $4(2^t-1) \le ts$: as in Proposition 7.1, $r \le n/2$ so $s \ge n/2$; and $d \ge 4$ gives $ds \ge 4 \cdot n/2 = 2n \ge n$. Meanwhile $2^t \le 2^\ell = n$. Hence $$2^t - 1 \lt n \le ds, \qquad 4(2^t - 1) \le 4\,ds = ts.$$
  • $d \ge 3$: clear.

Proposition 7.1 yields $L \ge n^2\log_2(d-1)/144$. Finally, $\ell \ge 16$ gives $$d - 1 = \Bigl\lfloor\frac{\ell}{4}\Bigr\rfloor - 1 \ge \frac{\ell}{4} - 2 \ge \frac{\ell}{8},$$ (the last step because $\ell/8 \ge 2$), so $\log_2(d-1) \ge \log_2\ell - 3 = \log_2\log_2 n - 3$. Substitution proves (1.1). Since $\log_2\log_2 n - 3 \to \infty$, the bound is superquadratic. ∎

Where each hypothesis bit. The three conditions of (7.2) each earned their keep: $d \ge 3$ made Proposition 5.1's rewriting engine run ($q \ge 2$); $n \ge 6t$ kept a constant fraction of the matrix variable; the scale condition $4(2^t-1) \le ts$ is the exponential wall $2^t \lesssim n$ that pins $d \lesssim \tfrac14\log_2 n$ and limits the whole method to $\log\log$ gains. Any improvement of the critical-locus bound (5.2) in its $2^t$ term would propagate directly into a stronger circuit bound — see the outlook in §14.
