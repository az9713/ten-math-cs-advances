5. Slicing away the critical locus (Lemma 4.2)

Lemma 4.1 wants a square homogeneous map ($\mathbb{C}^k \to \mathbb{C}^k$) whose only zero is the origin. What the permanent will naturally give us is a gradient: $m$ partial derivatives of one polynomial in $m$ variables, which may vanish on a whole positive-dimensional set. This section builds the bridge between the two shapes. First, the objects.

Definition (gradient, critical locus, codimension). For a polynomial $P \in \mathbb{C}[x_1,\dots,x_m]$, $$\nabla P(x) = \Bigl(\frac{\partial P}{\partial x_1}(x), \dots, \frac{\partial P}{\partial x_m}(x)\Bigr), \qquad \operatorname{Crit}(P) = \{x \in \mathbb{C}^m : \nabla P(x) = 0\}.$$ $\operatorname{Crit}(P)$ — the critical locus — is an affine algebraic set: the common zero set of the $m$ derivative polynomials. Its codimension is $m - \dim\operatorname{Crit}(P)$. Large codimension means the derivatives vanish together only on a very small set.

The plan: if $\operatorname{Crit}(P)$ has codimension at least $k$, then a generic $k$-dimensional linear subspace through the origin meets it only at $0$. Restricting the gradient to such a subspace kills all unwanted zeros; projecting its $m$ outputs down to $k$ (again generically) produces the square map Lemma 4.1 wants. Both “generic” steps are the avoidance principle, Lemma A. Fig. 8 shows the slicing step on a computed example.

Lemma 4.2. Let $P \in \mathbb{C}[x_1,\dots,x_m]$ be homogeneous of degree $d \ge 2$, and let $\delta \ge 0$ satisfy $\dim\operatorname{Crit}(P) \le \delta$. If $1 \le k \lt m$ and $k + \delta \le m$, there are linear maps $W : \mathbb{C}^k \to \mathbb{C}^m$ and $A : \mathbb{C}^m \to \mathbb{C}^k$, with $W$ injective, such that the map $$F(u) = A\,\nabla P(Wu)$$ is homogeneous of degree $d-1$ with $F^{-1}(0) = \{0\}$.

Step 0: the critical locus is a cone. Since $P$ is homogeneous of degree $d$, each $\partial P/\partial x_i$ is homogeneous of degree $d - 1 \ge 1$. A homogeneous polynomial $g$ of positive degree satisfies $g(cx) = c^{\deg g} g(x)$, so if $x$ is critical then so is $cx$ for every scalar $c$: $\operatorname{Crit}(P)$ is a cone containing $0$. Its nonzero points define the projectivization $$\mathbb{P}\operatorname{Crit}(P) = \{[x] \in \mathbb{P}^{m-1} : x \in \operatorname{Crit}(P)\setminus\{0\}\},$$ of dimension at most $\delta - 1$ by Import 1(f). (If $\operatorname{Crit}(P) = \{0\}$ the projectivization is empty, of dimension $-1$, and the avoidance below is automatic.)

Step 1: choose the slice $W$. Apply Lemma A in $\mathbb{P}^{m-1}$ (so $N = m-1$) to the set $S = \mathbb{P}\operatorname{Crit}(P)$ with $r = \delta - 1$ and $l = k - 1$. The hypothesis $k + \delta \le m$ gives $$(k-1) + (\delta - 1) \;\lt\; m - 1,$$ so some projective $(k-1)$-plane avoids $\mathbb{P}\operatorname{Crit}(P)$. That plane is the projectivization of a $k$-dimensional linear subspace of $\mathbb{C}^m$; choose an injective linear map $W : \mathbb{C}^k \to \mathbb{C}^m$ with that subspace as image. Avoidance says precisely: $$\nabla P(Wu) \ne 0 \quad\text{for every } u \ne 0.$$ (A nonzero $u$ maps to a nonzero point $Wu$ of the subspace; if the gradient vanished there, the direction $[Wu]$ would lie in both the plane and $\mathbb{P}\operatorname{Crit}(P)$.)

Step 2: choose the projection $A$. The restricted gradient now has no unwanted zeros but still has $m$ output coordinates. Because the gradient coordinates are homogeneous of the common degree $d-1$ and (by Step 1) never all vanish for $u \ne 0$, the assignment $$[u] \longmapsto [\nabla P(Wu)]$$ is a well-defined morphism $\mathbb{P}^{k-1} \to \mathbb{P}^{m-1}$: replacing $u$ by $cu$ multiplies the whole gradient by $c^{d-1}$, which does not change its projective class. Its image is an algebraic subset of $\mathbb{P}^{m-1}$ of dimension at most $k - 1$ (Import 1(e)). Fig. 9 draws what remains to be avoided.

Apply Lemma A again, to the image $S' = \{[\nabla P(Wu)]\}$ with $r = k-1$, and to planes of dimension $l = m - k - 1$. The required inequality holds with room to spare: $$(m-k-1) + (k-1) = m - 2 \;\lt\; m-1.$$ So there is a projective $(m-k-1)$-plane disjoint from $S'$; it is the projectivization of an $(m-k)$-dimensional linear subspace $K \subseteq \mathbb{C}^m$ meeting no gradient direction. Choose any rank-$k$ linear map $A : \mathbb{C}^m \to \mathbb{C}^k$ with kernel exactly $K$ (for instance, project onto a complement of $K$ and identify it with $\mathbb{C}^k$).

Step 3: conclude. Set $F(u) = A \nabla P(Wu)$. Its coordinates are linear combinations of the $\partial P/\partial x_i$ evaluated at the linear substitution $Wu$, hence homogeneous of degree $d-1$ in $u$. If $F(u) = 0$ for some $u \ne 0$, then the vector $\nabla P(Wu)$ — nonzero by Step 1 — lies in $\ker A = K$, so its direction lies in $\mathbb{P}K$, contradicting Step 2. Hence $F^{-1}(0) = \{0\}$. ∎

What was bought, and for what price. Lemma 4.2 converts one geometric hypothesis — the critical locus has codimension $\ge k$ — into exactly the shape Lemma 4.1 consumes, at zero multiplicative cost: $W$ and $A$ are linear, so composing them with any circuit is free in the model of §4.1. The next section supplies the last missing piece: computing $\nabla P$ from a circuit for $P$ at the price of a factor of three.
