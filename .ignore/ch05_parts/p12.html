11. Formulas II: a log-size matching with $n^2$ independent secrets (Lemma 9.1)

A matching $Y$ in the $n \times n$ variable matrix $X$ is a set of entries with no repeated row and no repeated column. This section proves the formula spine's engine: for a matching of merely $O(\log n)$ entries, the permanent's expansion in those variables contains $\Omega(n^2)$ algebraically independent coefficients. By Lemma 8.1, any division-free formula must then spend $\Omega(n^2)$ leaf occurrences on that one matching — and §12 will sum this over $\Omega(n^2/\log n)$ disjoint matchings.

11.1 A $5\times5$ warm-up: rows and columns separate

Choose distinct $p_1,p_2,p_3 \in \mathbb{C}$ and, independently, distinct $q_1,q_2,q_3 \in \mathbb{C}$, and consider $$B = \begin{pmatrix} y_e & 0 & p_1 & p_2 & p_3\\ 0 & y_f & 1 & 1 & 1\\ 1 & q_1 & w_{11} & w_{12} & w_{13}\\ 1 & q_2 & w_{21} & w_{22} & w_{23}\\ 1 & q_3 & w_{31} & w_{32} & w_{33} \end{pmatrix}.$$ The matching is the pair of diagonal variables $y_e, y_f$; call their rows and columns internal and the remaining three of each external. Fig. 21 color-codes the anatomy.

The permanent is multilinear in the marked variables, so $$\operatorname{per}(B) = c_{ef}\,y_ey_f + c_f\,y_f + c_e\,y_e + c_\emptyset,$$ where $c_S$ collects the permutation terms using exactly the marked entries in $S$. Each $c_S$ is a polynomial in the unmarked entries; we probe it through the external block, computing $\partial c_S/\partial w_{ab}$ and only then evaluating every $w_{ij}$ at $1$. Write $$G_b = \sum_{j \ne b} p_j, \qquad H_a = \sum_{i \ne a} q_i.$$ Differentiating by $w_{ab}$ pins external row $a$ to external column $b$; counting the remaining matchings, term by term:

  • $\dfrac{\partial c_{ef}}{\partial w_{ab}} = 2$: both internal rows and columns are consumed by their own diagonal entries; the two remaining external rows match the two remaining external columns in $2! = 2$ ways, all weights $1$.
  • $\dfrac{\partial c_{f}}{\partial w_{ab}} = 2G_b$: the $f$-row sits on its diagonal, but the $e$-row must exit to an external column $j \ne b$, weight $p_j$ (hence $G_b$); the orphaned $e$-column receives either of the $2$ remaining external rows, weight $1$; the rest is forced.
  • $\dfrac{\partial c_{e}}{\partial w_{ab}} = 2H_a$: the mirror image — the $f$-column receives an external row $i \ne a$, weight $q_i$ (hence $H_a$), and the $f$-row exits with $2$ free choices.
  • $\dfrac{\partial c_{\emptyset}}{\partial w_{ab}} = G_bH_a$: both internal diagonals unused; the $e$-row exits (factor $G_b$), the $f$-column receives (factor $H_a$), and these choices are independent — the $e$-row chooses a column, the $f$-column chooses a row — with everything else forced.

Restrict to the four probe variables $w_{11}, w_{12}, w_{21}, w_{22}$ and stack the four gradients: $$J = \operatorname{diag}(2,2,2,1) \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1\\ G_1 & G_2 & G_1 & G_2\\ H_1 & H_1 & H_2 & H_2\\ G_1H_1 & G_2H_1 & G_1H_2 & G_2H_2 \end{pmatrix} = \operatorname{diag}(2,2,2,1)\,\bigl(V_H \otimes V_G\bigr),$$ where $V_G = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1 & 1\\ G_1 & G_2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $V_H = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1 & 1\\ H_1 & H_2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ and $\otimes$ is the Kronecker product (each entry of $V_H$ multiplied by the whole of $V_G$). Since $G_2 - G_1 = p_1 - p_2 \ne 0$ and $H_2 - H_1 = q_1 - q_2 \ne 0$, $$\det J = 8\,\det(V_H)^2\det(V_G)^2 = 8\,(p_1-p_2)^2(q_1-q_2)^2 \ne 0.$$ By the Jacobian criterion (Import 6), the four coefficients $c_{ef}, c_f, c_e, c_\emptyset$ are algebraically independent — a nonzero minor at one specialization certifies independence of the polynomials themselves. The plan for general $n$: replace the single $e$ and $f$ by $\ell$ indices each; subsets of the $e$-indices will encode external column data in binary, subsets of the $f$-indices external row data, and the two $2\times2$ Vandermonde factors will become $m \times m$ Vandermonde-type factors certifying $m^2$ independent coefficients.

11.2 Parameters, and the chosen coefficient family

Definition (formula parameters (9.1)). For $n \ge 32$ set $$\ell = \lceil \log_2 n\rceil, \qquad k = 2\ell, \qquad m = n - k.$$

Three inequalities are used throughout; all follow from $n \ge 32$ (so $\ell \ge 5$ and $2^{\ell - 1} \lt n \le 2^{\ell}$): (i) $k \le n/2$, equivalently $n \ge 4\ell$ — at $\ell = 5$, $n \ge 32 \gt 20$; for $\ell \ge 6$, $n \ge 2^{\ell-1}+1 \ge 4\ell + 1$ (check $2^{\ell-1} \ge 4\ell$ at $\ell = 6$: $32 \ge 24$; doubling the left side beats adding $4$ to the right). (ii) $m \ge k + 1$, equivalently $n \ge 4\ell + 1$ — same computation. (iii) $m \le 2^\ell$, since $m \lt n \le 2^\ell$. Interpretation: $k$ marked entries suffice to hold two groups of $\ell$ binary digits; $m$, the number of external rows and columns, is both large ($\ge n/2$ by (i)) and addressable by $\ell$ bits (by (iii)); and (ii) guarantees enough external columns for every injection appearing below.

Lemma 9.1. If $Y$ is a matching of $k$ entries of $X$, then $\operatorname{td}_Y(\operatorname{per}_n) \ge m^2$.

Step 1: coefficients of marked diagonal entries. Permuting rows and columns of $X$ permutes the monomials of $\operatorname{per}_n$ (row permutations relabel the $\sigma$'s, column permutations compose them), so it changes neither the permanent up to relabeling nor any $\operatorname{td}_Y$; assume $Y = \{y_1,\dots,y_k\}$ with $y_i = x_{ii}$. For $S \subseteq [k]$ let $c_S$ be the coefficient of $\prod_{i \in S} y_i$. By multilinearity, $$c_S = \Bigl(\prod_{i \in S} \frac{\partial}{\partial y_i}\Bigr)\operatorname{per}_n(X)\ \Bigl|_{\,y_1 = \dots = y_k = 0}: \tag{9.2}$$ differentiating selects the terms through the marked diagonal entries of $S$; zeroing discards terms using any other marked entry. Equivalently, $c_S$ is the permanent of the matrix with rows and columns $S$ deleted and the remaining marked diagonal entries replaced by $0$.

Step 2: internal/external anatomy, and the magic specialization. Split the marked indices into two halves $[k] = E \sqcup F$, $E = \{e_0,\dots,e_{\ell-1}\}$, $F = \{f_0,\dots,f_{\ell-1}\}$; call all $k$ marked rows/columns internal, index the $m$ remaining external rows and columns by $[m]$, and write $$X = \begin{pmatrix} D & U\\ V & W\end{pmatrix},$$ with $D$ ($k \times k$, internal–internal), $U$ ($k \times m$, internal rows to external columns), $V$ ($m \times k$, external rows to internal columns), $W = (w_{ab})$ ($m \times m$, external–external). Choose distinct $p_1,\dots,p_m \in \mathbb{C}$ and, independently, distinct $q_1,\dots,q_m \in \mathbb{C}$, and define the specialization $\xi$ of the unmarked variables (Fig. 22): for $i \ne j$ in $[k]$, $0 \le u,v \lt \ell$, $a,b \in [m]$, $$D_{ij} = 0, \qquad w_{ab} = 1, \tag{9.3}$$ $$U_{e_u, b} = p_b^{\,2^u}, \qquad U_{f_v, b} = 1, \qquad V_{a, e_u} = 1, \qquad V_{a, f_v} = q_a^{\,2^v}. \tag{9.4}$$ Derivatives are always taken before evaluating at $\xi$. The powers $2^u, 2^v$ make subsets of $E$ and $F$ encode integers in binary without collisions: an internal $e$-row exiting to external column $b$ picks up $p_b$ raised to its binary weight; an external row $a$ entering an internal $f$-column picks up $q_a$ likewise; all other internal–external entries weigh $1$.

The coefficient family. For $0 \le \alpha, \beta \lt m$, let $P(\alpha) \subseteq E$ and $Q(\beta) \subseteq F$ hold the $1$-digits of the $\ell$-bit binary expansions of $\alpha, \beta$ (digit $0$ least significant; this is where $m \le 2^\ell$ enters). Set $$T(\alpha,\beta) = P(\alpha) \cup Q(\beta), \qquad S(\alpha,\beta) = [k] \setminus T(\alpha,\beta).$$ These give $m^2$ coefficients $c_{S(\alpha,\beta)}$ of distinct marked monomials. Taking the complement matters: the indices in $S$ are consumed by their own diagonal entries, while the indices in $T$ survive into the coefficient permanent and must be matched through the unmarked blocks — they are the message carriers. Our goal is the invertibility of the $m^2 \times m^2$ Jacobian $$J_{(\alpha,\beta),(a,b)} = \frac{\partial c_{S(\alpha,\beta)}}{\partial w_{ab}}\Bigl|_{\xi}.$$ Once $\det J \ne 0$ is established, Import 6 finishes the lemma.

Step 3: count the surviving permutation terms. Fix $\alpha,\beta,a,b$; abbreviate $P = P(\alpha)$, $Q = Q(\beta)$, $T = P \cup Q$, $r = |T| \le k$. In the permanent computing $c_{S(\alpha,\beta)}$ (rows/columns $S$ deleted, remaining marked diagonal zeroed), differentiation by $w_{ab}$ fixes external row $a$ to external column $b$ and deletes both. What remains: the internal rows and columns $T$, and $m - 1$ external rows and columns. At $\xi$ the internal block on $T$ is entirely zero — off-diagonal entries by (9.3), diagonal ones by the zeroing in (9.2) — so no internal row can match an internal column. Therefore:

  • the internal rows $T$ exit via an injection $\rho : T \hookrightarrow [m]\setminus\{b\}$ into distinct available external columns (well-defined since $r \le k \le m - 1$ by parameter fact (ii));
  • the internal columns $T$ receive an injection $\theta : T \hookrightarrow [m]\setminus\{a\}$ of distinct available external rows;
  • the $m - 1 - r$ external rows not used by $\theta$ match the $m-1-r$ external columns not used by $\rho$ in $(m-1-r)!$ ways, all weight $1$ ($W \equiv 1$ at $\xi$).

The choices $\rho$ (columns) and $\theta$ (rows) are independent, so $$J_{(\alpha,\beta),(a,b)} = (m-1-r)!\ \Bigl(\sum_{\rho\,:\,T \hookrightarrow [m]\setminus\{b\}}\ \prod_{i \in T} U_{i,\rho(i)}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{\theta\,:\,T \hookrightarrow [m]\setminus\{a\}}\ \prod_{j \in T} V_{\theta(j), j}\Bigr). \tag{9.5}$$ This is the general form of the four hand counts of §11.1. To convert the two injection sums into something whose invertibility is visible, we express each as the value of a univariate polynomial.

Step 4: forbidden-column sums are polynomial evaluations. For $P' \subseteq E$ define the binary weight and the unrestricted injection sum $$D_{P'} = \sum_{e_u \in P'} 2^u, \qquad H_{P'} = \sum_{\rho\,:\,P' \hookrightarrow [m]}\ \prod_{e_u \in P'} p_{\rho(e_u)}^{\,2^u},$$ (so $H_\emptyset = 1$), and define $g_{P'} \in \mathbb{C}[z]$ recursively by $$g_\emptyset(z) = 1, \qquad g_{P'}(z) = H_{P'} - \sum_{e_u \in P'} z^{2^u}\, g_{P'\setminus\{e_u\}}(z) \quad (P' \ne \emptyset). \tag{9.6}$$ Claim: $$g_{P'}(p_b) = \sum_{\rho\,:\,P' \hookrightarrow [m]\setminus\{b\}}\ \prod_{e_u \in P'} p_{\rho(e_u)}^{\,2^u}, \qquad \deg g_{P'} = D_{P'}, \qquad [z^{D_{P'}}]\,g_{P'}(z) = (-1)^{|P'|}\,|P'|!\,. \tag{9.7}$$ For the evaluation identity, induct on $|P'|$: an injection of $P'$ into all of $[m]$ either avoids $b$, or sends exactly one index $e_u$ to $b$ — contributing $p_b^{2^u}$ times an injection of $P'\setminus\{e_u\}$ that avoids $b$ (it must avoid $b$: injectivity forbids reuse), i.e. $z^{2^u}g_{P'\setminus\{e_u\}}(z)$ at $z = p_b$ by the inductive hypothesis. Subtracting the $b$-users from $H_{P'}$ is precisely (9.6). For degree and leading coefficient, induct again: $H_{P'}$ is a constant, and each subtracted term $z^{2^u}g_{P'\setminus\{e_u\}}(z)$ has degree $2^u + D_{P'\setminus\{e_u\}} = D_{P'}$ with leading coefficient $(-1)^{|P'|-1}(|P'|-1)!$; the minus sign in front and the $|P'|$ choices of $e_u$ give $(-1)^{|P'|}|P'|!$ — nonzero in characteristic zero. (Sanity: $g_{\{e_0\}}(z) = \sum_b p_b - z$, matching $G_b$ of the warm-up.) The row side is identical: for $Q' \subseteq F$ define $D_{Q'} = \sum_{f_v \in Q'}2^v$, the injection sum $K_{Q'}$ over rows, and $$h_\emptyset(z) = 1, \qquad h_{Q'}(z) = K_{Q'} - \sum_{f_v \in Q'} z^{2^v} h_{Q'\setminus\{f_v\}}(z),$$ which satisfies the mirror of (9.7) with $q_a$ in place of $p_b$. $\tag{9.8}$

Step 5: factor each Jacobian entry. Write $(M)_s = M(M-1)\cdots(M-s+1)$ for the falling factorial, $(M)_0 = 1$. In the first sum of (9.5), choose the images of the indices in $P$ first: their weighted total is $g_P(p_b)$ by (9.7). Each index of $Q$ has $U$-weight $1$, so each choice extends to the $Q$-indices in exactly $(m-1-|P|)_{|Q|}$ ways (distinct columns from the remaining pool). Symmetrically for the second sum: the $Q$-images weigh $h_Q(q_a)$, and the $P$-indices extend in $(m-1-|Q|)_{|P|}$ weight-$1$ ways. Substituting into (9.5): $$J_{(\alpha,\beta),(a,b)} = L_{P,Q}\ g_P(p_b)\,h_Q(q_a), \tag{9.9}$$ $$L_{P,Q} = (m-1-|P|-|Q|)!\ (m-1-|P|)_{|Q|}\ (m-1-|Q|)_{|P|} \ne 0, \tag{9.10}$$ where all factors are positive because $|P| + |Q| \le 2\ell = k \le m-1$: every falling factorial counts injections that exist. Crucially, $L_{P,Q}$ depends only on the coefficient row $(\alpha,\beta)$, not on the probe $(a,b)$: it is a row scaling.

Step 6: two Vandermonde factors. By construction $D_{P(\alpha)} = \alpha$ and $D_{Q(\beta)} = \beta$ — the binary encoding pays off exactly here. So the polynomials $g_{P(0)}, g_{P(1)}, \dots, g_{P(m-1)}$ have degrees $0, 1, \dots, m-1$ with nonzero leading coefficients (9.7): their coefficient matrix in the monomial basis $1, z, \dots, z^{m-1}$ is triangular with nonzero diagonal, hence invertible. The evaluation matrix $$A_p = \bigl(g_{P(\alpha)}(p_b)\bigr)_{b, \alpha} = \bigl(p_b^{\,j}\bigr)_{b,j} \cdot \bigl(\text{coefficient matrix}\bigr)$$ is a product of the Vandermonde matrix on the distinct nodes $p_1,\dots,p_m$ (invertible — its determinant $\prod_{b \lt b'}(p_{b'}-p_b) \ne 0$) with that triangular matrix: invertible. Identically, $A_q = (h_{Q(\beta)}(q_a))_{a,\beta}$ is invertible. Now (9.9) says that, up to the pairing of index pairs, $J$ is the Kronecker product $A_p^{\mathsf T} \otimes A_q^{\mathsf T}$ scaled by the nonzero row factors $L_{P,Q}$ (Fig. 23): a Kronecker product of invertibles is invertible ($\det(A \otimes B) = \det(A)^m\det(B)^m$ for $m\times m$ factors), row scalings preserve invertibility, and permuting the pairing is a permutation similarity. Hence $\det J \ne 0$, and Import 6 delivers $$\operatorname{td}_Y(\operatorname{per}_n) \ge m^2.$$ ∎

What just happened, in one paragraph. The permanent treats the $k$ marked diagonal cells as switches: the coefficient $c_S$ is a sub-permanent in which the off switches ($T = [k]\setminus S$) must route through the rest of the matrix. The specialization $\xi$ wires each off $e$-switch to broadcast a distinguishable power of $p$, each off $f$-switch to receive a distinguishable power of $q$, and everything else to be neutral. Probing with $\partial/\partial w_{ab}$ reads off a product (column signal at $b$) $\times$ (row signal at $a$) — a rank-one structure per coefficient whose stacked matrix is a Kronecker product of two invertible Vandermonde-type systems. The determinant argument never needed to expand a single permanent: multilinearity, injective routing, and binary bookkeeping did everything. §15 (check 6) runs the entire construction at $n = 13$, $\ell = 3$, $m = 7$: it rebuilds all $49$ coefficients by brute-force permanents, confirms (9.9) entrywise, and inverts the $49\times49$ Jacobian in exact arithmetic.
