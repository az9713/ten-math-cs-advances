14. The landscape: what is proved, and what is not

Lower bounds in algebraic complexity form a zoo of computational models, and results are routinely misquoted across model boundaries. This section places Theorems 1.1–1.3 on the map, states explicitly what they do not claim, and records where each simplification made in this chapter is repaid.

14.2 Where the ideas came from, and what is genuinely new

  • Bézout + gate equations + Baur–Strassen (§4–§6) are Strassen-era classics [Str73a, BS83, Hei83]. The new content is permanent-side: a specialization with simultaneously high degree ($\Theta(\log n)$), many variables ($\Theta(n^2)$), and critical-locus codimension $\Theta(n^2)$ — no previous specialization had all three.
  • Minor sums via partition Möbius inversion (§7) is the rectangular Binet–Minc identity [Min79, Rot64]; square-zero (“zeon”) algebras encoding permanental minors appear in [FM11, BP15]; root-of-unity filters echo Ryser and Glynn [Rys63, Gly10, AH14]. Lemma 6.1's block-selective combination of the three is new; Friedland–Levy's completion [FL06] realizes one minor sum inside a permanent but without block selectivity.
  • Nečiporuk-style blockwise charging (§10, §12) descends from Boolean formulas [Nec66] through Kalorkoti's arithmetic version [Kal85, KS14]. The closest antecedent of the log-size-matching idea is Hrubẽš–Joglekar's work on read-limited determinantal representations [HJ25], which packs $\Theta(n^2/\log n)$ matchings in a different model. Lemma 9.1's Vandermonde–Kronecker independence certificate — $\Omega(n^2)$ independent coefficients from $O(\log n)$ marked entries of the permanent — is the new engine; algebraic-independence and Jacobian technology in lower bounds goes back to [BMS11, ASSS16].
  • The division extension (Lemma 10.2) follows Kalorkoti's direct analysis of rational formulas [Kal85] rather than Strassen's division elimination [Str73b], which would not preserve the sharp occurrence counts.

14.3 Idealizations made in this document, and where each is repaid

  • “Compute” = symbolic identity over $\mathbb{C}$. Everything here concerns exact formal computation. Numerical evaluation, approximation, and border complexity (limits of determinantal representations [LMR13, MS01, BIP19]) are genuinely different settings; nothing in this chapter transfers to them automatically.
  • Characteristic zero is load-bearing. It enters at least four times: $q! \ne 0$ in the reduction (5.6); generic smoothness (Import 5); the Jacobian criterion (Import 6); and the sign cancellations $\sum_\sigma \operatorname{sgn}\sigma = 0$. In characteristic 2 the permanent equals the determinant and every permanent-vs-determinant question collapses.
  • The imports are real debts. Seven boxed theorems (§3, §13) are consumed without proof: dimension theory, Nullstellensatz, two Bézouts, generic smoothness, the Jacobian criterion, and the ABV singular-locus bound. Each is classical and cited to a standard source; none is folklore-fragile.
  • Explicit constants over asymptotics. The constants $144, 128, 192$ are what the bookkeeping in §9 and §12 actually yields, at thresholds $n \ge 2^{16}$ resp. $n \ge 32$; §15 re-verifies the full chains numerically at the thresholds. Optimizing constants was nobody's goal — the growth types are the content.
  • What would push further. The circuit bound is gated by the $2^t$ power-sum count in Proposition 5.1 (forcing $d = O(\log n)$); any polynomial-in-$t$ replacement would immediately lift $\Omega(n^2\log\log n)$ toward $\Omega(n^2\log n)$. The formula bound saturates its own method's $N^2/\log N$ ceiling [CKSV22]; passing it requires abandoning blockwise coefficient charging altogether.
