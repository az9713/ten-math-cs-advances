15. Numerical validation: ten exact checks

Every identity in this document that can be exercised at small parameters has been, in exact arithmetic — integers, rationals, or $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]$ with $\zeta^2 + \zeta + 1 = 0$; no floating point anywhere. The listing below is self-contained standard-library Python; running it re-derives every claim in Fig. 29 and prints eleven PASS lines (check 7 has two parts). Total runtime is dominated by check 7, which brute-forces $49 \times 49$ permanent-derivatives of size up to $12 \times 12$ by Ryser's formula and then eliminates the $49\times49$ integer Jacobian exactly.

What the toys do and do not validate. The runs exercise the mechanics — every algebraic identity, cancellation, factorization, and inequality chain — at parameters far below the theorems' regimes ($n = 13$ instead of $n \ge 32$; the Lemma 9.1 toy uses the proof's actual constraints $m \le 2^\ell$, $k \le m-1$ directly, since (9.1)'s defaults only bite at $n \ge 32$). The asymptotic statements get their own exact checks at the true thresholds: C8 verifies the complete Theorem 1.2 chain at $n = 32$ ($\nu = 96$ matchings, pairwise entry-disjointness by exhaustive set comparison, $\nu m^2/4 = 11616 \ge n^4/(128\log_2 n) = 1638.4$), and C9 verifies every hypothesis and inequality of Proposition 7.1 and Theorem 1.1 at $n = 2^{16}$, where $d = 4$, $t = 16$, $b = 2048$, $m = rs = 1{,}073{,}872{,}896$, and the final bound is $\lfloor n^2/144\rfloor \cdot (\log_2\log_2 n - 3) = 29{,}826{,}161$ gates.
