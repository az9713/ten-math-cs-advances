3. Theorem 1 and the plan of the proof

We can now state the main theorem in its final form. (The proof is assembled in §14; everything in between builds its two halves.)

Theorem 1 (main theorem). There is a deterministic polynomial-time mapping that assigns to each 3SAT formula $\varphi$ a nonsingular matrix $B \in \mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}$, a target $t \in \mathbb{Z}^n$, and a radius $r \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ such that $$\varphi \text{ satisfiable} \;\Longrightarrow\; \mathrm{dist}_2(t, L(B)) \le r, \qquad\qquad \varphi \text{ unsatisfiable} \;\Longrightarrow\; \mathrm{dist}_2(t, L(B)) > n^{1/400}\, r.$$ Consequently, $\mathrm{GapCVP}^{(2)}_{n^{1/400}}$ is NP-hard under deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions.

The reduction factors through an intermediate combinatorial problem: $$\text{3SAT} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{binary nearest codeword} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{Euclidean CVP}.$$ The second arrow is a classical, dimension-preserving construction (the “parity-lift” lattice), which converts a Hamming-weight gap of factor $\Gamma$ into a Euclidean-distance gap of factor $\sqrt{\Gamma}$ — squaring appears because Euclidean distance squared counts $\pm 1$ coordinates. We prove it first, in §4, as Lemma 7. The first arrow is the paper's contribution: a deterministic encoding of 3SAT into a binary affine system $Hx = b$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$ (the field with two elements, defined properly in §4) whose minimum-weight solutions are rigid enough to encode satisfiability, with a weight gap of factor $4M^{1/200}$ in dimension $M$. Chaining the arrows: $\sqrt{4M^{1/200}}\ \ge M^{1/400} = n^{1/400}$, since the lattice rank is exactly $n = M$.

Where does the strange exponent come from? Not from one clever trick but from a cascade of budgets, each chosen to dominate the next with room to spare. Reading Fig. 9 top to bottom: the encoding needs a moment budget $T = N^{30}$ (enough power sums to run a valuation argument whose window reaches $\approx 5N^{21}$, §12); the soundness analysis caps fiber sizes at $K = N^4$ and needs $|P| \approx q$ to exceed error terms of size $\approx 2dK^2T \approx 2N^{39}$ (§9); and the Markov-style discard of oversized fibers needs the total solution weight $4M^{1/200}R$ divided by $K$ to be a tiny fraction of $q$ (§11). Setting $q \approx N^{200}$ satisfies everything simultaneously with slack; then $M \approx q^2$ forces the binary gap exponent $1/200$, and the square root of Lemma 7 halves it to $1/400$. The exponents $4, 30, 200, 400$ are engineering choices, not magic constants — and in §17 every one of the resulting inequalities is re-verified by exact integer arithmetic at $N = 100$.

How to read the next eleven sections. Two independent tracks meet in the middle. The construction track (§4–§7) is finite linear algebra: codes, lattices, tables, constraints, and the easy completeness direction. The algebra track (§8–§10) is field theory: power sums, Hankel determinants, and valuations, developed with no reference to 3SAT at all. Soundness (§11–§13) is where the second track dismantles the first: it takes an arbitrary low-weight solution of the constraints and squeezes a satisfying assignment out of it. If you ever lose the thread, return to Fig. 3.
