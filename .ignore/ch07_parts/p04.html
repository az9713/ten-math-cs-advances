4. The last mile first: binary codes and the parity-lift lattice (Lemmas 6–7)

We prove the pipeline's final arrow before building anything else. Once it is in hand, the rest of the chapter never has to mention Euclidean geometry again: everything reduces to weights of binary vectors.

4.1 Binary linear codes and two decoding problems

Write $\mathbb{F}_2 = \{0,1\}$ for the field with two elements, with arithmetic modulo $2$; note $-1 = 1$, so subtraction and addition coincide. The vector space $\mathbb{F}_2^M$ consists of length-$M$ binary words with coordinatewise XOR as addition. The Hamming weight $\mathrm{wt}(x)$ of a word is its number of nonzero coordinates, and the Hamming distance between two words is $\mathrm{wt}$ of their difference.

Definition (binary linear code; two problems). A binary linear code is a linear subspace $\mathcal{C} \le \mathbb{F}_2^M$; its elements are codewords. A generator matrix has rows spanning $\mathcal{C}$; a parity-check matrix is any $H \in \mathbb{F}_2^{r\times M}$ with $\mathcal{C} = \ker_{\mathbb{F}_2} H$ (rows need not be independent). Given a code and a received word $u \in \mathbb{F}_2^M$, the nearest-codeword problem asks for $$d_H(u, \mathcal{C}) = \min_{c \in \mathcal{C}} \mathrm{wt}(u - c).$$ Given a parity-check matrix $H$, a syndrome $b \in \mathbb{F}_2^{r}$, and the consistent affine system $Hx = b$, the syndrome-decoding (minimum-weight affine solution) problem asks for $$W(H, b) = \min\{\, \mathrm{wt}(x) : x \in \mathbb{F}_2^M,\ Hx = b \,\}.$$ Promise versions carry a radius $R > 0$ and factor $\gamma$: YES if the minimum is $\le R$, NO if it is $> \gamma R$.

The two problems are the same problem in different clothes. If $Hu = b$, then the solution set of $Hx = b$ is the affine coset $u + \mathcal{C}$, so $W(H,b) = \min_{c\in\mathcal{C}} \mathrm{wt}(u - c) = d_H(u,\mathcal{C})$ (using $-=+$ in characteristic two); conversely, given $(\mathcal{C}, u)$, set $b = Hu$. Gaussian elimination over $\mathbb{F}_2$ converts between generator and parity-check descriptions, tests whether $Hx=b$ is consistent (i.e. $\operatorname{rank} H = \operatorname{rank} [H\,|\,b]$), and computes a particular solution — all in polynomial time, by the row-reduction algorithm of ordinary linear algebra, which is valid verbatim over any field. Fig. 10 shows the smallest interesting example, which doubles as the paper's worked example.

4.2 The parity-lift lattice

Now lift the mod-2 picture to the integers. Let $$\rho : \mathbb{Z}^M \longrightarrow \mathbb{F}_2^M, \qquad \rho(z) = z \bmod 2$$ be coordinatewise reduction. Given a parity-check matrix $H$, define the parity-lift lattice $$\Lambda_H \;=\; \rho^{-1}(\mathcal{C}) \;=\; \{\, z \in \mathbb{Z}^M : H(z \bmod 2) = 0 \,\}, \qquad \mathcal{C} = \ker_{\mathbb{F}_2} H .$$ Because $\mathcal{C}$ is a subgroup of $\mathbb{F}_2^M$ and $\rho$ is a group homomorphism, $\Lambda_H$ is a subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}^M$; it contains $2\mathbb{Z}^M$ (which $\rho$ kills), so it spans $\mathbb{R}^M$ and is a full-rank lattice. Writing $e_c \in \{0,1\}^M$ for the obvious integer lift of a codeword $c$, the lattice decomposes into cosets of $2\mathbb{Z}^M$: $$\Lambda_H \;=\; \bigcup_{c \in \mathcal{C}} \big( e_c + 2\mathbb{Z}^M \big), \qquad 2\mathbb{Z}^M \subseteq \Lambda_H \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^M .$$ This inverse-image construction is classical (it appears in Leech and Sloane's 1971 sphere-packing constructions, and Bennett–Peikert's randomized SVP reduction combines the same lift with Reed–Solomon codes). Fig. 11 shows it in the plane.

A CVP instance needs a basis, not a membership test. One is produced by putting the code in systematic form: Gaussian elimination plus a permutation of the $M$ coordinates writes $$\mathcal{C} = \{\, (f,\ P_{\mathrm{sys}} f) : f \in \mathbb{F}_2^{k_0} \,\}, \qquad k_0 = \dim_{\mathbb{F}_2}\mathcal{C} = M - \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_2} H,$$ where the first $k_0$ coordinates are free (“information”) and $P_{\mathrm{sys}} \in \mathbb{F}_2^{(M-k_0)\times k_0}$ determines the remaining (“parity”) coordinates. Write $\widetilde{P}_{\mathrm{sys}} \in \{0,1\}^{(M-k_0)\times k_0}$ for its entrywise integer lift.

Lemma 6 (explicit parity-lift lattice basis). In systematic coordinate order, $\Lambda_H$ has the integer basis $$B_H \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} I_{k_0} & 0 \\ \widetilde{P}_{\mathrm{sys}} & 2 I_{M-k_0} \end{pmatrix} , \tag{1}$$ i.e. $\Lambda_H = B_H \mathbb{Z}^M$, and $\lvert \det B_H \rvert = 2^{M-k_0} = 2^{\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_2} H}$. The basis and the coordinate permutation are computable in polynomial time from $H$.
Work in systematic coordinates and split an integer vector as $z = (f, g)$ with $f \in \mathbb{Z}^{k_0}$, $g \in \mathbb{Z}^{M-k_0}$. Membership unwinds step by step: $$z \in \Lambda_H \iff (f, g) \bmod 2 \in \mathcal{C} \iff g \equiv \widetilde{P}_{\mathrm{sys}} f \pmod 2 \iff g = \widetilde{P}_{\mathrm{sys}} f + 2w \ \text{ for some } w \in \mathbb{Z}^{M-k_0} \iff z = B_H \begin{pmatrix} f \\ w \end{pmatrix}.$$ (The second equivalence is the systematic description of $\mathcal{C}$ applied to $\rho(z)$; the third rewrites a congruence as an equation.) Hence $\Lambda_H = B_H\mathbb{Z}^M$. The matrix is lower block-triangular, so its determinant is the product of the diagonal entries, $1^{k_0} \cdot 2^{M-k_0}$. Gaussian elimination computes $P_{\mathrm{sys}}$ and the permutation in polynomial time; applying the inverse permutation to the rows of $B_H$ restores the original coordinate order. ∎
Worked example. $H = [1\ 1\ 1]$, $b = 1$. Then $\mathcal{C} = \{000, 011, 101, 110\}$ and $\Lambda_H = \{z \in \mathbb{Z}^3 : z_1 + z_2 + z_3 \equiv 0 \bmod 2\}$. Here $k_0 = 2$, $P_{\mathrm{sys}} = [1\ 1]$ (the third coordinate is the sum of the first two), and $$B_H = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \lvert\det B_H\rvert = 2 .$$ With target $u = (1,0,0)$: the binary solutions of $x_1+x_2+x_3 = 1$ are the odd-weight words, of minimum weight $1$, and correspondingly $\mathrm{dist}_2(u, \Lambda_H) = 1$ (Fig. 13). This instance is verified by brute force in §17.

4.3 The distance identity

The point of the construction is an exact identity between lattice distances and binary weights — not an inequality with losses.

Lemma 7 (nearest codeword to CVP, exactly, in every norm). Let $H \in \mathbb{F}_2^{r\times M}$, $b \in \mathbb{F}_2^r$, and suppose $Hx = b$ is consistent. In deterministic polynomial time one can construct the nonsingular $B_H \in \mathbb{Z}^{M\times M}$ of Lemma 6 and a target $u \in \{0,1\}^M$ such that, for every real $p \ge 1$, $$\min_{\lambda \in L(B_H)} \lVert u - \lambda \rVert_p^p \;=\; W(H, b),$$ and in particular $\mathrm{dist}_2(u, L(B_H))^2 = W(H,b)$. The lattice rank is exactly $M$.
Compute (Gaussian elimination) a particular binary solution $u$ of $Hu = b$, the systematic form, and $B_H$; restore the original coordinate order. Then $L(B_H) = \Lambda_H$ by Lemma 6.

Lower bound. Let $\lambda \in \Lambda_H$ and set $x = (u - \lambda) \bmod 2$. Since $H(\lambda \bmod 2) = 0$, linearity gives $Hx = Hu = b$, so $x$ is a binary solution and $\mathrm{wt}(x) \ge W(H,b)$. Each nonzero coordinate of $x$ arises from an odd integer coordinate of $u - \lambda$, whose absolute value is therefore at least $1$; all coordinates contribute nonnegatively (Fig. 12). Hence $$\lVert u - \lambda \rVert_p^p = \sum_{j=1}^{M} \lvert u_j - \lambda_j \rvert^p \;\ge\; \#\{j : u_j - \lambda_j \text{ odd}\} = \mathrm{wt}(x) \;\ge\; W(H,b).$$ Upper bound. Let $x$ be any binary solution of $Hx = b$ and $e_x \in \{0,1\}^M$ its integer lift. Then $(u - e_x) \bmod 2$ is a codeword's residue: $H((u-e_x)\bmod 2) = b - b = 0$, so $\lambda = u - e_x \in \Lambda_H$, and $\lVert u - \lambda \rVert_p^p = \lVert e_x \rVert_p^p = \mathrm{wt}(x)$ because every entry of $e_x$ is $0$ or $1$. Minimizing over binary solutions $x$ makes the two bounds meet. Setting $p = 2$ gives the squared-distance identity, and $B_H$ is a nonsingular $M \times M$ matrix, so the rank is $M$. ∎

Consequently a binary weight gap converts into a distance gap with a square root: if either $W(H,b) \le R$ or $W(H,b) > \Gamma R$, then correspondingly either $$\min_{\lambda} \lVert u - \lambda \rVert_p \le R^{1/p} \qquad \text{or} \qquad \min_{\lambda} \lVert u - \lambda \rVert_p > \Gamma^{1/p} R^{1/p},$$ an $\ell_p$ gap of factor $\Gamma^{1/p}$ — for the Euclidean norm, $\sqrt{\Gamma}$. Two cautions the paper records, which will matter in §14: the thresholds $R^{1/p}$ need not be rational (the final instance uses the integer radius $\lceil \sqrt R\, \rceil$ and pays a factor $\le 4$), and the construction needs a particular solution $u$, hence a consistent system — the inconsistent case will be handled by a separate trivial branch.
