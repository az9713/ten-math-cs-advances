14. Transfer to CVP: the proof of Theorem 1

All the pieces exist; this section is the final assembly — including the unglamorous but necessary bookkeeping that makes the map total (defined on every formula) and its guarantees strict. Fig. 35 is the algorithm.

Proof of Theorem 1. We describe the map $\varphi \mapsto (B, t, r)$ branch by branch, then verify the two promise implications and the running time.

Degenerate branches. After the preprocessing of §6: if no clauses remain, $\varphi$ is satisfiable; return the fixed YES-instance $(B,t,r) = ((1), (0), 1)$, whose distance is $0 \le 1 = r$. If an empty clause remains, $\varphi$ is unsatisfiable; return $$(B, t, r) = \big( (2),\ (1),\ \tfrac12 \big):$$ the lattice is $2\mathbb{Z}$, the target is $1$, so the distance is exactly $1$, and since here $n = 1$ we have $n^{1/400} r = 1 \cdot \tfrac12 = \tfrac12 < 1$ — a strict NO-instance.

Main branch. Otherwise construct the explicit affine system (3) with the parameters of §7.4, and test consistency by Gaussian elimination over $\mathbb{F}_2$. Note carefully: this tests whether $Hx=b$ has any binary solution, a polynomial-time question; it does not decide whether $\varphi$ is satisfiable, which is about low-weight solutions. If the system is inconsistent, return the fixed NO-instance above — legitimately, because by Lemma 8 a satisfiable formula always yields a consistent system, so inconsistency certifies unsatisfiability.

On the consistent branch, compute a particular solution $u \in \{0,1\}^M$ of $Hu = b$ and the parity-lift basis $B_H$ of Lemma 6 (with the inverse coordinate permutation applied). Output $$(B, t, r) \;=\; \Big( B_H,\ u,\ \big\lceil \sqrt{(\ell+1)\lvert P\rvert}\, \big\rceil \Big).$$ The dimension is $n = M$, the basis is square and nonsingular, and basis, target, and radius are integers in binary.

Completeness. If $\varphi$ is satisfiable, Lemma 8 gives a binary solution of weight $R = (\ell+1)\lvert P\rvert$, and Lemma 7's identity gives $$\mathrm{dist}_2\big(u, L(B_H)\big) = \sqrt{W(H,b)} \le \sqrt{R} \le r .$$

Soundness. Let $\varphi$ be unsatisfiable and suppose, for contradiction, that $\mathrm{dist}_2(u, L(B_H)) \le M^{c} r$ with $c = 1/400$. Lemma 7 converts distance to weight: $$W(H, b) \;=\; \mathrm{dist}_2\big(u, L(B_H)\big)^2 \;\le\; M^{1/200}\, r^2 \;\le\; 4 M^{1/200} R,$$ where $r^2 \le 4R$ because $r = \lceil \sqrt R\,\rceil \le \sqrt R + 1 \le 2\sqrt R$ (using $R \ge 1$). By Proposition 14 the formula would be satisfiable — contradiction. Hence $$\mathrm{dist}_2\big(u, L(B_H)\big) \;>\; M^{c}\, r \;=\; n^{1/400}\, r,$$ and the inequality is strict, as the promise definition requires.

Running time and size. The field construction is deterministic $N^{O(1)}$ (Proposition C, with $e = O(\log N)$); each of the $O(N^{401})$ rows of $H$ (bound (5)) is a field-arithmetic computation over $O(N^{401})$ columns (bound (4)); Gaussian elimination, the systematic form, and $B_H$ are polynomial in the $O(N^{802})$-bit dense description. Every step is deterministic. Since $N \le 100 + 3\cdot(\text{input length})$, the whole map runs in deterministic polynomial time in the length of $\varphi$. With Import 1 (3SAT is NP-complete) and the composition of reductions, $\mathrm{GapCVP}^{(2)}_{n^{1/400}}$ is NP-hard. ∎

Two small subtleties worth savoring. (i) The radius must be part of the instance and rational: $\sqrt R$ itself may be irrational, and rounding up to $\lceil\sqrt R\rceil$ costs at most the benign factor $4$ inside the soundness threshold $4M^{1/200}R$ that Proposition 14 was engineered to tolerate. (ii) The NO-guarantee must be strict ($>$, not $\ge$), and it is, both on the main branch (strict contrapositive) and on the degenerate branch ($1 > \tfrac12$). Promise-problem definitions are picky exactly here, and the paper is careful; so are we.
