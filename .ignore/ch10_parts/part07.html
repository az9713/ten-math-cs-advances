9. The sampled Hamming-ball host

9.1 The construction and its two thresholds

The Hamming distance $\mathrm{dist}(x, y)$ between two binary strings $x, y \in \{0,1\}^m$ is the number of coordinates where they differ. For $m \ge 1$ put $$U = \{0,1\}^m, \qquad Q = 2^m, \qquad p = 2^{-\beta m}, \qquad k = \lfloor \tau m\rfloor,$$ where the Hamming radius $\tau \in (0, \tfrac12)$ and the sampling exponent $\beta \in (0,1)$ are parameters. Take two disjoint copies $U_{\mathrm L}, U_{\mathrm R}$ of $U$ and join $x \in U_{\mathrm L}$ to $y \in U_{\mathrm R}$ whenever $\mathrm{dist}(x,y) \le k$ — a bipartite graph in which the neighborhood of any vertex is a Hamming ball of radius $k$ on the opposite side. Now sample: retain each of the $2Q$ vertices independently with probability $p$, and let $G_m$ be the subgraph induced by the retained vertices (Fig. 18, left).

Two exponential thresholds govern the construction: $$A(\tau) := \kappa + \tau \log_2 3, \qquad C(\tau) := 2h(\tau) - 1.$$ $C(\tau)$ is the edge-count threshold: the host's edges per side are $Q \cdot |B_k|$ where the ball size is $|B_k| = 2^{(h(\tau)+o(1))m}$ (proved in §11), and after sampling, $\beta \lt C(\tau)$ is exactly what makes the retained graph denser than $n^{3/2}$. $A(\tau)$ is the exclusion threshold: it is the largest per-coordinate conditional entropy that Lemma 5.1 permits a child at disagreement rate $\tau$, once the entropy-swing term is amortized away; $\beta \gt A(\tau)$ will make surviving copies of $H$ too improbable to exist. We therefore assume $$A(\tau) \;\lt\; \beta \;\lt\; C(\tau), \tag{13}$$ deferring to §11 the verification that such $(\tau, \beta)$ exist — the window is genuinely there, but hair-thin. Given (13), fix a slack $$0 \lt \delta \lt \frac{\beta - A(\tau)}4,$$ then fix $L_0 \ge 4$ large enough that for every $L \ge L_0$ (possible since $\eta(L) = o(1)$ and $\binom L2 \asymp L^2$ while the other terms are $O(L)$): $$\eta(L) \lt \delta, \qquad L + 3\log_2\!\left(\binom L2 + 1\right) - \delta \binom L2 \;\lt\; -1, \tag{14}$$ and finally an integer $s \ge 2$ with $$2s\bigl(\beta - A(\tau) - 2\delta\bigr) \gt 1. \tag{15}$$ These are the parameters $L_0, s$ of the layered graph $H$ from §7. Everything is now fixed; only $m$ (hence $n$) grows.

9.2 Parent arrays, child arrays, and the entropy score

For a parent array $\mathbf u = (u_a)_{a=1}^L \in U^L$ (an ordered list of $L$ strings on one side, repeats allowed) and a child array $\mathbf z = (z_{\{a,b\}})_{\{a,b\} \in \binom{[L]}2} \in U^M$, $M = \binom L2$ (one string per parent pair), run the following experiment: choose a uniformly random parent pair $\{a, b\}$ and orient it with an independent fair coin. In coordinate $j$, write $X_j, Y_j$ for the two parent bits (in the coin's order) and $Z_j$ for the child bit $z_{\{a,b\}}(j)$, and define the entropy score $$E(\mathbf u, \mathbf z) := \frac1m \sum_{j=1}^m H(Z_j \mid X_j, Y_j).$$ This is precisely the quantity Lemmas 5.1–5.2 bound: for each coordinate, $(X_j, Y_j)$ is a without-replacement draw of two bits from the parent column $j$.

The exclusion argument is a race of exponentials, and it pays to see the ledger before the proof (Fig. 18, right). A child array whose score is $E$ has only about $2^{mME}$ realizations (a counting bound, below); requiring all $M$ children to survive sampling costs $2^{-\beta m M}$. Since $M \asymp L^2$ swamps $L$, the $2^{mL}$ choices of parent array are rounding error: whenever $E \lt \beta$, expected counts vanish. That is why the threshold $A(\tau)$, which caps realizable scores, must sit below the price $\beta$.

9.3 Excluding low-entropy arrays

The counting bound behind the ledger is the classic entropy bound on binomial coefficients:

Proposition J (binomial–entropy bound). For integers $0 \le r \le N$, $\;\binom Nr \le 2^{N h(r/N)}$.

Trivial for $r \in \{0, N\}$. Otherwise, with $\rho = r/N \in (0,1)$: expanding $1 = (\rho + (1-\rho))^N$ by the binomial theorem and keeping one term, $1 \ge \binom Nr \rho^r (1-\rho)^{N-r}$; rearranging, $\binom Nr \le \rho^{-r}(1-\rho)^{-(N-r)} = 2^{Nh(\rho)}$. ∎

Lemma 7.1 (exclusion of low-entropy arrays). With probability at least $1 - 2s\,2^{-m}$, simultaneously on both host sides and at every transition $i \in \{1, \dots, s\}$: there is no parent array of length $L_{i-1}$ and pairwise-distinct retained child array of length $L_i$ satisfying $$E(\mathbf u, \mathbf z) \;\le\; \beta - \delta. \tag{16}$$

Fix a host side, a transition, and a parent array $\mathbf u$; write $L = L_{i-1}$, $M = L_i = \binom L2$. For each coordinate $j$, partition the $M$ parent pairs into groups by their bit type at coordinate $j$: $$\mathcal G = \{00,\ 11,\ 01\},$$ where $01$ collects the mixed pairs in both orientations. Let $N_{g,j}$ be the size of group $g$ (determined by $\mathbf u$ alone), and let $b_{g,j}$ count the children of group-$g$ pairs whose $j$-th bit is $1$. Conditioned on the type of the drawn pair at coordinate $j$, the pair is uniform in its group and the child bit is $1$ with probability $b_{g,j}/N_{g,j}$ (for the mixed group this holds whichever orientation the coin chose, since the child bit ignores orientation); hence $$H(Z_j \mid X_j, Y_j) \;=\; \sum_{g \in \mathcal G} \frac{N_{g,j}}M\, h\!\left(\frac{b_{g,j}}{N_{g,j}}\right).$$

Call $(b_{g,j})_{g \in \mathcal G,\, j \le m}$ the profile of $\mathbf z$ (given $\mathbf u$): there are at most $(M+1)^{3m}$ possible profiles. For a fixed profile, a child array is determined by choosing, for every $(g, j)$, which $b_{g,j}$ of the $N_{g,j}$ group members get bit $1$ at coordinate $j$; so the number of child arrays with that profile (repeats among children allowed) is $$\prod_{j=1}^m \prod_{g \in \mathcal G} \binom{N_{g,j}}{b_{g,j}} \;\le\; 2^{\sum_{j,g} N_{g,j}\, h(b_{g,j}/N_{g,j})} \;=\; 2^{m M\, E(\mathbf u, \mathbf z)},$$ by Proposition J and the display above. Therefore at most $(M+1)^{3m}\, 2^{mM(\beta - \delta)}$ child arrays satisfy (16).

If the entries of $\mathbf z$ are pairwise distinct, the event that all $M$ of them are retained has probability exactly $p^M = 2^{-\beta m M}$. Union-bounding over the $Q^L = 2^{mL}$ parent arrays and the low-entropy child arrays, (14) gives $$\mathbb P(\text{some bad retained array}) \;\le\; 2^{mL} (M+1)^{3m}\, 2^{mM(\beta-\delta)}\, 2^{-\beta mM} \;=\; 2^{m[L + 3\log_2(M+1) - \delta M]} \;\le\; 2^{-m}.$$ A union bound over the two host sides and the $s$ transitions finishes. ∎

10. The potential argument: no copy of $H$ survives

Now the two halves meet: the entropy inequality says an embedded layer must have a high score whenever the potential doesn't rise much, and Lemma 7.1 says retained layers must have low… rather, that scores at most $\beta - \delta$ are unavailable — forcing every embedded transition's score above $\beta - \delta$, which in turn forces the potential to rise by a fixed amount, layer after layer. A quantity trapped in $[0,1]$ cannot rise forever.

Proposition 8.1 (exclusion of the layered graph). With probability $1 - o(1)$ as $m \to \infty$, the sampled graph $G_m$ is $H$-free.

Work on the event of Lemma 7.1 and suppose an embedding $\iota : H \hookrightarrow G_m$ exists. $H$ is connected and bipartite with the layers as color classes refined by parity (Fact 6.1), so consecutive layers of $\iota(H)$ occupy opposite host sides. For transition $i$, list the images of $V_{i-1}$ as a parent array $\mathbf u_i$ (length $L_{i-1}$) and the images of $V_i$ as the child array $\mathbf z_i$ indexed by parent pairs — exactly the format of §9.2, with the children pairwise distinct (injectivity of $\iota$) and retained (they lie in $G_m$). Lemma 7.1 therefore forbids (16): $$E_i := E(\mathbf u_i, \mathbf z_i) \;\gt\; \beta - \delta. \tag{17}$$

Define the entropy potential of layer $i$: $$\Phi_i \;=\; \frac1m \sum_{j=1}^m h\!\left(\frac1{L_i}\sum_{z \in \iota(V_i)} z(j)\right) \;\in\; [0, 1]$$ — the average, over coordinates, of the binary entropy of the layer's bit-frequency at that coordinate. (It is $0$ for a layer of identical strings, $1$ for perfectly balanced columns; it measures how “spread out” the layer is, coordinate by coordinate.)

At transition $i$, write $d_j = \tfrac12(\mathbb P(X_j \neq Z_j) + \mathbb P(Y_j \neq Z_j))$ for the per-coordinate disagreement in the experiment of §9.2. Every child is adjacent in the host to both its parents, so $\mathrm{dist}(u_a, z_{\{a,b\}}) \le k$ and $\mathrm{dist}(u_b, z_{\{a,b\}}) \le k$; averaging over the uniform pair (each of the $L_i$ pairs equally likely) and summing over coordinates, $$\frac1m \sum_{j=1}^m d_j \;=\; \frac1{2 m L_i} \sum_{\{a,b\} \in \binom{V_{i-1}}2} \Bigl(\mathrm{dist}\bigl(u_a, z_{\{a,b\}}\bigr) + \mathrm{dist}\bigl(u_b, z_{\{a,b\}}\bigr)\Bigr) \;\le\; \frac{2k L_i}{2 m L_i} \;=\; \frac km \;\le\; \tau.$$

Now apply Lemma 5.2 in each coordinate $j$ (its $q$ is the parents' bit-frequency, its $v$ the children's, its $L = L_{i-1} \ge L_0$ so $\eta(L_{i-1}) \lt \delta$ by (14)) and average over $j$: the $h(q_j)$-average is $\Phi_{i-1}$, the $h(v_j)$-average is $\Phi_i$, and the $d_j$-average is at most $\tau$: $$E_i \;\le\; \kappa + (\log_2 3)\,\tau + \frac{\Phi_i - \Phi_{i-1}}2 + \eta(L_{i-1}) \;=\; A(\tau) + \frac{\Phi_i - \Phi_{i-1}}2 + \eta(L_{i-1}). \tag{18}$$ Combining (17), (18), and $\eta(L_{i-1}) \lt \delta$: $$\Phi_i - \Phi_{i-1} \;\gt\; 2\bigl(\beta - A(\tau) - 2\delta\bigr) \qquad (1 \le i \le s).$$ Telescoping over the $s$ transitions and applying (15): $$\Phi_s - \Phi_0 \;\gt\; 2s\bigl(\beta - A(\tau) - 2\delta\bigr) \;\gt\; 1,$$ contradicting $0 \le \Phi_i \le 1$ (Fig. 19). So on the Lemma 7.1 event there is no embedding: $$\mathbb P(H \subseteq G_m) \;\le\; 2s\, 2^{-m} = o(1). \qquad\qquad$$∎
