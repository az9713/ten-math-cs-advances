13. Scope — proved here, imported, open

Proved in full in this document. All of §1 (Mantel; Kővári–Sós–Turán for $C_4$; projective-plane witnesses including all counting); Lemma E; the entire upper-bound chain Lemmas 3.1–3.3 and Proposition 3.4; the quadrangle axioms and girth (Propositions F–G), the footprint Lemma H, the point-class $J$-pattern exclusion, and Proposition 4.3; Theorem 1.1; Fact 6.1; the Gibbs inequality, Lemma 5.1 with the optimization made rigorous (Lemma I), and Lemma 5.2 with an explicit $\eta(L)$; Proposition J, Lemma 7.1, Proposition 8.1; Proposition K, Lemma L, Proposition M; Theorem 1.2. The two theorems' proofs are, modulo the two imports below, complete on this page.

Imported (deep finite geometry), stated precisely, machine-verified in small cases. Import 1: self-duality of $W(q)$ for even $q$. Import 2: the $0$-or-$2$ triad law in the dual quadrangle for odd $q$. Both are textbook results of the theory of generalized quadrangles [PT09]; our checks at $q = 2, 3$ (§12) confirm the exact statements used. Nothing else in the chapter is taken on faith.

What the counterexamples do and do not say. Honest calibration of the two headline results:

  • Compactness. The refutation is asymptotic and slow: the separation $n^{1/48}$ passes a factor of $10$ only around $n \approx 10^{48}$ (Fig. 15). The true order of $\mathrm{ex}(n, \mathcal F)$ is unknown — the proof traps it below $n^{21/16}$, but the best lower bounds for the whole family (from high-girth graphs, which avoid every member for girth beyond $30$) are far smaller, and nobody expects $21/16$ to be sharp. A compactness statement could survive in weakened forms — e.g. for families of two graphs, or with $n^{o(1)}$ losses — and where the true boundary lies is open.
  • Degeneracy. The exponent gain over the conjectured $3/2$ is at most $\varepsilon \lt 0.0178$ by this method, and the witness $H$ is a fixed graph with $\approx 2^{10^{109}}$ vertices — the construction refutes the conjecture without exhibiting anything a computer could store. Finding a small 2-degenerate counterexample, determining the largest achievable $\varepsilon$, and deciding the fate of the conjecture's weaker cousins (is $\mathrm{ex}(n,H) = O(n^{2 - 1/r - o(1)})$ for $r$-degenerate $H$? the Alon–Krivelevich–Sudakov exponent $2 - \tfrac1{4r}$ is still the best general upper bound) are all open. For $r \ge 3$ the conjecture (3) now looks doomed, but no counterexample is written down.
  • What survives. The positive cases in the table of §2.2 (bounded-degree side; tree blow-ups; grids) are untouched: they delimit where the conjecture is a theorem. And the guiding heuristic behind both dead conjectures — extremal numbers are governed by local densities — survives in the many settings where it is proved, just not universally.
