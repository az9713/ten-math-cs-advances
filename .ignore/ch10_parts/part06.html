7. The layered graph $H$

We turn to Theorem 1.2. Two objects must be built: the fixed 2-degenerate pattern $H$, and, for each $n$, a host graph with $n^{3/2+\varepsilon}$ edges avoiding it. This section builds $H$; it depends on two integer parameters — a base size $L_0$ and a depth $s$ — whose values are fixed in §9 and §11.

Definition (the layered graph). Take disjoint layers $V_0, V_1, \dots, V_s$ with $$|V_0| = L_0, \qquad V_i = \binom{V_{i-1}}{2} \quad (1 \le i \le s),$$ i.e. layer $i$ has one vertex for every unordered pair of vertices of layer $i-1$. Join each vertex $\{a, b\} \in V_i$ to its two parents $a, b \in V_{i-1}$, and let $H$ be the resulting graph. Write $L_i = |V_i|$, so $L_i = \binom{L_{i-1}}2 \ge L_{i-1} \ge L_0$ (the inequality since $L_0 \ge 4$, fixed below).
Fact 6.1. $H$ is connected, bipartite, and 2-degenerate.

Bipartite: every edge joins consecutive layers, so coloring the layers by parity of $i$ is a proper two-coloring. Connected: any two base vertices $a, b \in V_0$ are joined through their child $\{a,b\} \in V_1$ (here $L_0 \ge 2$ suffices), so $V_0 \cup V_1$ is connected; and each vertex of $V_i$, $i \ge 1$, is attached by an edge to $V_{i-1}$, so connectivity propagates up the layers by induction. 2-degenerate: let $S$ be any nonempty subgraph of $H$ and let $i$ be the highest layer index with $S \cap V_i \neq \varnothing$. A vertex $v \in S \cap V_i$ has all its neighbors in layers $i \pm 1$; those in layer $i+1$ are outside $S$ by maximality, and those in layer $i-1$ are its at most two parents (none if $i = 0$). So $v$ has degree at most $2$ in $S$. ∎

Why should this pattern be hard to force? Heuristically: $H$ is 2-degenerate but maximally so — every pair of layer-$i$ vertices has a common child. In any embedding of $H$ into a host, the layer-$(i{+}1)$ vertices are forced to realize all $\binom{L_i}2$ pairwise “combinations” of the layer-$i$ images. In the Hamming host of §9, being a common neighbor of $a$ and $b$ means being close to both, and “a distinct close companion for every pair” turns out to be an information-theoretic impossibility once layers are large and deep — each layer's worth of children must inject fresh entropy that a bounded potential cannot keep absorbing (§10). The tool that makes “fresh entropy per layer” precise is a bespoke inequality about three binary random variables, to which we now turn.

8. The entropy toolbox, and Lemma 5.1 in full

8.1 Entropy in five definitions

All logarithms and entropies are base two. For a random variable $W$ taking finitely many values, the (Shannon) entropy is $\mathbb H(W) := -\sum_w \mathbb P(W = w)\log_2 \mathbb P(W = w)$ (terms with zero probability contribute $0$) — the average number of bits of uncertainty in $W$. For a binary variable taking the value $1$ with probability $x$, the entropy is the binary entropy function $$h(x) := -x\log_2 x - (1-x)\log_2(1-x), \qquad h(0) = h(1) = 0,$$ concave on $[0,1]$, symmetric about $x = \tfrac12$, maximal there with $h(\tfrac12) = 1$. The conditional entropy of $Z$ given $(X,Y)$ is the average residual uncertainty $$H(Z \mid X, Y) := \sum_{x,y} \mathbb P(X = x, Y = y)\; \mathbb H\bigl(Z \mid X = x, Y = y\bigr),$$ where $\mathbb H(Z \mid X=x, Y=y)$ is the entropy of the conditional distribution. For binary $Z$ it always lies in $[0, 1]$. Two standard facts we prove as we use them; and one constant rules the section: $$\kappa \;:=\; \frac32 - \frac34\log_2 3 \;=\; 0.311278\ldots$$

The engine of everything is the Gibbs inequality (equation (10) of the chapter), which lets one bound an entropy by any convenient “guess weights” $t_z$:

Lemma (Gibbs, eq. (10)). For a probability vector $(r_0, r_1)$ and nonnegative numbers $t_0, t_1$ (not both zero): $$-\!\!\sum_{z \in \{0,1\}}\! r_z \log_2 r_z \;+ \sum_{z \in \{0,1\}}\! r_z \log_2 t_z \;\le\; \log_2(t_0 + t_1). \tag{10}$$

Write $T = t_0 + t_1$. The difference (right minus left) is $\sum_z r_z \log_2\!\frac{r_z}{t_z/T}$, the relative entropy between the distributions $(r_z)$ and $(t_z/T)$; it is nonnegative because $\ln \xi \ge 1 - 1/\xi$ gives $\sum_z r_z \ln\frac{r_z}{s_z} \ge \sum_z r_z\bigl(1 - \tfrac{s_z}{r_z}\bigr) = 1 - 1 = 0$ for any two probability vectors (terms with $r_z = 0$ vanish; if some $t_z = 0 \lt r_z$ the left side of (10) is $-\infty$). ∎

8.2 The independent-parent entropy inequality

Here is the chapter's key analytic device. Picture a child bit $Z$ and two parent bits $X, Y$: in §10, $X, Y$ will be one coordinate of two randomly chosen layer-$i$ strings and $Z$ the same coordinate of a child string, and the Hamming-ball host will guarantee the child rarely disagrees with its parents. The inequality converts an average disagreement bound into a conditional entropy bound — with the three constants ($\kappa$, $\log_2 3$, and the $\tfrac12$ on the entropy swing) that make the final exponent tick.

Lemma 5.1 (independent-parent entropy). Let $X, Y$ be independent Bernoulli variables with common parameter $q$ (i.e. each equals $1$ with probability $q$, independently), and let $Z$ be any binary variable jointly distributed with them. Write $$v = \mathbb P(Z = 1), \qquad d = \frac{\mathbb P(X \neq Z) + \mathbb P(Y \neq Z)}2 .$$ Then $$H(Z \mid X, Y) \;\le\; \kappa + (\log_2 3)\, d + \frac{h(v) - h(q)}2 . \tag{9}$$

Put $$c(x,y,z) = \frac{\mathbf 1_{x \neq z} + \mathbf 1_{y \neq z}}2 \in \{0, \tfrac12, 1\}, \qquad \pi_z = \mathbb P(Z = z),$$ the per-outcome disagreement score and the marginal law of $Z$. For each fixed $(x,y)$, apply Gibbs (10) to the conditional distribution $r_z = \mathbb P(Z = z \mid X = x, Y = y)$ with guess weights $$t_z = \sqrt{\pi_z}\; 3^{-c(x,y,z)}$$ — engineered so that its two log-factors will average precisely to the two terms we want to subtract. Averaging the resulting inequality over $(X, Y)$: the first term averages to $H(Z \mid X, Y)$; the second to $\mathbb E \log_2 t_Z = \tfrac12 \mathbb E \log_2 \pi_Z - (\log_2 3)\,\mathbb E\, c(X,Y,Z) = -\tfrac{h(v)}2 - (\log_2 3)\, d$. Hence $$H(Z \mid X, Y) - \frac{h(v)}2 - (\log_2 3) d \;\le\; \mathbb E \log_2\!\left(\sum_{z=0}^1 \sqrt{\pi_z}\, 3^{-c(X,Y,z)}\right). \tag{11}$$

It remains to bound the right side by $\kappa - h(q)/2$. Set $$a = \sqrt{1 - v}, \qquad b = \sqrt v, \qquad u = 2q - 1 \in [-1,1],$$ so $a^2 + b^2 = 1$. The unordered pair $\{X, Y\}$ has three possible types, with probabilities and inner sums:

type of $(X,Y)$probability$\sum_z \sqrt{\pi_z}\, 3^{-c(x,y,z)}$
$00$$\alpha_0 = (1-q)^2 = \dfrac{(1-u)^2}4$$t_0 = a + \dfrac b3$   ($c = 0$ at $z{=}0$, $c=1$ at $z{=}1$)
$11$$\alpha_1 = q^2 = \dfrac{(1+u)^2}4$$t_1 = \dfrac a3 + b$

So, adding $h(q)/2$ to the right-hand side of (11), our target is to show $F \le \kappa$ where $$F \;=\; \tfrac12 h(q) + \alpha_0 \log_2 t_0 + \alpha_1 \log_2 t_1 + \alpha_* \log_2 t_* .$$ Since $\log_2$ lies below each of its tangent lines, $\log_2 t \le \log_2 \lambda + (\log_2 e)\bigl(\tfrac t\lambda - 1\bigr)$ for all $t, \lambda \gt 0$. Apply this with $\lambda = \tfrac4{3\sqrt2}$ to $t_0$ and $t_1$, and with $\lambda = \sqrt{2/3}$ to $t_*$ (these are the values of $t_0, t_1, t_*$ at the symmetric point $a = b = \tfrac1{\sqrt2}$ — tangency exactly where equality must hold). Multiplying by $\alpha_0, \alpha_1, \alpha_*$ and summing, the two pieces are:

Constant piece. $(\alpha_0 + \alpha_1)\log_2\!\frac4{3\sqrt2} + \alpha_*\log_2\!\sqrt{\tfrac23}$ with $\alpha_0 + \alpha_1 = \frac{1+u^2}2$, $\alpha_* = \frac{1-u^2}2$, and $\log_2\frac4{3\sqrt2} = \frac32 - \log_2 3$, $\log_2\sqrt{2/3} = \frac{1 - \log_2 3}2$; expanding, $$\tfrac{1+u^2}2\Bigl(\tfrac32 - \log_2 3\Bigr) + \tfrac{1-u^2}2 \cdot \tfrac{1-\log_2 3}2 \;=\; 1 - \tfrac34\log_2 3 + \tfrac{u^2}4 \log_2 \tfrac43 .$$

Linear piece. $(\log_2 e)\bigl[\tfrac{3\sqrt2}4(\alpha_0 t_0 + \alpha_1 t_1) + \sqrt{\tfrac32}\,\alpha_* t_* - 1\bigr]$. Compute the bracket's coefficient of $a$: $\tfrac{3\sqrt2}4\bigl(\alpha_0 + \tfrac{\alpha_1}3\bigr) + \tfrac{\alpha_*}{\sqrt 2}$, and $\alpha_0 + \frac{\alpha_1}3 = \frac{3(1-u)^2 + (1+u)^2}{12} = \frac{1 - u + u^2}{3}$, giving $\tfrac{\sqrt2}4(1 - u + u^2) + \tfrac{\sqrt2}4(1 - u^2) = \tfrac{2-u}{2\sqrt2}$; symmetrically the coefficient of $b$ is $\tfrac{2+u}{2\sqrt2}$. So the linear piece is $$(\log_2 e)\left(\frac{(2-u)a + (2+u)b}{2\sqrt2} - 1\right) \;\le\; (\log_2 e)\left(\sqrt{1 + \frac{u^2}4} - 1\right),$$ by Cauchy–Schwarz: $(2-u)a + (2+u)b \le \sqrt{(2-u)^2 + (2+u)^2}\,\sqrt{a^2+b^2} = \sqrt{8 + 2u^2}$.

Assembling, and writing $h(q) = h\bigl(\tfrac{1+u}2\bigr)$: $$F \;\le\; g(u) \;:=\; \frac12 h\!\left(\frac{1+u}2\right) + 1 - \frac34\log_2 3 + \frac{u^2}4\log_2\frac43 + (\log_2 e)\left(\sqrt{1 + \frac{u^2}4} - 1\right). \tag{12}$$ The chapter says a direct optimization shows $g$ is maximized at $u = 0$, where $g(0) = \tfrac12 + 1 - \tfrac34\log_2 3 = \kappa$; we prove this as a lemma below, which completes the proof of (9). ∎

Lemma I (the optimization behind (12)). For all $u \in [-1, 1]$: $\;g(u) \le g(0) = \kappa$.

Two quadratic estimates. First, $h\bigl(\tfrac{1+u}2\bigr) \le 1 - \tfrac{u^2}2 \log_2 e$: the function $\varphi(u) = 1 - \tfrac{u^2}2\log_2 e - h\bigl(\tfrac{1+u}2\bigr)$ has $\varphi(0) = \varphi'(0) = 0$ (using $\tfrac{d}{du} h\bigl(\tfrac{1+u}2\bigr) = \tfrac12\log_2\tfrac{1-u}{1+u}$, which vanishes at $0$) and $$\varphi''(u) = -\log_2 e + \frac{\log_2 e}{1 - u^2} \;\ge\; 0,$$ so $\varphi$ is convex with minimum value $0$ at $u=0$: $\varphi \ge 0$. Second, $\sqrt{1 + \tfrac{u^2}4} \le 1 + \tfrac{u^2}8$ (square both sides). Substituting both into (12): $$g(u) \;\le\; \kappa + u^2\left(-\frac{\log_2 e}4 + \frac{\log_2(4/3)}4 + \frac{\log_2 e}8\right) \;=\; \kappa + \frac{u^2}4\left(\log_2\frac43 - \frac{\log_2 e}2\right).$$ The bracket is negative: $\log_2\tfrac43 \lt \tfrac12\log_2 e$ is equivalent to $(4/3)^2 \lt e$, i.e. $16/9 = 1.78 \lt 2.71$. (Numerically the $u^2$-coefficient is $-0.0766$.) Hence $g(u) \le \kappa$, with equality only at $u = 0$. ∎

8.3 Sampling parents without replacement

In the layered construction the two parents of a child are two distinct vertices of a layer — a draw without replacement, not independent Bernoulli. For large layers the difference is negligible, and the correction can be made explicit:

Lemma 5.2 (without-replacement correction). Let $L \ge 4$, let $x_1, \dots, x_L \in \{0,1\}$ be fixed bits, and sample an ordered pair of distinct indices uniformly; let $X, Y$ be the corresponding bits, and let $Z$ be a binary variable whose conditional law given $(X, Y)$ is arbitrary. Put $q = \tfrac1L\sum_a x_a$ and define $v, d$ as in Lemma 5.1. Then $$H(Z \mid X, Y) \;\le\; \kappa + (\log_2 3)\, d + \frac{h(v) - h(q)}2 + \eta(L), \qquad \eta(L) := \frac{1 + \log_2 3}{L} + \frac{h(1/L)}2,$$ and $\eta(L) = O\bigl(\tfrac{\log_2 L}L\bigr) = o(1)$, uniformly over all bit strings and conditional laws.

Coupling. Draw an index $A$ uniformly; draw $B'$ uniformly and independently; if $B' \neq A$ set $B = B'$, else redraw $B$ uniformly from the other $L-1$ indices. Then $(A, B)$ is a uniform ordered distinct pair, $(A, B')$ a uniform independent pair, and they differ only when $B' = A$: probability $1/L$. Set $X = x_A, Y = x_B$ and $X' = x_A, Y' = x_{B'}$; then $X', Y'$ are independent Bernoulli($q$), and the joint laws $P$ of $(X,Y)$ and $P'$ of $(X',Y')$ satisfy $\sum_{x,y}|P - P'| \le 2\,\mathbb P\bigl((X,Y) \neq (X',Y')\bigr) \le 2/L$.

Transfer. Give $Z'$, conditionally on $(X', Y')$, the same conditional law used to define $Z$ from $(X,Y)$, and write $f(x,y)$ for any functional of that shared kernel with values in $[0,1]$ — three cases matter: $f = \mathbb H(Z \mid X{=}x, Y{=}y)$, $f = \mathbb P(Z {=} 1 \mid x,y)$, and $f = \tfrac12(\mathbb P(Z {\neq} x \mid x,y) + \mathbb P(Z {\neq} y \mid x,y))$. For each, the two averages differ by $$\Bigl|\sum_{x,y}(P - P')\,f\Bigr| = \Bigl|\sum_{x,y}(P - P')\bigl(f - \tfrac12\bigr)\Bigr| \le \frac12\sum_{x,y}|P - P'| \le \frac1L,$$ using $\sum (P - P') = 0$ and $|f - \tfrac12| \le \tfrac12$. So $|H(Z|X,Y) - H(Z'|X',Y')| \le \tfrac1L$, $|v - v'| \le \tfrac1L$, and $|d - d'| \le \tfrac1L$.

Assembly. Apply Lemma 5.1 to $(X', Y', Z')$ and pass back: the entropy costs $\tfrac1L$; the $d$-term costs $(\log_2 3)/L$; and $h(v') \le h(v) + h(1/L)$ — because $|h(\xi) - h(\xi')| \le h(|\xi - \xi'|)$, a consequence of concavity: for $0 \le \xi \le \xi + \epsilon \le 1$, concavity gives $h(\xi + \epsilon) - h(\xi) \le h(\epsilon) - h(0) = h(\epsilon)$, and the reverse difference reduces to the same statement via the symmetry $h(t) = h(1-t)$. Total surcharge: $\eta(L)$ as displayed; finally $h(1/L) = \tfrac{\log_2 L}L + \tfrac{\log_2 e}{L}(1 + o(1))$, giving the stated order. ∎
